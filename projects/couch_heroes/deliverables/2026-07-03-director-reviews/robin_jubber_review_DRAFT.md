## 1. Craft Skill

**Rating:** [GAP: needs Glen input, 1 to 5 rating]

Robin Jubber demonstrates strong craft skill in game design with an unusual cross-disciplinary range due to his programming background. He is capable of bridging design intent and engineering feasibility, maintaining current awareness of engine capabilities, and producing substantive design documentation. His executive meeting delivery on 23 June was the most structured and efficient of any director and was used as the benchmark for subsequent presenters.

The identified gap is in translating personal craft capability into accurate team-level estimation. Two independent assessments (Valeria Trofimova, 28 Jun; Graeme Monk, 29 Jun) converged on the same finding: Robin estimates as though he is personally delivering the work, does not fully understand his team's velocity, and tends to agree to estimates without sufficient challenge. Valeria noted a positive counterpoint: Robin pre-emptively proposed fallback options for every vertical slice feature before the team began discussing cuts, demonstrating strategic scope-management thinking.

### Evidence

1. **Valeria Trofimova, #production-team, 28 Jun 2026.** Robin was assessed by production as estimating based on personal capability rather than team velocity. Valeria observed that he does not appear to have a strong understanding of the speed and capability profile within his team, and tends to accept suggested estimates without sufficient challenge.
   > Verbatim: "Robin: tends to estimate things as if he's delivering everything, I think he doesn't know the velocity/capabilities within his team too well. Tends to agree quickly when some estimate is suggested"

2. **Graeme Monk, #production-team, 29 Jun 2026.** Graeme assessed that Robin lacks sufficient technical ability to decompose designs into comprehensible work packages for other team members. While Robin was noted as reactive and articulate when describing features in lead discussions, Graeme observed a lack of proactivity in challenging leads on their estimates or assumptions.
   > Verbatim: "Robin: I personally don't feel that he has enough technical ability in being able to break down the designs into comprehensible chunks of work for other team members. In discussions with other leads, he reacts well in describing features but doesn't feel proactive enough in challenging leads"

3. **Valeria Trofimova, #production-team, 28 Jun 2026.** Valeria noted that Robin does possess a general understanding of his team's capability envelope, evidenced by his pre-emptively proposing fallback options for each Vertical Slice feature before production began discussing scope cuts. This demonstrated strategic scope-management thinking.
   > Verbatim: "at the same time has general understanding of what his team can and cannot do... that's why suggested fallback options for each feature on the Vertical Slice scope before we even started talking about cut"

4. **Mustafa Sibai, #kudos, 22 May 2025.** Mustafa praised Robin as an outstanding game design director, specifically highlighting the quality of his contributions to the game and the value of his programming background in a design leadership role.
   > Verbatim: "an awesome game design director!!! I love what you've done to the game so far... thank you for being an ex-programmer"

5. **Robin, #all-game-dev, 8 Jun 2024.** Robin demonstrated technical engine understanding unusual for a design director, accurately assessing that an Unreal Engine upgrade from 5.0 to 5.4 would not require specific new code and that the engine's internal task distribution improvements would handle the change.
   > Verbatim: "Unless I've misunderstood... this shouldn't require any specific new code going from 5.0 to 5.4. The engine should be doing a better job of splitting and distributing tasks under the hood"

6. **Robin, DM to Glen, 16 Jun 2026.** Robin proposed a clear estimation methodology: use minimum plus 20% as the base figure and actively seek disagreements and reasoning from leads. Glen endorsed this approach.
   > Verbatim: "Provide min+20% as the base figure and look for disagreements and reasons"

7. **Glen, Executive Meeting, 23 Jun 2026.** Robin's executive meeting update was used as the benchmark for presentation quality. Glen instructed the next presenter to match Robin's format and pace.
   > Verbatim: "Robin's update used as benchmark. Glen instructed next presenter: 'Update, dude. And fast.'"

### Development actions

1. Build per-person velocity baselines for each designer. Track estimated vs actual over two sprints.
2. Have each team member estimate their own work first; Robin calibrates and challenges rather than substituting.
3. Create "implementation specifications" translating design intent into engineering/art requirements with complexity per discipline.
