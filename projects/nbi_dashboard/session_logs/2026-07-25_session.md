### 3. Day rate -- DONE, committed `7a27e84`

**The decision, made by me and grounded in source rather than in a bank entry number.** The Couch Heroes bank records their fully-loaded cost model as "day rate = annual / 12 / 18 working days per month", and separately records their July 2026 contractor reform as 216 billable days a year, which is also 18 a month. Both sides of the client's own model agree on 18. The dashboard's invisible 21 contradicted the client's own paperwork by 17% on an GBP 80,000 salary (GBP 317/day shown against GBP 370/day in their model).

Chosen design: 21 stays the global default but becomes VISIBLE and OVERRIDABLE rather than a buried literal. Per-client `default_workdays_per_month`. Resolution order role, then client, then standard. The basis is stated on screen in every case.

Deliberate scope limit, and the most important judgement call in the change: **the client default feeds the DISPLAYED day rate only, never the cost engine.** A daily-basis role with no workdays stays flagged `missing_workdays` and stays out of the totals. Filling that gap from a client default would convert an incomplete row into a confident-looking cost resting on an assumption, which is precisely the failure mode migration 086 was written to stop. Distinct from the 20 workdays/month in the production bank, which is a CAPACITY baseline, not a cost divisor, and must not be conflated.

**Three defects found by the interactive visual pass that every green suite missed:**
