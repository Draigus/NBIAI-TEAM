# Coloured-Dot Game Direction Boundary Exercise

## Key Content

Facilitated workshop exercise to map each creative stakeholder's personal design boundaries on shared axes. Each participant uses coloured dots (or equivalent markers) to indicate:
- What they will actively advocate for (strong preference)
- What they will accept (neutral / live with)
- What they will resist (strong objection)

Applied across the same game-design axes as the 2x2 alignment grid (e.g. choice/impact vs. cozy, solo vs. group). Running this after the 2x2 grid reveals not just where people stand, but how firmly they hold each position and where they have flexibility.

**Purpose:** stakeholders who appeared aligned on the 2x2 map may have very different tolerance zones. Two people who both sit in the "solo/challenging" quadrant may have entirely different boundaries on what they will accept within that space. The coloured-dot exercise surfaces this at a level of nuance the 2x2 cannot.

**Facilitation note:** the exercise should be run with all relevant creative leaders in the same session so positions are visible simultaneously, not in sequence. Positions stated in isolation are often diplomatic; positions stated in view of others' positions are more honest.

- Pattern: boundary-mapping is more revealing than position-mapping; knowing where someone will stop advocating (their "accept" threshold) is often the most useful piece of data for predicting how they will behave in production conflict.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio has completed a creative vision alignment session and needs to understand how firm each position is before entering production conflict.
Relevant when: facilitating a game direction workshop with multiple creative leads -- run 2x2 first to establish positions, then coloured-dot to establish boundaries; the two together give a complete picture.
Relevant when: advising on a personnel risk (e.g. a creative leader who may not be right for the game) -- the boundary exercise often reveals whether the gap between the leader's position and the studio's direction is bridgeable or structural.
