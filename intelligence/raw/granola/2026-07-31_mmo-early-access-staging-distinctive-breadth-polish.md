# MMO Early Access Build Order: Distinctive First, Breadth Second, Polish Third

## Key Content

Three-stage build prioritisation for an MMO approaching early access playtesting:

**Stage 1 -- Build distinctive:** What makes this game different from every other MMO. The hook that would pull a player away from their current game. This is the only reason anyone leaves a game they already like; if stage 1 isn't present, no amount of polish saves the launch.

**Stage 2 -- Add breadth:** Approachability, social hooks, variety, onboarding flow. The features that make distinctive content accessible to the target archetypes. Stage 2 without stage 1 is just another competent MMO.

**Stage 3 -- Polish:** Depth, smoothness, AAA surface quality for mainstream audience. Comes last because polish on an undistinctive game is wasted.

**Seed group principle:** "Quirky, talkable, and unique beats smooth and forgettable." The early access audience is recruited to spread the word -- they need something to talk about, not something flawless.

**Vertical slice scope note:** the vertical slice is not a representative build for the audience -- it is an artefact for learning to build the software and understanding the shape of the game. Pillars drive what goes into the VS, not the other way around.

## Decisions / Insights

- CPO stated: build what makes the game different and distinctive first; social hooks and breadth second; polish for mainstream third
- CPO stated: "Quirky, Frankie, and talkable beats smooth and forgettable" for the seed group
- CPO stated: vertical slice = learning to build software and understanding the game's shape; it is not the game

## Context

Design and narrative positioning session at a ~60-person UK/Cyprus MMO studio, July 2026. Studio ~6-10 weeks from VS1 completion, pillars under active development. Context: choosing what to prioritise in next phase of development.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio on early access sequencing -- the distinctive-breadth-polish order prevents studios from polishing features that don't hook the player.
Relevant when: reviewing a studio's pre-production roadmap -- if Stage 3 work appears before Stage 1 is complete, flag it as a sequencing risk.
Relevant when: advising on vertical slice scope -- VS is a learning artefact, not a pitch sample; keeping VS and early access sequencing separate prevents feature creep into the VS.
Relevant when: investor pitch for an early-stage MMO -- framing the early access as "distinctive hook first" rather than "complete game first" is a more credible launch strategy.
