# Studio Staff Mix KPI: 80% Mid/Senior Target and Director Performance Assessment

## Key Content

A ~65-person live-service studio identified junior-heavy staffing as a structural quality risk. Baseline: ~63% of the studio was junior-level. Target: 80% mid/senior, 20% junior (approximately 20 junior roles in a ~100-person studio).

**The risk articulated:**
- In a market of 20,000+ Steam releases per year, "good enough" output does not clear the bar
- High junior ratios mean more management overhead per deliverable and slower quality iteration
- Studios that tolerate underperformance because "I can make anyone work" are carrying hidden risk -- the optimism is a management style, not a quality guarantee

**Director performance assessment approach (informal):**
- Directors rated above/below an implicit performance line, not on a formal scale
- Assessment dimensions: command presence, ability to give direct negative feedback, discipline-specific technical output, self-management vs. management of others
- Key distinction: technically strong but leadership-weak directors are as risky as technically weak ones -- the technical skill creates false confidence in the overall rating
- Rapid improvement path: present anonymous staff feedback with concrete examples in a structured 1:1; set a clear behavioural change target; if improvement occurs, close the matter

**Phased departure approach:**
- Departures executed in groups of 2-3 with 2-3 weeks pipeline overlap before contract closes
- Replacement pipelining begins before the departure notification to prevent the gap affecting delivery
- No large-wave reductions: phased approach reduces disruption to team morale and production continuity

**Communication framing:**
- Studio framing: "evolution phase" and "skill uplift," not layoff or restructure language
- Each departure has a corresponding backfill to support the net-growth narrative

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio advisor decided: target staff mix is 80% mid/senior; junior proportion will be managed down over successive hiring rounds and selective departures.
- Studio advisor concluded: director assessment should be private, two-dimensional (technical + leadership), and evidence-based -- anonymous feedback from direct reports is the most credible source.
- Studio advisor observed: a director who is technically strong but fails to give timely, direct feedback to underperformers is creating a quality problem that will compound over multiple sprints; the leadership failure is as urgent as a technical failure.
- Studio advisor decided: departures are executed in small cohorts with overlap, not announced in large waves -- production continuity and team morale are protected.

## Context

Studio leadership sync at a ~65-person live-service MMO studio. Date: 2026-06-25. Corroborated by a separate internal planning session the same day. Named individuals, specific employees, and specific studio anonymised.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: auditing a studio's talent composition -- 80/20 mid-senior/junior is the target ratio for a production-stage live-service game.
- Relevant when: a client studio has a director who is technically excellent but weak on people leadership -- the fix is a structured 1:1 with anonymised peer feedback, a clear behavioural target, and a defined review date.
- Relevant when: a studio needs to reduce headcount without triggering morale collapse -- phase in groups of 2-3 with backfill pipelining already underway before notification.
- Relevant when: a studio founder defends underperforming staff with "I can make anyone work" -- this is a management hubris signal; surface the downstream risk to quality output and to the founder's own time.
- Relevant when: communicating staff changes to the broader team -- "evolution phase" and "skill uplift" are the correct frames; layoff or restructure language triggers defensive behaviour and attrition in people you want to keep.
