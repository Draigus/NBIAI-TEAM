# Four-Layer Studio Meeting Structure for Governance at Scale

## Key Content

Studios that mix strategic, operational, and team-level content into the same meeting cadence suppress hard executive conversations and create an audience problem: the wrong people are in the room for sensitive decisions.

**Four-layer structure:**

| Layer | Participants | Agenda |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| Executive / C-level | Founders, advisors, GC, CFO | Run the business: staffing decisions, red/amber/green on product and studio health, financial runway |
| Studio leadership | Department directors + above | Product and studio health updates; directors own their vertical |
| Product Council / Directors | All directors + producers | Alignment and feedback, flowing up and down between exec and leads |
| Leads | Team leads per discipline | Team-level updates, blockers, sprint commitments |

**Key design choices:**
- Legal and HR sit at executive layer (required) and studio leadership (initially required, becomes optional as trust builds)
- Mixed audience suppresses hard conversations: exec topics become guarded when leads are in the room
- Status reporting uses project-level framing, not department framing: "the investment round is red" rather than "legal is red" -- the project carries the label, not the person
- This removes defensive posturing from status conversations

**Red/amber/green application:** each layer reads status from the layer below and escalates only what needs a decision. Leads do not need to know the financial red the board is seeing.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio advisor decided: restructure the executive meeting to remove the mixed audience that was suppressing hard conversations -- the problem was structural, not interpersonal.
- Studio advisor concluded: project-level status framing ("Investment round = red") rather than department framing ("Legal = red") removes the incentive to defend one's corner and focuses the room on solving the constraint.
- Studio advisor observed: GC and HR should transition executive meeting attendance from required to optional as structural trust matures -- their time is better spent at layer 1 only.

## Context

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio has grown past ~30 people and a single all-hands cadence is creating audience problems for sensitive conversations.
- Relevant when: department leads are becoming defensive in status reviews -- project-level red/amber/green removes the personal framing.
- Relevant when: setting up a new client advisory engagement -- establish which meeting layer you sit in and what decisions flow up vs. down through you.
- Relevant when: legal or HR are attending every meeting "just in case" -- design the cadence so they attend the layer where they add decision-making value, not information value.
- Relevant when: a studio is transitioning from founder-led to department-led structure -- the four-layer model creates the escalation path without removing founder visibility.
