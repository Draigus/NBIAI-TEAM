# Candidate Game Pitch Consistency: Scripted Game Description for Hiring Managers

## Key Content

Root cause and fix for candidate drop-out due to unclear game pitch during hiring at a ~60-person MMO studio:

**The problem:** A senior design candidate withdrew from the pipeline after speaking with multiple team members and concluding: unclear what the game was, who it was for, and whether the team knew what they were doing. The withdrawing candidate had not yet heard the game pitched directly by the appropriate hiring manager.

**Root cause:** No consistent, concise pitch script for what the game is. Different team members gave inconsistent or incomplete descriptions. Skill search team independently recommended a scripted standard game description.

**The fix: scripted game pitch for all hiring interactions.** Covers: what the game is, who it's for, why it matters. Used by all hiring managers and C-level staff in candidate conversations, not just the primary hiring contact.

**Hiring process clarity:** Hiring manager role must be formally confirmed and communicated. Ambiguity about who the hiring manager is causes process breakdowns (e.g., access to candidate feedback blocked, candidate questions routed to wrong people).

**Three-stage hiring structure:** Pre-screen → hiring manager interview → hiring manager close (or C-level close for senior hires). Simple and repeatable.

**Design principles over hard skills:** In design interviews, the priority is: explain the game, observe the candidate's reasoning and reaction. Strong design principles are rare; technical skills are trainable.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO concluded: the absence of a consistent game pitch script is a hiring risk -- senior candidates assess cultural confidence from the coherence of what the team says about their game
- Studio CPO decided: a scripted game description (what it is, who it's for, why it matters) should be distributed to all hiring managers before any candidate touchpoint
- Studio CPO decided: hiring manager roles must be formally assigned and communicated; ambiguity creates process failure

## Context

Headcount planning and hiring process review at a ~60-person UK-based MMO studio, July 2026. Studio in vertical slice phase, actively hiring across design, engineering, and art disciplines. Senior design candidate withdrawal triggered the root cause diagnosis.
