# MMO Narrative Director Interview Rubric: MMO-Specific Questions Generic Interviews Miss

## Key Content

A standard writing test and interview cannot evaluate whether a narrative director can handle the structural demands of an MMO. The evaluation must include MMO-specific probes that do not appear in Assassin's Creed or Witcher-style narrative work.

**Three critical MMO-specific interview questions:**

1. **Story pacing across session depth:** How do you pace story unlock across a 200-hour session? (An MMO player is still finding story content at hour 150; a single-player narrative exhausts its arc by hour 30.)

2. **Player segmentation:** How do you serve story-driven players and non-story-driven players simultaneously? (The MMO audience is fundamentally split; a single narrative track fails one segment or both.)

3. **Visual storytelling as narrative gap-fill:** How do you use visual storytelling -- broken statues, environmental cues, prop placement -- as a narrative layer for players who never read dialogue? (In an MMO, environmental narrative is a primary channel, not a garnish.)

**Writing test evaluation rubric (beyond "do I like the writing"):**
- Story arc completeness within the brief
- Branching quality: avoid binary choices (virtuous vs. monstrous); look for moral middle ground and consequence gradation
- Fit to brief: did they write to the world, or did they write what they prefer?
- Environmental detail: are props and space part of the storytelling?
- Character voice range: can they differentiate characters who inhabit the same world differently?

**Seniority signal:** an MMO background is the strongest differentiator. A candidate who has shipped narrative in an MMO understands constraints (session breaks, alt characters, community lore generation) that non-MMO writers must learn from scratch.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio game director decided: writing test rubric must go beyond personal preference -- the five dimensions above are the evaluation framework
- Studio game director observed: a narrative director who "gives up on" the current vertical slice and wants to re-envision the whole game is a fit problem, not a creative problem -- alignment to brief is the first filter
- Studio leadership decided: MMO experience is a strong positive signal that overrides time zone preference in otherwise equivalent candidates
