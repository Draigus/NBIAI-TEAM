# Racing Genre Live Service Retention: 85% MAU Loss in First 2 Months (Horizon Series Benchmark)

## Key Content

Data point cited in Lighthouse Studios advisory context (July 2026): a prestige racing game in the Horizon franchise generated 2.1M MAU in month one, declining to 315K by month two -- an 85% audience loss in 8 weeks.

This represents the retention curve risk for prestige-quality racing games shipped without embedded retention-first design. The Horizon series is widely regarded as the gold standard of open-world racing; this benchmark indicates that even maximum brand quality and production value does not sustain a live service audience when the underlying system design is not retention-first.

Implication for studios entering racing game live service: prestige launch quality drives month-one MAU but will not sustain it. A prestige release without live service loop design will decay to ~15% of launch MAU by month two. For a live service revenue model, this decay rate is fatal -- unit sales cover launch costs but not operating costs.

## Decisions / Insights

- NBI MD concluded: if a prestige racing game ships as "Horizon++" without live service loop design, it will follow the same decay curve and live service revenue is dead on arrival.
- NBI MD observed: this benchmark should be used to calibrate investor expectations for the difference between launch MAU (what you pitch) and steady-state MAU (what funds the service).
- Pattern: racing genre audiences have low inherent retention compared to RPG or shooter genres; live service design must compensate, not assume carry-over.
