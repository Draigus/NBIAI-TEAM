# Forced Art Direction Lock: Convene, Decide, Close

## Key Content

A process for breaking chronic art direction drift in a studio that has iterated without locking style for years. Applied after four years and approximately six restarts driven by direction changes rather than personnel failure.

**Root cause pattern:** Art direction absent from governance. Lead making aesthetic calls unilaterally, being overridden in passing (e.g., a game director naming a style "Victorian" in a meeting without a formal decision). No locked reference point; no authority structure preventing reopening.

**Lock-forcing process:**
1. Convene decision-makers in one room (executive, creative director, art director)
2. Present multiple aesthetic options side by side
3. Collect feedback and force a binary decision in the room
4. Issue a documented lock: only one named authority can reopen it, and only intentionally
5. Any future proposals must be presented as alternatives for a new decision -- the room is not re-opened for debate

**Post-lock protocol:** Present options, collect feedback, issue a locked decision. No ongoing negotiation. The authority figure who locks it is the only one who can unlock it.

**Key diagnostic signal:** If a senior stakeholder can rename the art style in passing during an unrelated meeting and the name sticks, the lock does not exist.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: force an immediate art style lock by convening the decision-makers, presenting options, and marking up choices -- only Glen can unlock after the decision is issued.
- Glen observed: the pattern of "restarting due to direction changes, not personnel" is a symptom of absent art direction governance, not creative disagreement.
- Glen concluded: allowing aesthetic debate to continue without a formal lock means every senior voice becomes a de facto art director in every meeting they attend.

## Context

Solo working notes by NBI senior advisor at a ~55-person MMO studio, 2 July 2026. The studio had been developing for four years without a locked art direction; game had restarted approximately six times due to direction changes. The advisor is acting as fractional CPO and holds authority to lock creative decisions.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio has been iterating on art direction for more than two milestone cycles without a locked reference kit -- the diagnostic is whether a new stakeholder can rename the style in any meeting without formal pushback.
- Relevant when: creative direction is being set by whichever senior voice was last in a meeting rather than by a formal authority -- the fix is to assign unlock authority to one person and document it.
- Relevant when: advising on studio governance for a project that has restarted multiple times -- investigate whether the restarts trace to direction changes rather than technical or team failure; if they do, the root cause is governance, not execution.
- Relevant when: a game director or art director is asking for feedback on style choices -- the ask signals the lock doesn't exist; do not give incremental feedback, force the decision.
