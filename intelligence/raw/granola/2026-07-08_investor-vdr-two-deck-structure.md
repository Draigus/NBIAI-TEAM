# Two-Deck VDR Structure for a Game Studio Investment Round

## Key Content

For an active game studio investment round, two distinct decks are required in the virtual data room (VDR):

**Deck 1 -- Pitch deck (narrative-focused):**
- Game narrative, audience definition, go-to-market strategy
- Purpose: entry point for investors; sets the creative and commercial story

**Deck 2 -- Due diligence deck (financial/operational):**
- Financials, live service plan, risk and exposure
- Scale: ~53 slides based on a comparable prior round
- Purpose: satisfies investor due diligence; completeness matters more than brevity here

**Role split:**
- External advisor/CPO leads narrative and deck construction
- Founder leads investor network and delivery of commitments
- Division of labour reduces bottlenecks; each principal plays to their strength

**Key observations:**
- Neither deck typically exists in usable form at the point fundraising begins; all existing materials tend to be outdated
- Treat prior-round materials as outdated by default; rebuild rather than update
- Concept artists at low utilisation during pre-vertical-slice are available to produce bespoke pitch assets

**Common failure modes:**
- Starting fundraising with outdated or placeholder materials
- Under-resourcing the DD deck because it is less visible than the pitch deck
- Conflating the two decks into one (different audiences, different purposes)

## Decisions / Insights

- CPO observed: a VDR for a game studio round requires two separate decks -- pitch deck and DD deck -- with different authors and different audiences.
- CPO observed: the DD deck at this round scale runs to ~53 slides; teams consistently underestimate its scope.
- CPO observed: concept artists at low utilisation during pre-production are a resource for pitch asset production; brief them early.
- CPO decided: external advisor leads narrative; founder leads network and delivery -- clear division prevents the CPO bottlenecking on investor relationship work.
