# UK Contractor IR35 Risk Under Cypriot Entity: Three Resolution Paths

## Key Content

A ~65-person MMO studio had UK-based staff contracted under a Cypriot entity as a mechanism to manage IR35 exposure. When rolling out a company-wide contractor policy, this arrangement required individual resolution per affected person.

**Three resolution paths:**

1. **New Cypriot entity contract:** Keep the individual under the Cypriot entity but issue a compliant contract under a new template. Requires building a UK-contractor-under-Cypriot-entity template (does not exist by default); adds legal work but preserves current structure.

2. **Convert to FTE:** Convert to full-time UK employment. Cleanest from a compliance perspective; appropriate when the role is permanent and the individual is a retention priority.

3. **Terminate and replace:** End the contract and replace with a new FTE or properly-structured contractor. Appropriate when the individual is not a retention priority or a confirmed backfill already exists.

**Decision criteria:** Retention priority (is the individual high-value?), replaceability (is a confirmed backfill in pipeline?), line manager advocacy weighted against capability evidence.

**Policy rollout sequencing:** Contract templates and HR system setup must be ready 6 weeks before the effective start date; one week for staff to review contracts; one week to sign; four-day buffer for manager procedures. September start was not achievable; October was the locked target.

**Entity transition goal:** Moving all staff off the Cypriot entity entirely, targeting a Greek entity as the successor vehicle (pending fund establishment).

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO observed: UK contractors under a foreign entity face a normalisation cliff when company-wide contractor policy rolls out -- each individual needs a case-by-case decision using the three-path framework
- Studio CPO applied: contractor policy rollout requires a 6-week preparation runway before effective date -- templates ready, HR system configured, then 1-week review + 1-week signing + 4-day manager buffer
- Studio CPO decided: the long-term goal is to exit the Cypriot entity entirely; a Greek entity is the target vehicle; rollout timing should align with that transition to avoid re-doing the work

## Context

HR and operations planning session between studio CPO and People Operations lead at a ~65-person UK/Cyprus/Greece MMO studio, July 2026. Company-wide contractor policy rollout underway; five UK-based staff under the Cypriot entity identified as requiring resolution before rollout could complete.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a UK game studio has used a foreign entity (Cypriot, Maltese, etc.) to structure UK contractors and faces IR35 normalisation pressure -- the three-path framework is the decision structure for each affected individual.
Relevant when: advising a studio on contractor policy rollout sequencing -- the 6-week preparation window before effective date is the minimum viable runway; September targets set in July will slip to October.
Relevant when: a studio is planning a full legal entity restructure (e.g., Cypriot to Greek) -- align the timing with the contractor policy rollout to avoid doing both separately and re-issuing contracts twice.
Relevant when: a line manager is lobbying to retain a contractor facing compliance resolution -- frame the decision using explicit criteria (retention priority + replaceability + backfill status) rather than as a subjective keep/cut call.
