# Game Pillar Anatomy: Header + Examples + Mental Effort = Actionable

## Key Content

Abstract pillar statements give no actionable guidance. A pillar is only effective if it contains a corpus beneath the top-line statement.

**Anatomy of a functioning game pillar:**
1. **Broad header statement** -- the slogan or principle (e.g. "Bring out your inner geek")
2. **Positive examples** -- concrete instances of the pillar expressed in game design or art
3. **Negative examples** -- what violates the pillar; behaviour or design decisions the pillar explicitly excludes
4. **Optional image** -- a visual reference that anchors the feeling
5. **Mental effort requirement** -- the pillar should require the reader to do some work to connect their discipline to it; connection must be possible but not automatic

The corpus under the pillar defines the shape. The pillar statement alone never gets it done.

**Blizzard reference:** "Bring out your inner geek" worked because it had shaping concepts underneath, not just because the slogan was good.

**Test:** anyone in the studio should be able to ask "how does my work fit pillar X?" and find an answer. If they cannot, the pillar is incomplete.

## Decisions / Insights

- Design lead concluded: current pillars too abstract ("players should always have choice" is a statement, not a pillar) -- each needs positive and negative examples
- CPO concluded: each pillar should have a broad header, do/don't examples, and an image if needed
- CPO concluded: requiring mental effort from the reader is correct -- pillars should shape, not prescribe

## Context

Design and CPO 1:1 at a ~60-person UK/Cyprus MMO studio, July 2026. Reviewing draft pillar document; identifying what makes pillars actionable vs. decorative. Studio is in pre-production, pillars are blocking role alignment and design direction work.

Relevant when: reviewing a game studio's design pillars -- check for supporting examples, not just the header statement.
Relevant when: advising on internal design communication -- pillar documents without positive and negative examples fail to guide daily decisions.
Relevant when: auditing creative alignment -- if teams cannot connect their work to a stated pillar, the pillar anatomy is incomplete.
Relevant when: building brand or IP identity documents -- the corpus under the positioning statement matters more than the statement itself.
