# Design Brief Why-First Structure: Context Before Features

## Key Content

A Head of Design joining a studio with three years of accumulated ambiguity identified that existing design briefs were feature lists with no context. Designers receiving feature lists cannot fill gaps intelligently because they don't know why the feature exists or what it connects to.

Proposed brief structure (why first):
1. **Why**: the reason this feature exists in the game -- what player problem or experience it addresses, what pillar or promise it fulfils
2. **Context**: what the feature connects to, how players encounter it, what systems it interacts with, what would break if it were absent
3. **Features**: what to build -- the specific deliverables

Process:
- Lead writes the why and context sections first; does not touch features section
- Sits with the team, takes questions, refines the top two sections based on what the team doesn't understand
- Once the why and context are solid, hands to designers to flesh out the features section themselves
- Designers who fill out their own features section have ownership; they will flag misunderstandings before building starts

**Why it changes team dynamics:**
- Designers coaching mode (asking questions, framing problems) produces better engagement than directing mode (telling designers what to build)
- Three years of ambiguity creates a team that needs a stake in the ground -- the why section provides it
- Misunderstandings surface in the brief review, not mid-build when they are expensive to fix

## Decisions / Insights

- Head of Design decided: all new briefs to lead with why (player experience reason) before context and features; feature lists without why are not accepted brief format.
- Glen fully aligned: the studio has had three years of ambiguity; designers need a stake in the ground before they can make good feature decisions.
- Head of Design observed: coaching designers (asking questions, letting them fill features) produces noticeably better energy and engagement than directing (telling them what to build).

## Context

1:1 between Head of Design and CPO at a ~55-person MMO studio, 14 Jul 2026. The Head of Design had recently joined and was restructuring how design briefs were written and reviewed. The studio's previous briefs were described as feature lists with no rationale or context.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a design team is producing work that doesn't match the intent -- the diagnostic is whether the brief contains a why section; if not, designers are building features without understanding the player experience goal.
Relevant when: a new design lead is joining a studio that has accumulated ambiguity -- why-first briefs give the team a concrete reference point to push back against or build from, breaking the ambiguity pattern.
Relevant when: advising on design process for a game studio -- the brief review session (lead presents why, team asks questions, designer fills features) is a 1-2 hour process that prevents multi-week misalignment from forming mid-build.
Relevant when: a studio's design team is disengaged or passive -- coaching mode (questions + handoff of features section) is a faster engagement intervention than directive mode (top-down feature specification).
