# Studio Budget Two-House Governance Model

## Key Content

A studio budget structure deliberately split into two separate houses with intentional friction between them as a discipline mechanism.

**Macro budget codes (5):** Operations, Art/Game, Marketing/Brand/PR, Game development, CTO

**L&D split:**
- Studio-wide L&D: line item under HR budget, centrally owned
- Departmental training: separate line item per head of department, with direct spend authority up to a threshold tied to performance process
- Leaders authorised to approve training within threshold without escalating

**Petty cash rules for directors (not leads):**
- Must be recorded; director decides allocation within envelope
- AI tools excluded: any AI spend involving IP goes through IT and legal
- Licensed or revenue-share purchases through IT procurement regardless
- Cautionary example: a director petty-cashed three machines at full retail (~$15K) vs ~$5K via IT procurement

**Hardware refresh cycle:** 18 months, critical roles first, based on runway; anything under 18 months requires director approval

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: intentional budget friction between game dev and studio ops is desirable, not a problem to eliminate -- it enforces business discipline.
- Glen decided: petty cash authority stops at director level; leads do not have direct spend authority.
- Glen observed: excluding AI tools from petty cash is not optional -- IP and licensing risk requires IT and legal oversight regardless of spend size.

## Context

Operations meeting at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 1 2026. Participants: senior advisor, Head of HR, Finance lead (new hire first day). Context: establishing budget governance structure for Lily (new finance hire) and embedding it into Lorenza's HR operations plan.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: setting up budget governance for a first-time studio finance hire -- the two-house framing gives them a structural model to work within rather than inheriting an undifferentiated cost pool.
- Relevant when: defining director-level spend authority -- petty cash with clear exclusions (AI/IP, licensing) prevents expensive procurement bypasses that an IT team would have caught.
- Relevant when: advising a studio on hardware refresh policy -- an 18-month cycle tied to runway and role criticality is a defensible, non-arbitrary policy.
- Relevant when: L&D budget ownership is ambiguous -- splitting studio-wide from departmental with clear ownership prevents HR and department heads from pointing at each other.
