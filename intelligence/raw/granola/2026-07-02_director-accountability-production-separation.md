# Director Accountability: Separating Director Ownership from Production Coverage

## Key Content

A structural principle for studio leadership: production stops covering for directors who are not performing their accountability functions.

**Director accountabilities (non-delegable):**
- Estimate quality: directors commit to estimates they have reviewed; approval without reviewing is a performance failure
- Scope management: directors own the scope of their discipline
- Team conflicts: directors do not pass interpersonal friction to production to absorb
- Delivery: directors say yes and deliver; saying yes and not delivering is treated as dishonesty

**"You cannot direct what you don't understand"**
- If a director cannot explain their estimates, that is a coaching and performance moment
- If production keeps filling the gap, directors never have to improve
- Practical example: a director approved estimates without review; senior production staff cut them by 40% on closer inspection

**Escalation rule for producers:**
- If friction is resolved: great
- If friction keeps recurring: pass it up, do not absorb it
- Production's job is not to compensate for director gaps; it is to flag them

**Onboarding newly accountable directors:**
- Start handover of accountability deliberately; do not continue covering while expecting behaviour to change
- "Stop cuddling directors" as a managerial instruction to senior production staff

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: production will no longer compensate for directors who do not review their own estimates -- accountability is returned to directors immediately.
- Glen observed: when production covers for directors repeatedly, the director has no incentive to improve; the cover-up surfaces eventually anyway and is more damaging.
- Glen concluded: directors must commit and deliver; the same "don't lie to me" rule that applies to all staff applies to directors' yes/no commitments.

## Context

1:1 between senior advisor and Head of Production at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2 2026. Conversation arose after discovering a director had approved team estimates without reviewing them; production staff had caught and corrected a 40% discrepancy.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: production staff are routinely fixing errors that originate with directors -- the structural fix is removing the coverage mechanism, not adding more production bandwidth.
- Relevant when: advising a Head of Production who absorbs director failures -- reframing their role as escalation rather than absorption is the coaching intervention.
- Relevant when: a director's estimates are consistently unreliable -- the inability to explain their own numbers is the diagnostic for whether this is a skill gap or an accountability gap.
- Relevant when: onboarding a director who is used to production backstopping their commitments -- making the new expectation explicit at the start of the engagement prevents a slow drift back to the old pattern.
- Relevant when: building studio governance around a director tier -- separating director accountability domains upfront prevents the cross-subsidisation pattern from forming in the first place.
