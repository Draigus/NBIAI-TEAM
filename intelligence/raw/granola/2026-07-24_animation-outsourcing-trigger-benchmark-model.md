# Animation Outsourcing Triggers: Benchmark Comparison and Resourcing Options

## Key Content

Decision framework for when to outsource animation at a game studio, triggered by an internal benchmark failure.

**The benchmark model:**
- Baseline expectation: one week per character animation batch (internal team)
- Comparable external reference: a junior studio in Eastern Europe delivered a full playable character batch in 2.5 months -- comparable or faster than the internal team on complex deliverables
- Trigger: when internal team cannot match an external junior studio on the same task type, outsourcing evaluation is warranted

**Three resourcing options evaluated (in order of speed vs. cost):**
1. Short-term contractors -- fastest to start, variable quality risk
2. Outsourcing to an established animation studio -- consistent quality, longer lead time to start
3. Large animation pack purchase -- cheapest, limited customisation, useful for filler animations not hero content

**Secondary trigger:** when animation delays are causing downstream friction across multiple departments (VFX, design, production), not just a local bottleneck.

**Asset triage alongside outsourcing decision:** identify assets stuck in pipeline due to animation gaps and make a hard call (e.g., build the pending asset, animate it, move on -- don't let incomplete work create compounding drama).

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio leadership decided: all three outsourcing options -- contractors, studios, and asset packs -- to be evaluated in parallel rather than sequenced
- Studio leadership observed: an animation bottleneck that is generating friction across multiple teams is a systems problem, not a capacity problem -- the lead is the root cause when complaints are multi-directional
- Studio leadership observed: animation pack purchases are a useful relief valve for NPC and filler animation but not for hero character content requiring studio-specific rigs

## Context

1:1 between embedded CPO and art leadership at a ~55-70 person MMO game studio. 2026-07-24. Studio approaching vertical slice with animation output significantly below expectation. Eastern European junior studio benchmark cited as external reference point.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio's animation team is underperforming against external benchmarks -- use a comparable studio's delivery as the trigger metric, not just internal gut feel.
Relevant when: animation delays are generating cross-department friction -- treat it as a lead/management issue, not just a capacity issue.
Relevant when: evaluating outsourcing options at speed -- run all three tracks (contractors, studios, packs) in parallel rather than eliminating sequentially.
Relevant when: assessing a vertical slice risk register -- animation is consistently the longest-lead-time dependency; flag it early.
