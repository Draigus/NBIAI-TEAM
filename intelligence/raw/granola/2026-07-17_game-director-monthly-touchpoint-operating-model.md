# Game Director Operating Model: Monthly Department Touchpoints, Directional Questions Only

## Key Content

Game Director role definition for a studio where the Lead Designer is now onboarded and capable of owning design detail:

**What the Game Director stops doing:** lead-designer-mode work (design documents, detailed system specification, day-to-day design decisions). These transfer to the Lead Designer.

**What the Game Director does instead:**
- Touch each department (engineering, art, design) once per month
- Ask directional questions only: "How do you feel about the direction of the game?" -- then listen without solving
- Bring findings to Product Council; Product Council decides on solutions
- Own the schedule proactively: review last month, determine who genuinely needs Game Director time, do not let meetings happen by default

**Why this works:** the Game Director is the only person with full cross-disciplinary sight; monthly touchpoints across departments surface misalignment earlier than waiting for it to surface in production. Asking without solving preserves the team's ownership of solutions while giving the Game Director the signal they need.

**Failure mode to avoid:** Game Director in design detail means the Lead Designer is undermined and the cross-disciplinary view is lost simultaneously.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen (NBI MD / CPO) concluded: once a Lead Designer is in place, the Game Director's hands-on design involvement becomes a liability, not an asset.
- Pattern: Game Directors who came up through design have a strong pull back into design work; structural guardrails (monthly cadence, "directional questions only" rule) counteract this pull.
- Observation: "don't let meetings happen to you" is the operating principle -- proactive schedule ownership is a meaningful shift for directors who previously had fully managed calendars.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio hires a Lead Designer into a team previously managed directly by the Game Director -- the transition requires explicit role boundary redefinition, not just an informal handoff.
Relevant when: advising a Game Director who is still doing design work -- the cost is cross-disciplinary blind spots and Lead Designer disempowerment; the monthly touchpoint model is the practical alternative.
Relevant when: building a Product Council operating model -- the Game Director's role in Product Council is to bring directional findings, not design solutions; the structure needs to be explicit.
Relevant when: a Game Director complains about losing influence as the team grows -- reframe: the influence comes from the monthly cross-team view, not from attending every design meeting.
