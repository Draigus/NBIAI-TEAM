# Studio Design Roles Accountability Model: Architect / Game Director / EP / Creative Director

## Key Content

Four-role design leadership model for a 55-70 person game studio with embedded advisory CPO:

**Architect (Head of Design):** blueprints the vision, audits features, drives clarity across functions. Pushes for decisions but does not make them unilaterally. Role is to coalesce information, surface alignment gaps, and get the core group to agree. Does not own execution.

**Game Director:** execution lead for VS and major milestones. Aligns art and engineering to design intent. Owns VS delivery timeline and team coordination.

**EP (Executive Producer / CPO):** ships on budget, schedule, and acceptable risk. Does not own creative direction; owns delivery constraints and risk management.

**Creative Director (studio founder/CEO):** owns the vision. Needs "boxes to colour in" rather than open-ended creative direction -- defined constraints and phase structures prevent diverging. Works best when the vision is structured for them, not when they are generating structure.

**Reporting structure (formation phase):** Architect, Game Director, and EP all report to the CPO during the initial setup period. Final structure resolved once the team is past initial setup.

**Roles document approach:** circulate individually for feedback before aligning as a group. Prevents public position-taking before private alignment.

**Founding pattern:** a founder Creative Director who communicates in black-and-white terms is often more open to reasoned pushback than it appears -- the initial position is a starting frame, not a final decision. Framing their role as "owning the vision" while the Architect structures the delivery of that vision resolves the authority ambiguity.

Relevant when: a founder CEO is also acting as Creative Director and generating scope divergence -- the "boxes to colour in" framing channels creative energy within defined constraints.
