# Studio AI Adoption: Discipline-Level Resistance Pattern and Two-Track Onboarding Model

## Key Content

At a ~55-70 person MMO studio, a consistent pattern emerged across creative disciplines: leads support AI adoption in other disciplines but resist it within their own. This is a structural bias, not idiosyncratic -- observed independently in character art, environment art, and concept art leads.

**Root cause hypothesis:** The leads who are fastest in their discipline have the most to lose from AI parity. Their competitive advantage is craft speed; AI compresses that advantage. Resistance is rational self-preservation, not technophobia.

**Counter-data point:** The studio's fastest human performer -- noted as someone who "could make a strong case for not needing AI" -- is the exception that proves the rule: if you can genuinely outpace AI, your resistance is evidence-based. If you cannot, it is not.

**Proposed two-track onboarding model:**
1. Dedicated AI specialist embedded in the studio to drive adoption across teams
2. Per-discipline short-term contractors (one per discipline: animation, audio, VFX, concept) who train each team in their domain

Preferred outcome: both tracks, not either/or. The generalist cannot go deep enough; the specialist cannot cover breadth.

**ROI framing used to justify training investment:** "One month of structured AI learning equals twelve months of efficiency gain." Used to argue for bringing in external trainers rather than self-learning.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO observed: leads resist AI in their own discipline while supporting it in others -- structural pattern across character, environment, and concept art
- Studio CPO hypothesised: better AI setup and rigging inputs produce significantly better outputs; the quality gap is in the prompting and prep, not the tool
- Studio CPO proposed: hire a dedicated AI person for studio-wide adoption AND bring in per-discipline contractors to run structured onboarding
- Studio CPO concluded: a single AI generalist cannot achieve per-discipline depth; the contractor-trainer model covers what the generalist cannot

## Context

Production planning session at a ~55-70 person MMO studio, July 2026. AI adoption discussed in the context of art team throughput gaps and resourcing constraints. Leads had been managing AI adoption organically with inconsistent results. Decision to formalise the model prompted by visible throughput delta between AI-assisted and non-AI-assisted workflows.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio on AI adoption -- expect lead-level resistance in their own disciplines regardless of stated enthusiasm for AI in other teams; design the adoption programme to work around this structural bias.
Relevant when: a studio is considering a single "AI champion" hire -- assess whether the role can go deep enough per discipline; if not, the per-discipline contractor-trainer model is the complement.
Relevant when: calculating ROI for AI training investment -- the 1:12 month-to-efficiency ratio is a useful anchor for justifying external training spend vs. organic adoption.
Relevant when: an individual practitioner claims AI cannot improve their workflow -- verify against their actual output speed before accepting the claim; the fastest performers may be right.
