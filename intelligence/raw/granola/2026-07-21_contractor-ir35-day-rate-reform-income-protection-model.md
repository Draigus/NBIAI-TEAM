# Contractor IR35 Reform: Day Rate Model That Protects Annual Income

## Key Content

Pattern for UK game studios that have been treating contractors as de facto employees (paying for time off, providing equipment, pension). How to fix IR35 compliance without cutting contractor annual income:

**Core principle:** Shift from fixed monthly invoices to daily rate billing for actual days worked, then calibrate the daily rate so that contractors who work their expected annual days earn the same total income as before.

**Calibration formula:**
- Total annual working days: 260
- Less expected leave: 36 vacation days + 8 sick days = 44 non-billable days
- Billable days target: 216
- Effective result: contractor earns same annual total at a higher daily rate, while the studio only pays for days actually worked

**Governance structure:**
- Monthly soft cap (e.g., 20 days) with manager pre-approval for days beyond it
- Minimum threshold per quarter (e.g., 44 days = 80% of expected) as a contract breach trigger -- framed as breach, not dismissal
- All extra-day approvals happen before work, not at invoice stage
- Real-time visibility via HR system pre-approval flow; finance receives a downloadable feed

**Activity monitoring caveat:** Audit trail tools (Slack, Jira, Perforce) are valid as reactive audit evidence when there is prior suspicion, but must not be positioned as daily monitoring. If communicated to contractors as surveillance, it damages the relationship and may reintroduce employment indicators.

**Mid-year transition:** Calculate each contractor's vacation position to date, settle over/under on the final old-format invoice, then start fresh on the new model.

## Decisions / Insights

- Pattern: studio offering time off pay to contractors was the key IR35 indicator; removing time-off pay and switching to pure day-rate is the compliance fix.
- Pattern: protecting annual income (via calibrated daily rate) makes the compliance change manageable for contractors and reduces turnover risk during transition.
- Pattern: a Q&A roundtable before rollout (alongside individual contractor letters) surfaces objections early and signals transparency.
- Anti-pattern: mid-year transitions disadvantage contractors who took more leave early in the year; a full-year calculation and top-up/reduction on the final old invoice is the clean solution.

## Context

Derived from a contractor compliance reform session at a ~55-person UK game studio, 21 Jul 2026. The studio had been paying contractors for vacation, bereavement, and sick leave, creating IR35 breach risk. The reform was designed by HR, Finance, and executive leadership with legal input.
