# Leadership Ratio Framework: Management-to-Execution Progression for Senior Technical Hires

## Key Content

A phased ratio model for calibrating how much time a senior technical hire should spend managing vs. doing hands-on work.

**Phase 1 target:** shift from ~80/20 (managing/doing) to ~60/40
- Address the gap before team resentment builds; junior team members are not learning because the lead cannot demonstrate or model the skill
- Trigger: feedback from multiple stakeholders independently (CEO, COO, HR, internal team) converging on the same gap

**Phase 2 target:** once stabilised at 60/40, shift toward ~50/30/20 (managing/doing/cross-team)
- Cross-team component covers coordination with art, engineering, and game design peers
- Total "doing" component remains meaningful throughout

**Diagnostic signals that the ratio is wrong:**
- Team unhappy that lead cannot teach core discipline skills
- Lead praised for organisational delivery but not for craft contribution
- Senior stakeholders value the output (schedule, coordination) but junior staff are not growing
- Principal hire that was meant to compensate for skill gap never materialised

**Follow-up cadence:** book explicit follow-up before changes set in; one-week lag between the coaching conversation and the hands-on re-engagement is too long for time-sensitive vertical slice delivery.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: phase 1 correction for the art producer role is 80/20 → 60/40, starting immediately; phase 2 is a future state, not a current ask.
- Glen observed: when CEO, COO, HR, and internal team feedback independently converge on the same gap, the gap is real and the response should be specific and immediate, not deferred to a broader performance review.
- Glen concluded: define concrete deliverables for what "hands-on" means for a given senior role -- without specifics, "do more doing" is not a coaching instruction.

## Context

1:1 between senior advisor and Art Producer at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2 2026. Feedback received across four independent sources pointing to the same pattern: high organisational contribution, insufficient hands-on discipline work alongside the team.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: coaching a senior technical hire who has drifted into full management mode -- the phase-based ratio framework gives a concrete intermediate target rather than a binary "manage or do" demand.
- Relevant when: multi-stakeholder feedback independently converges on the same leadership gap -- the convergence pattern is diagnostic; the response should be proportionate and specific.
- Relevant when: a junior team is not growing under a senior lead -- the inability to teach is often the signal that the senior is not doing enough themselves.
- Relevant when: a principal hire meant to close a skill gap never materialised -- the ratio rebalance is the interim fix while the principal hire pipeline reopens.
- Relevant when: advising on what "hands-on" means for a senior role -- specifying concrete deliverables (not just time allocation) is required before the instruction becomes actionable.
