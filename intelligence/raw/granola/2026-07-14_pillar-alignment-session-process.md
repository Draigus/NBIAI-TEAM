# Pillar Alignment Session: Multi-Author Consolidation and Red-Team Process

## Key Content

When multiple senior stakeholders have each produced their own design pillar documents, a structured session is needed to consolidate and lock a shared version. A process developed at a ~55-person MMO studio:

**Pre-session:**
- Consolidate all existing documents into a single hybrid draft (do not share the draft before the session; each principal should bring their raw position, not a pre-aligned one)
- Identify specific divergences between authors' documents; note them for time-boxed resolution in the session

**Session format:**
- Attend with all principals whose buy-in is required (design lead, game director, CEO, CPO in this case)
- Time-box each pillar; do not allow open-ended discussion
- For each pillar: mark it "ready" or "not ready"
- Red-team the double-clicks: challenge "what does this mean?" until alignment is concrete, not aspirational -- e.g. "the world remembers differences" sounds aligned until you ask what it means for two principals who have different mental models
- Items that don't need resolution now go on a deferred list -- they are not dropped, they are explicitly parked

**Locking:**
- Once locked, the pillar document becomes the reference for art direction, systems design, and design briefs
- Future decisions are checked against the pillars; the pillars are not re-opened for each new feature debate

**Diagnosis from prior red-team failures:**
- Misalignment at the detail level is common even when headline agreement exists
- Aspirational pillar language ("the world feels alive") can mask two opposite mental models
- The red-team must reach concrete examples, not just head-nods to the headline

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO decided: pillar document not circulated to all principals before the session; raw reactions in the group are more diagnostic than pre-aligned positions.
- Studio CPO observed: previous red-team exposed that two principals agreed on pillar headline language but had opposite mental models for what it meant in practice -- "world remembers differences" was the example cited.
- Studio CPO decided: deferred list for items that don't require immediate resolution; explicit parking prevents scope creep in the alignment session without losing the item.

## Context

1:1 between Head of Design and CPO at a ~55-person MMO studio, 14 Jul 2026. Three pillar documents existed (one from CEO, one from Game Director, one from Head of Design); a unified hybrid was being drafted. The alignment session had not yet been booked; this was pre-session planning. A prior red-team had revealed that headline agreement did not mean conceptual alignment.

## Applicability

Relevant when: multiple senior stakeholders have produced separate design pillar documents -- consolidate to one hybrid draft before the alignment session; do not circulate in advance.
Relevant when: a game studio's design pillars have been agreed in words but not in decisions -- red-team each pillar with "what does this mean when X happens?" until concrete examples are used, not abstract statements.
Relevant when: a pillar alignment session is running long -- time-boxing each pillar with a "ready / not ready" binary keeps sessions from drifting; deferred list handles genuine uncertainty without blocking progress.
Relevant when: advising a studio on how to lock design vision for investor or VDR purposes -- locked pillars with concrete red-team examples are more credible than aspirational language; investors and partners will ask the double-click questions during diligence.
