# Strike-Based Employee Performance Protocol for Returning or At-Risk Staff

## Key Content

A structured re-engagement and escalation protocol for an employee with a documented performance history returning to a role, or for a current employee placed on informal performance management.

**Setup phase (before re-engagement):**
- Deliver feedback themes from the previous period upfront, before the employee starts
- Set clear and specific behavioural expectations at the outset
- Establish documented 1:1 cadence with the direct manager from day one

**Escalation structure:**
- Three-strike system: senior advisor notified by the direct manager at each strike occurrence
- Strike definition: a clear instance of the previously identified behaviour recurring
- After three strikes: contract closed; no further escalation steps
- Senior advisor tracks the pattern across all three strikes rather than hearing about it retrospectively

**Decision attribution:**
- The re-engagement decision is publicly attributed to the senior advisor, not the line manager or CEO
- Rationale: protects the manager's/CEO's relationship with the employee; gives the senior advisor leverage to coach the CEO on people decisions regardless of outcome
- Creates a documented trail: if the employee succeeds, the studio benefits; if the employee fails, the documented trail supports the coaching conversation with the CEO

**Value regardless of outcome:**
- Employee succeeds: direct studio benefit
- Employee fails: documented evidence supports the coaching conversation about people decision-making with the decision-maker who originally pushed for the re-engagement

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: attribute the re-engagement decision publicly to himself (senior advisor) rather than to the CEO or manager -- protects their relationships and creates coaching leverage.
- Glen observed: a three-strike documented system gives the senior advisor active visibility into the pattern as it develops, rather than being told about a failure after the contract is already closed.
- Glen concluded: the value of the protocol is symmetric -- it works whether the employee succeeds or fails, because the documented trail is useful in both cases.

## Context

1:1 between senior advisor and COO at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2 2026. Discussion arose around a previously departed employee returning to the team at the CEO's insistence, against the team's consensus. The senior advisor designed the protocol to make the re-engagement defensible and educational regardless of outcome.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a CEO or founder pushes for re-engaging a previously departed employee despite team resistance -- the protocol converts a contentious people decision into a structured experiment with a clear exit condition.
- Relevant when: a manager is about to take ownership of a re-engagement decision they did not make -- attributing the decision to the senior advisor protects the line manager's relationship with the employee.
- Relevant when: coaching a CEO on people decision-making -- a documented three-strike case is more persuasive evidence than a summary of "it didn't work out."
- Relevant when: re-engaging any employee with a documented performance history -- the upfront delivery of prior feedback themes prevents the employee from claiming they were not warned.
