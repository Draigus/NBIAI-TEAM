# MMO Player Archetype Multi-Axis Design Framework

## Key Content

Building a social MMO for multiple player archetypes requires two axes, not one. A single solo-to-group axis produces mediocrity in the middle; adding a second axis (no-impact to high-impact) creates four distinct quadrants, each with its own design contract.

**The two axes:**
1. **Solo to forced-group** -- how much does optimal play require other people?
2. **No world impact to high world impact** -- how much does the player's presence change anything?

**Archetype mismatch in pillar language:** MMO design documents habitually write pillar language from one archetype's point of view. "No gear treadmill" reads as "no progression" to an achiever. "No endless grinding" alienates players who treat grinding as core gameplay. Pillar language must be tested against achievers, combat-focused players, cosmetics-motivated players, and casual social players simultaneously.

**WoW as cautionary success:** World of Warcraft succeeded by accident -- it built distinct content tracks for each archetype (crafters, questers, raiders) with full game loops per type. Most studios don't map this explicitly and then wonder why 70% of the potential audience feels excluded.

**Pokemon model for archetype balance:** Optimal play exists but is never required. Both skill-driven and cosmetics-motivated playstyles win. This is the benchmark for a game that doesn't force everyone onto one axis.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO decided: design must solve both axes simultaneously; a one-axis solo/group solution still leaves impact as unsolved and results in a mediocre middle.
- Studio CPO and Head of Design agreed: game cannot skew cozy-only (loses ~70% of audience) or hardcore-only (loses another large segment); explicit archetype balance required.
- Studio CPO observed: current design direction skews toward "look pretty, don't be pushed" due to pillar language anchored to cosmetics-motivated and casual archetypes.
- Head of Design agreed: Pokemon is a viable model -- optimal play exists, no archetype is excluded, all styles win something meaningful.

## Context

1:1 between Head of Design and CPO at a ~70-person MMO studio, 15 Jul 2026. Head of Design had produced a merged pillar document for pre-session review. Archetype mismatch surfaced when the CPO noted his own player profile (achiever, combat-focused, skill-driven) was unaddressed by the current pillar language, while the Head of Design's profile (cosmetics-motivated, casual raider, values social cohesion) dominated the framing. A pillar alignment session with full leadership was scheduled for the following day.

## Applicability

Relevant when: reviewing game design pillars for a social or multiplayer game -- test each pillar against at least four archetype profiles (achiever, cosmetics-motivated, casual social, combat-focused) before locking.
Relevant when: diagnosing audience skew in a studio's current game direction -- map each design decision onto the two-axis quadrant to reveal structural exclusions.
Relevant when: advising a studio that can't articulate what audience it's building for -- the two-axis diagram forces the conversation, especially when leadership holds different player profiles.
Relevant when: preparing a game for investor review -- investors will ask which archetypes are served and why; a two-axis map with concrete examples is more credible than archetype-neutral "for everyone" framing.
