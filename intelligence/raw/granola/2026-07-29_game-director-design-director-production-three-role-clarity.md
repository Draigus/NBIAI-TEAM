# Game Director, Design Director, Production: Three-Role Accountability Model

## Key Content

Three distinct leadership roles with non-overlapping accountability lenses:

**Design Director:** Authors and maintains the creative vision -- what the game is. Blueprints the intended player experience, locks pillars, resolves creative conflicts against the vision document. This is a creative authoring role.

**Game Director:** Cross-department role checking whether the shipped experience matches the design director's vision. Does not author the vision; audits execution against it. Operates across all departments. Accountability: "Does this feel like what we said it would feel like?"

**Production:** On-time, on-scope, on-quality delivery. Owns schedule, scope, and quality gate. Does not define the vision or audit creative alignment.

**Common failure mode:** Studios conflate the game director role with head of design (treating it as a creative authoring role) or with production (treating it as a delivery role). The game director's distinctive function -- execution alignment audit across departments -- falls through the gap when no one owns it explicitly, and no one is asking "does the shipped thing feel like what we promised?" across department boundaries.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO decided: game director is a cross-department execution-alignment role, not a creative authoring role and not a production role -- its accountability is matching shipped experience to authored vision
- Studio CPO concluded: design director (vision) and game director (alignment check) must be distinct; combining them produces a creative lead who cannot step back to audit their own output
- Studio CPO observed: when the game director function is absent, no one asks "does this feel right?" across department boundaries -- the question disappears into the gap between design and production

## Context

Org chart review between studio CPO and Head of People at a ~65-person MMO studio, July 2026. The game director role had been a point of confusion -- unclear whether it sat under design, production, or was cross-functional. The three-role model was applied to clarify accountability before rolling out the roles and responsibilities document to leadership.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio's creative leadership structure produces confusion about who owns "does this feel right?" -- add a game director role with an explicit auditing (not authoring) remit.
Relevant when: a design director is becoming a bottleneck because they're expected to both author the vision and constantly audit execution across departments -- split the two functions.
Relevant when: a studio's production team is being asked to make creative alignment calls -- this is a scope overreach; the game director role should absorb it.
Relevant when: building a first leadership structure for a studio scaling from 30 to 100+ people -- this three-role model prevents early conflation patterns that become difficult to untangle at scale.
Relevant when: a studio can't afford all three roles simultaneously -- the game director function can be held part-time by a founding CPO, but it must be explicitly named and owned, not left diffuse.
