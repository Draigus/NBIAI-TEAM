# QA Lead Hired Above Strong IC: Growth-Enabling Pattern

## Key Content

A studio identified a strong individual contributor (IC) in QA with no prior management experience. The team's decision: hire a QA Lead above the IC rather than promoting the IC into the lead role.

**Pattern:**
- IC assessment: structured, high-quality output, strong culture fit, no management experience
- Role being created: QA Lead, scope includes managing the IC plus a potential outsourced QA pool (~15 contractors for 3-4 months of sprint testing)
- QA Lead profile: player-manager who also gets hands dirty
- IC outcome: set up to grow under a competent lead rather than fail in an overloaded management role

**Key reasoning:**
- Promoting an IC with no management experience directly into a lead role covering outsourced teams is high-risk
- The IC's lack of management experience is not a disqualifier -- it is a growth path trigger
- A strong player-manager lead is the right structure for a studio that will use outsourced QA for burst capacity

**Operational note:** verify the IC's current official title before posting the lead role -- title conflicts on hire are common when an IC is titled similarly to the incoming lead.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio leadership decided: do not promote the IC QA into the QA Lead role; hire a lead above them.
- Studio leadership observed: the right call sets the IC up to grow rather than fail.
- Studio leadership decided: QA Lead must be comfortable managing an outsourced QA pool alongside in-house IC.
- Studio leadership observed: always verify current titles before posting roles that sit directly above an existing team member.

## Context

Senior production meeting at a ~55-person MMO studio, July 2026. Studio converting a mid-QA role to QA Lead due to anticipated burst testing needs during vertical slice. The IC under discussion is high-performing but early in their career.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a client wants to promote a strong IC into a management role without evidence of prior management experience -- recommend hiring a lead above them first.
- Relevant when: a studio is planning outsourced QA burst capacity -- the player-manager QA Lead profile is the right hire (not a pure manager, not a pure tester).
- Relevant when: creating a role that sits above an existing team member -- check titles first to avoid a conflict on the job posting.
- Relevant when: a high-performing IC seems "ready" for management -- use this pattern to test the principle: are they set up to grow, or set up to fail?
