# Finding Fun Before Going Bankrupt: Games as Recipes, Not Feature Bags

## Key Content

Glen's articulation of the core production tension in game development and the philosophy that governs his advisory approach.

**Core framing:**
- Glen's job description: "Find the recipe for fun before going bankrupt"
- Two failure modes pull studios apart: pure production (ship something boring) vs. pure exploration (never ship)
- "Shipping boring is not something I'm willing to do" -- the quality bar is non-negotiable even under schedule pressure

**Games as recipes, not feature bags:**
- WoW's combat is great because it suits WoW, not because it is objectively superior combat
- Removing one element from a working recipe destabilises the others; players experience the whole, not the parts
- Implication for advisors: when a studio wants to change a mechanic, the question is not "is this mechanic good" but "does this change break the recipe"

**Holistic vs. siloed development:**
- Concern about modern developers: too siloed, optimising their one area without thinking about how it serves the whole
- Contrast with an earlier era: teams played their own games without being told to, building for themselves as the target player
- The chess board model: different specialists hold distinct mandates (finding fun / building discipline / creative architecture) but must be aligned on the whole

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen observed: studios that ship boring games and studios that never ship are mirror failures; the job is to resolve the tension, not pick a side.
- Glen concluded: treating a game as a feature bag (additive, siloed) is the root of both scope creep and creative incoherence; the recipe model forces every element to justify its relationship to the others.
- Glen observed: the best games came from teams who played their own games; building for yourself as a target player produces more coherent design than building for an abstracted persona.

## Context

1:1 session between studio advisor (Glen) and the creative director at a ~65-person MMO studio, Jul 1 2026. The conversation arose from discussing plugin proposals (could they save time while preserving the fun formula?) and the broader challenge of maintaining creative coherence during vertical slice production.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: advising a studio that is drifting toward pure delivery and losing creative ambition -- the "shipping boring" framing is a useful stake in the ground.
- Relevant when: a studio is debating whether to change a core mechanic -- the recipe model redirects the question from isolated quality to systemic fit.
- Relevant when: a production leader is concerned about siloed team culture -- the holistic development argument (play your own game, build for yourself) gives a concrete cultural remedy.
- Relevant when: scoping a vertical slice and needing to prioritise -- the recipe framing helps identify which elements are load-bearing and which are substitutable.
