# Pillars vs. Value Creations: A Framework for Stress-Testing Game Vision

## Key Content

Game design pillars are frequently too aspirational to be useful as decision-making tools. A two-layer framework distinguishes aspirations from operative constraints:

**Pillars = aspirations:**
- High-level statements of what the game should be or feel like
- Problem: blue-sky pillars without constraints are unenforceable; teams over-extend or misinterpret them
- Stress-testing method: ask "how will the team misinterpret or over-extend this?" before finalising; red-team the pillars against the team that will use them

**Value creations = razors (constraints and additions):**
- Operative constraints that shape how the pillars are delivered
- Each pillar should have at least one corresponding value creation that limits its scope
- Example: "ultimate player choice" (pillar) + "within a development window of X years and a budget of Y" (value creation)
- Two value creations identified as universally missing from early-stage game vision documents:
  - Commercial viability: the studio must still exist and be funded in 5 years
  - Delivery constraint: a playable game must ship within approximately 2.5-3 years

**Process:**
- Work up player outcome examples from each pillar statement (what does a player actually experience because of this pillar?)
- Define value creations for each pillar, starting with commercial viability and delivery constraints
- Red-team pillars against the people who will use them day-to-day, not just leadership

## Decisions / Insights

- Leadership concluded: existing pillars were too blue-sky; value creations (razors) are required to make them operative.
- Leadership identified: commercial viability and delivery constraint are the two value creations most commonly missing from early-stage vision documents.
- Leadership decided: stress-testing method is to ask how the team will misinterpret the pillar, not whether the pillar is well-written.
- Leadership observed: the person who writes the pillars and the person who closes the work against them are often different; the closer (more operationally minded) person is often better at defining value creations.

## Context

Alignment session at a ~55-person MMO studio, 7 Jul 2026. Studio had existing design pillars that were found to be insufficiently constrained after testing them against production decisions. The value-creation layer was introduced as the operative counterpart to the aspirational pillar layer.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio's design pillars are not functioning as decision-making tools -- the diagnostic is whether teams invoke the pillars when making tradeoffs; if not, they are too abstract.
- Relevant when: advising on early-stage game vision for a new project -- build the value-creation layer (especially commercial viability and delivery constraint) alongside the pillars, not after them.
- Relevant when: a client's pillars have been written but not tested -- red-team them by asking how the team that uses them daily will misinterpret or over-extend each one.
- Relevant when: a studio's vision person and execution person are different individuals -- the execution person often produces better value creations; involve them in pillar refinement.
