# CPO Model for Game Studio: Separating Game from Studio Health

## Key Content

An organisational model for a ~55-person MMO studio that creates a hard separation between "the game" (producer track) and "studio health" (CPO track).

**CPO scope:** HR, Finance, IT, Legal, Project Management
- Producers focus exclusively on the game; studio board view = top deliveries, headcount adds, health metrics
- CPO does not own game content, milestones, or scope decisions

**Marketing kept deliberately lean:** two people through development; PR engaged pre-launch; community starting ~6 months before launch

**BD structured differently from a traditional BD team:** hire people to chase games into the infrastructure (portal strategy), not outbound sales

**Budget split (two houses):** game dev vs studio ops deliberately separated to build friction -- a discipline mechanism for a founder-CEO learning to run a business alongside making a game

**Art team restructuring principle:** 30/55 staff ratio flagged as too high; strategic thinning via replacing underperformers with veterans, cost-neutral through lower junior headcount

**C-suite composition at ~55 staff:** Founder CEO, COO, CPO, CTO (search active), Head of HR under CPO

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: producers own the game; the CPO covers everything else -- this creates clean accountability at the director/head of department level.
- Glen observed: an art team at 30/55 is structurally distorted; fixing it requires veteran replacements, not just headcount cuts, to maintain output quality.
- Glen concluded: BD structured around a portal strategy (inbound via infrastructure) rather than outbound sales is more viable for a single-game studio.

## Context

Onboarding session for a new studio Finance hire, Jul 1 2026. Senior advisor briefing on studio structure, business priorities, and operating model. Studio is ~4 years from launch, with vertical slice targeted for September 2026 as the next investment milestone.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: advising a founder-led studio on first CPO hire -- the scope definition (HR + Finance + IT + Legal + PM) gives a clear job description and removes the ambiguity about what stays with the founder vs what is delegated.
- Relevant when: a studio's art team is disproportionately large -- the cost-neutral thinning model (exit juniors, replace with veterans) avoids net headcount reduction while improving output density.
- Relevant when: a studio has no marketing function yet -- the lean-until-pre-launch model is a defensible alternative to building marketing infrastructure too early.
- Relevant when: explaining the BD model to a new hire -- portal strategy vs outbound sales clarifies that BD at this stage is about market positioning, not individual deal pursuit.
