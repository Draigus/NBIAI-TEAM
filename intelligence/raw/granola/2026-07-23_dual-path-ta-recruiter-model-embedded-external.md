# Dual-Path TA Recruiter Model: Embedded FTC + External Contingency

## Key Content

Hiring strategy for a studio needing to fill ~12-14 roles across a 3-6 month window without committing to a full-time internal TA hire:

**Two-path model (run concurrently):**
- **Embedded TA (FTC):** referral candidate on 6-month fixed-term contract. Informal start possible within days. Owns pipeline management and interview coordination for urgent roles. Cost: FTC salary for 6 months.
- **External recruiter:** 2-month trial with deliverable-based fee structure. Handles hard-to-fill specialist roles. Fee model precedent: £7K embedded monthly fee + 10% contingency on placements (from comparable studio at ~similar headcount).

**Combined cost logic:** 9 months total spend (6 FTC + 2-month external overlap) vs 12 months for a single full-time TA hire. Prior agency spend rolled over to offset ~40-60% of the combined cost.

**Decision protocol:** when stakeholders disagree on the external recruiter, use "backbone disagree and commit" -- the dissenting party voices concern clearly, then commits to the agreed path and does not relitigate it.

**Process dependency:** external recruiter cannot commit to a fee model until they see the hiring plan. Share headcount plan (roles, salary ranges, regions) as the unlock step.

**Salary range modelling inputs:** global benchmarks by region, hub vs non-hub delta (~30% for e.g. London vs secondary UK city). Fully loaded employee cost at early stage: 20-26% uplift above base (pension + NI only; no additional benefit overhead yet).

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: drop prior agency entirely; run embedded FTC TA and external contingency recruiter concurrently
- Glen observed: combined 9-month dual-path cost is lower than 12-month single-hire TA cost when prior agency spend is rolled over
- Pattern identified: external recruiters consistently delay fee model commitment until they see the hiring plan -- share the plan as the unlock, not as a later step
- Pattern identified: "disagree and commit" framing enables team members to voice reservations while maintaining alignment on the chosen direction

## Context

Recruiting strategy session between Glen Pryer (CPO/NBI), Lorenza Menna (Head of HR, CH), and Aris (COO, CH). 2026-07-23. CH has ~12-14 open roles across art, engineering, design, and operations. Prior agency relationship being terminated due to underperformance. Salary ranges being built from global benchmarks by region and hub/non-hub split.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio needs to fill 10+ roles in a 3-6 month window but cannot justify a full-time TA hire at current headcount.
Relevant when: a studio has been overpaying a recruitment agency and wants to transition to a lower-cost model without losing hiring momentum.
Relevant when: an external recruiter is stalling on fee terms -- the hiring plan is the required unlock, not a later deliverable.
Relevant when: a leadership team disagrees on a hiring approach -- the disagree-and-commit framing prevents the minority position from undermining execution.
