# In-House TA Passive Pipeline: Sourcing and Hiring Manager Engagement Methods

## Key Content

Methodology demonstrated by a strong in-house TA practitioner at a ~65-person games studio:

**Passive sourcing over job ads:** Last two hires at prior studio sourced from speculative applicants, not posted roles. Pipeline built continuously through speculative application tracking, employee referrals, proactive direct outreach, and university/career fairs. Result: a warm bench before roles formally open.

**Hiring manager wrangling:** Studio TA practitioners routinely face disengaged hiring managers. Effective tactics: adapt communication style per manager, book calendar placeholders proactively to force interview scheduling rather than asking permission, use passive placeholder framing ("I've blocked time in case you're ready") rather than demanding action.

**Job spec co-writing:** Rather than submitting a spec and waiting weeks for feedback, two faster approaches: (a) draft a complete spec and send for binary yes/no decision, or (b) book a live call and write it together. Both eliminate the multi-week review loop.

**Multi-hat expectation:** Strong in-house TA at smaller studios stretches into adjacent areas (social media, external comms, PR) when recruitment volume drops, with TA always primary. An HR background adds elastic capacity for talent-adjacent work.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO observed: passive sourcing consistently outperforms job ads for quality-of-hire at comparable studios; last two hires at a 300+ person prior employer came from speculative applicants, not postings
- Studio CPO concluded: hiring managers who are unresponsive respond to calendar placeholders, not polite requests -- the TA practitioner must engineer the interaction rather than wait for manager availability
- Studio CPO applied: job spec co-writing (live call or immediate yes/no) eliminates multi-week feedback loops that stall pipeline velocity

## Context

Talent acquisition interview at a ~65-person UK-based MMO studio, July 2026. Candidate was a games recruiter with four years' in-house TA experience describing pipeline methodology unprompted. Studio had hired 12 people in the previous three months via external consultant and was assessing in-house TA capability.
