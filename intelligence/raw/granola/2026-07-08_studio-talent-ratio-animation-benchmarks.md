# Studio Talent Ratio Benchmarks and Animation Throughput Red Flags

## Key Content

Two concrete data points surfaced in a planning session at a ~55-person MMO studio.

**Talent mix benchmarks:**

| Level | Wrong (observed at this studio) | Correct (target for startup in production) |
|-------|-------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Senior/vet | ~4% (~2 of 55) | ~50% |
| Mid | ~36% | ~30% |
| Junior | ~60% | ~20% |

- Studio hired with aspiration to "support juniors" but threw them into production with no mentors and no learning time
- CPO's diagnosis: "Do you think hiring half your staff as juniors is supporting them when you're also trying to build a game?"
- The failure: juniors need a mentor and learning time; a production environment without either delivers neither support nor output
- Recovery: painful but unavoidable; retain junior records and re-engage when studio scales to 150+ with mentors and proper development plans in place
- Pattern: correction at 55-person scale requires an exit wave followed by targeted senior/mid backfill; cannot grow out of it organically

**Animation throughput benchmarks:**

- Red flag threshold: 3 production-quality animations through pipeline in 6 months at a studio with a dedicated animation team
- Context: ~20 blockouts existed but blockouts are not finished animations
- Industry benchmark: animations should reach functional in-game state in ~1--1.5 days from blockout state
- Root cause pattern: studio affection protecting an underperforming team from hard performance conversations; absence of a mentor who could raise output
- CPO framing: "I hired a lot. For all his screaming and yelling, I got three animations."

## Decisions / Insights

- CPO observed: 60% junior hiring at a game startup in production is structurally unsound regardless of intent to develop junior staff; juniors need mentors and learning time, not a production environment.
- CPO observed: the correct startup ratio for a studio in active production is ~50% vets/seniors, ~30% mids, ~20% juniors -- not the aspirational ratio of a developer at full scale.
- CPO observed: 3 production animations in 6 months with ~20 blockouts pending is a hard red flag requiring structural intervention, not coaching.
- CPO observed: studio affection protecting underperforming team members is the named failure mode; it delays structural intervention and compounds the problem.

## Context

Planning session between CPO and EP at a ~55-person MMO studio, 8 Jul 2026. The studio was mid-vertical-slice and planning a wave-one exit of underperforming staff. Animation was flagged as the most acute risk: "The animation team is the thing causing me the most concern" -- blocking vertical slice completion more than any other discipline.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio is hiring to "support junior development" on its first production title -- this is the cautionary case; the junior-heavy approach fails in a production context without dedicated mentors.
- Relevant when: assessing a studio's talent mix during due diligence or advisory engagement -- 50/30/20 (vet/mid/junior) is the target ratio for an early-stage studio in production.
- Relevant when: an animation team's blockout count far exceeds its production-quality animation count -- 1--1.5 days from blockout to functional is the benchmark; anything slower signals a structural problem.
- Relevant when: a studio is considering keeping underperforming team members due to personal relationships -- affection-driven protection is the named failure mode; it delays the structural intervention.
- Relevant when: advising a studio on how to recover from a junior-heavy hire -- exit wave + senior/mid backfill is the correction; retain junior records for re-engagement with mentors at scale.
