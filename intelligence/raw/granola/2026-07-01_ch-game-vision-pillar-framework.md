# Game Vision Pillar Format: Headline + Story + Red-Team Before Studio Rollout

## Key Content

A structured methodology for developing and hardening game vision pillars before communicating them to a studio.

**Pillar format (Simon's framing):**
- Structure: headline, subheading, and story
- Each pillar requires: clear statement, what it means, what it does NOT mean, do/don't examples
- Inspired by Hoshin model: the biggest goal should be felt by the smallest worker on the team
- Both a high-altitude principle AND specific examples are required -- one without the other fails teams at different altitudes

**Red-team test before rollout:**
- Before any pillar goes to the studio, find every way it can be misread or exploited
- Test example: "We don't dangle shinies" has 14+ valid interpretations and conflicts with live service mechanics -- failed the red-team
- Pillar "good level design" has no meaningful opposite (everything is good level design) -- a useless pillar by this test
- Target: a pillar should produce consistent behaviour across the studio without anyone asking what it means

**Good vs. bad examples from industry:**
- Blizzard cited positively: "Bring out your inner geek," "We create epic entertainment" -- clear, evocative, opinionated
- God of War pillars cited negatively: too vague to work with in daily decisions

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: vision pillars must be mandated locked before the studio AMA; three prior requests without result triggered mandatory deadline.
- Glen and creative director agreed: pillar format is headline + subheading + story, each with explicit is/is-not definitions and do/don't examples.
- Glen decided: red-team every pillar for misinterpretation before studio rollout; lead advisor to draft his own pillar version over the weekend and share for comparison.
- Glen and creative director concluded: vague pillars ("good level design") are not pillars -- a pillar must have a meaningful opposite and produce consistent, observable behaviour.

## Context

1:1 session between studio advisor (Glen) and the creative director at a ~65-person MMO studio, Jul 1 2026. The studio had been working on game vision pillars for several months. The session focused on format, red-teaming approach, and the upcoming studio AMA where pillars would be communicated company-wide.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio is struggling to make vision pillars operational -- the is/is-not + do/don't format converts abstract values into testable behavioural guidelines.
- Relevant when: preparing to communicate a creative direction to a large studio -- red-teaming pillars for misinterpretation before rollout prevents confusion and inconsistent application.
- Relevant when: auditing existing pillars for quality -- the "meaningful opposite" test and "consistent behaviour without asking" test quickly identify pillars that are too vague.
- Relevant when: advising a creative director on communication architecture -- the Hoshin principle (biggest goal felt by smallest worker) is a useful framing for why specificity at the example level is not optional.
