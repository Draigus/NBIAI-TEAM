# CEO Priority Framework for a Studio Founder

## Key Content

A three-priority framework for coaching a studio founder-CEO who has not held a CEO role before, designed to focus their attention on the things only the CEO can do.

**The three priorities:**
1. **Champion the culture of the studio** -- visible role model, steward of values, the person the studio looks to when behaviour is ambiguous
2. **Investor relations and fundraising** -- primary external-facing function; the CEO is the right face for capital conversations, not the COO or advisors
3. **Pick three judgment calls per week to make the studio better** -- decision muscle-building; not waiting for problems to escalate, but actively choosing small improvements each week

**COO's parallel priorities for the CEO:**
- Pay attention and remember across meetings (context continuity across conversations)
- Actively challenge reds and yellows (not just receive updates)
- Guide directs rather than simply update them
- Separate awareness from action: knowing something is a problem is not the same as directing it to be fixed

**Common failure mode:** founder-CEO agrees in the room, then reverses after a one-on-one with a subordinate who applies emotional pressure. Pattern is invisible to the COO unless explicitly mapped.

**Coaching tactics:**
- Use silence to force real listening -- do not fill the space for them
- Pick spots rather than nagging; timing matters more than repetition
- "Fake it till you make it" reframe: if the behaviour is right, the habit forms

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: three CEO priorities (culture, investor relations, weekly judgment calls) are the minimum viable coaching frame for a studio founder-CEO in a scaling studio.
- Glen observed: the conflict-avoidance pattern (agree in room, reverse after one-on-one) is the single most destabilising failure mode for a first-time studio CEO; it undermines the entire management chain.
- Glen concluded: using silence as a coaching tool (not filling the conversation) is more effective than direct instruction for a founder who tends to agree reflexively.

## Context

1:1 between senior advisor and COO at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2 2026. The studio is in a period of significant leadership restructuring; the CEO is a first-time CEO and a game creative by background.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: coaching a founder-CEO who has moved from creative lead to studio CEO without formal leadership training -- the three-priority framework gives them a navigable focus.
- Relevant when: a COO flags that the CEO is reversing decisions after individual subordinate conversations -- the conflict-avoidance pattern needs to be named and mapped before it can be coached.
- Relevant when: advising on how to involve a CEO in investor relations -- positioning it as their primary external function (rather than a task to delegate) changes how seriously they prioritise it.
- Relevant when: building a weekly habit for a CEO who is reactive rather than proactive -- "three judgment calls per week" is a concrete decision-muscle exercise.
