# Contractor Exit Protocol: Grace vs. Swift Removal

## Key Content

A studio with a history of abrupt contractor removals (instant Slack deactivation, no goodbye) codified a replacement policy distinguishing hostile from non-hostile exits.

**Core framework:**
- Hostile actors (misconduct, IP risk, security concern): swift, precise, no notice
- Everyone else: thoughtful, caring, and graceful exit -- time to say goodbye, acknowledgement of contribution, celebration of work

**Practical elements of a graceful exit:**
- Slack access maintained for a defined handover period; not cut on announcement
- Team members notified of departure with space to respond
- Work acknowledged publicly before the person leaves
- Exit tone set by leadership, not delegated to HR to handle coldly

**Legal caution:**
- Over-honesty with contractors about reasons for exit creates legal exposure (unfair dismissal analogues, discrimination claims)
- Communicate with dignity without disclosing the full performance rationale to the individual
- Lorenza (or equivalent HR lead) executes; C-level makes the decision but does not handle the conversation

**Reputational framing:**
- Leadership explicitly accepted the reputational risk of graceful exits (possibility that word gets out that the person was let go)
- Graceful treatment of exits is a studio culture signal; cold removal is remembered by the remaining team, not just the person leaving

## Decisions / Insights

- Leadership decided: graceful exit protocol is non-negotiable for non-hostile departures; reputational risk is accepted.
- Leadership framing: "hostile actors get swift and precise; everyone else gets thoughtful, caring, and graceful."
- Leadership identified: prior practice of instant access removal and no goodbye was damaging studio culture internally, not just externally.
- Leadership cautioned: over-honesty about performance reasons in contractor exit conversations increases legal exposure; dignity without full disclosure is the correct posture.

## Context

C-level leadership meeting at a ~55-person game studio undergoing a planned contractor replacement wave, 7 Jul 2026. The studio was processing multiple simultaneous exits and wanted to establish a clear protocol before Lorenza executed them. Prior bad practice (abrupt removal) had been cited as a culture issue by remaining staff.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: advising a studio on contractor exit process -- establish the hostile/non-hostile distinction before any exits are actioned.
- Relevant when: a studio is planning a replacement wave -- the graceful exit protocol reduces internal morale damage from the remaining team observing how departures are handled.
- Relevant when: an HR lead is about to execute multiple contractor exits -- the decision (who, when, why) sits at C-level; execution and communication sit with HR.
- Relevant when: a client has a history of abrupt access removal -- flag that remaining team members notice, and that cold exits signal how they will be treated if their time comes.
