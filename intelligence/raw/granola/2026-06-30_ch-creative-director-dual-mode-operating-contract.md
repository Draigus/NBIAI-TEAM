# Dual-Mode Operating Contract for Creative Directors

## Key Content

A framework for aligning a creative director's operating mode with domain type, addressing the common failure pattern where visionary leaders apply one mode uniformly across all decisions.

**The two modes:**
- **Visionary mode:** expansive, exploratory, generative. Appropriate for open-world/lore design, living world systems, exploration-space definition, long-horizon creative direction. The leader proposes broadly, the team responds and builds.
- **Decisive mode:** scoped, time-bound, locked. Appropriate for combat systems, crafting mechanics, feature-level decisions, vertical slice scope. The leader makes a call and the team executes. No more exploration.

**The operating contract:**
- An explicit agreement between the creative director, CEO, and studio advisor on which domains are visionary and which are decisive
- Written in advance; not negotiated case-by-case
- When in visionary mode: the leader's role is to generate and inspire; execution authority sits elsewhere
- When in decisive mode: the leader's role is to close the loop; revisiting the decision requires a formal escalation, not a hallway conversation
- Without this contract: a visionary leader tends to apply the same exploratory mode to everything, which works for world-building but creates paralysis on feature delivery

**Layer-cake communication model (paired principle):**
- High-level principle at the top (one sentence, communicable in a meeting)
- Bullet-pointed specifics below (for leads)
- Concrete examples at the bottom (for production staff)
- Different team members engage at different altitudes; both levels are needed. Top-down alone is fluffy; bottom-up alone lacks principles.

**Anti-pattern identified:**
- "Us and them" framing by a creative director (positioning themselves and the CEO vs. the studio) signals an unclear operating contract -- the leader has not internalised their role as a studio leader, only as a creative partner to the CEO
- Direct feedback approach: be blunt, no softening; visionary thinkers often need the clearest possible signal to change behavioural patterns

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio advisor decided: an explicit operating contract between creative director, CEO, and advisor must be written before the Thursday product council -- informal alignment is not sufficient.
- Studio advisor concluded: the "us and them" framing is a symptom of an unclear operating contract, not a personality problem -- fixing the contract fixes the behaviour.
- Studio advisor observed: the layer-cake communication model (principle + bullets + examples) is the correct structure for a creative director communicating across a studio with members at different levels of seniority and domain knowledge.
- CEO and studio advisor aligned: visionary mode and decisive mode must be domain-specific -- the creative director cannot choose their own mode per situation.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: advising a studio where a creative director is strong on vision but creating delivery paralysis -- the dual-mode operating contract is the structural fix.
- Relevant when: a studio's creative director has an "us and them" dynamic with the rest of the team -- this is almost always an unclear operating contract, not a personality issue.
- Relevant when: a creative director's communication style is not landing differently across team levels -- the layer-cake model (principle + bullets + examples) is the fix.
- Relevant when: a CEO and creative director are co-leading a studio and the team is unclear about decision authority -- the dual-mode contract makes authority explicit per domain type.
