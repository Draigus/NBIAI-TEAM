# Player Archetype Prioritisation: Early-Adopter Audience Definition Before Feature Decisions

## Key Content

Framework for defining the early-adopter audience before feature prioritisation in a systemic multiplayer game:

**Process order:** internal cohort exercise first, then market research, not the reverse.
1. Core team members each independently draft their view of the target early-adopter audience
2. Head of Design consolidates versions into a single aligned document for the core group to agree on
3. Internal alignment feeds into proper market research with hired expertise (TAM research is not a substitute for internal alignment)
4. External researcher (not internal design/UX team) conducts formal market sizing

**Archetype insight from practice:** "Killers" (Bartle) are the largest enthusiast population in online games but are highly nomadic and transient -- they move on once they have dominated, making them poor retention anchors. Building for killers first means building for players who leave.

**Disney Hub case study:** the largest actual audience for a PvP-adjacent social game was "competitive but friendly" -- not core competitive. This archetype wants the feeling of competition without the toxicity of hardcore PvP. It is often undercounted because it does not self-identify loudly.

**Key principle:** can't build for everyone. Early-adopter focus shapes feature prioritisation. Define who you're building for before deciding what to build.

**Recruiting signal:** when UX candidates are asked "what is this game?" and all five lead with the platform rather than the core experience, that is a recruiting pitch problem that reveals unclear internal positioning.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen and Simon agreed: define the target early-adopter audience before prioritising features -- not after
- Simon observed: killers are nomadic and transient; building for them creates a high-churn early adopter cohort
- Glen observed: if UX candidates lead with the platform rather than the core game, the recruiting pitch has a positioning problem
- Pattern identified: studios mistake market research for audience alignment -- TAM research is not a substitute for the core team agreeing on who they are building for

Relevant when: a studio team has differing views on who the game is for, and these disagreements are surfacing in design reviews or hiring conversations.
Relevant when: a client is about to begin feature prioritisation without a defined early-adopter archetype -- insert the audience alignment exercise first.
