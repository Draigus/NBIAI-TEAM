# Major Publisher Alpha Gate Review: Top-Down Sign-Off Process and Production Plan Requirement

## Key Content

A major Chinese games publisher (investment and publishing partner) requires senior leadership to personally review every funded title at alpha before authorising the next payment milestone.

**Process observed:**
- Senior publisher executive visits the studio in person for the alpha review
- Publisher explicitly dislikes presentation materials (PowerPoint); wants to play the game directly or see UI mockups only
- This creates a "dog-and-pony show" preparation dynamic: studio senior leadership mobilises for the visit
- Publisher organisation is highly top-down: production teams at the publisher hold decisions pending the senior review
- Payment milestone is not released until the senior review is complete and sign-off given

**Outcome spectrum:**
- Best case: full support and funding through to completion
- Worst case: "figure it out yourselves" -- funding reduced or support withdrawn
- Outright cancellation considered unlikely due to the amount already invested (too-big-to-fail dynamic)

**Risk factor identified:**
- Publisher's hard question at alpha: is there a clear production plan (timeline, cost, feature scope)?
- Absence of a coherent production plan is assessed as the primary failure risk at the gate -- not game quality alone
- Third-party acquirer interest (e.g. major Western publisher) may surface if the primary publisher withdraws, but only with a clean production plan already in place

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO observed: this publisher evaluates the game by playing it, not by review materials -- prepare a build, not a deck.
- Studio CPO observed: the real risk at the alpha gate is the absence of a production plan, not the game's quality state.
- Studio CPO observed: outright cancellation of a heavily invested title is unlikely; the risk is a reduction in active support, not termination.
- Studio CPO observed: a viable fallback acquirer will only emerge if the studio has a clean production plan on hand -- the plan is required for any outcome path.

## Context

Operational review between a studio CPO and head of analytics, July 2026. Studio in active development with a major Chinese publisher as the primary investor and publishing partner. Alpha review visit imminent.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: NBI is advising a studio with a Chinese publisher on upcoming milestone reviews -- prepare a playable build, not slides; ensure a production plan with timeline, cost, and feature scope is ready.
- Relevant when: a studio is approaching a publisher payment gate -- the gate is not only about game quality; production planning documentation is equally scrutinised.
- Relevant when: a studio's primary publisher relationship is at risk -- assess whether a Western publisher fallback is viable, and confirm that a production plan exists before initiating outreach.
- Relevant when: a studio describes their publisher as "top-down" with slow decisions -- this is a structural pattern in Chinese publishing relationships; production teams should route requests accordingly and not expect lateral decisions.
- Relevant when: NBI is advising on publisher relations strategy -- too-big-to-fail dynamics reduce outright cancellation risk but do not protect against passive withdrawal of support.
