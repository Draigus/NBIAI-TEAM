# Audience-First Game Design: For/Against Statements Before Pillars

## Key Content

A methodology for grounding game design in audience definition before establishing pillars or feature lists.

**Core principle:** who the game is for must be defined first; pillars and features flow from that filter, not the other way round.

**Format -- "for/against" player experience statements:**
- Write 15-20 statements maximum describing experiences the game is deliberately for or against
- Phrasing: "a game for people who enjoy meaningful risk" (Rust example)
- NOT granular persona archetypes (no Myers-Briggs, Bartle types, or 147-personality grids)
- Statements act as a filter: each design decision is tested against "does this serve who we said we're for?"
- Audience definition operates at a higher altitude than pillars -- pillars are the "how" in service of it

**What this replaces:** traditional persona work at large publishers (e.g. Microsoft/Xbox) that generated profiles too complex for production teams to internalize or apply. Studios using complex personas in daily design decisions are rare; studios using a 15-statement "this is who we're building for" sheet are operationally effective.

**Game loop as theme park model (related):**
- Players move fluidly between PvP, crafting, raiding, exploration based on mood -- not a single linear path
- Intrinsic motivation is the target: player decides, game facilitates, world state changes expand possibility space
- "Values to actions" diagram: player sets a top-level goal, sub-goals and actions flow from it (the game doesn't dictate the path)

## Decisions / Insights

- Design leads decided: define audience (for/against statements) before pillars; pillars must be derivable from the audience definition or they are not well-grounded.
- Design leads concluded: persona archetypes as used at large publishers are too complex to operationalise in day-to-day design decisions; the 15-20 statement "for/against" model is more usable.
- Design leads observed: treating the core loop as a theme park rather than a path gives designers the right mental model for a game with multiple parallel player motivations.

- Relevant when: a game studio's design pillar work feels disconnected from the target player -- the for/against audience pass should precede and ground the pillars.
- Relevant when: a studio is transitioning from publisher-style persona work (complex, not used) to a simpler operational filter that teams can apply in daily decisions.
- Relevant when: advising on game design process for a live-service title with multiple player archetypes -- the theme park loop model prevents the design team from over-engineering a single "correct" path.
