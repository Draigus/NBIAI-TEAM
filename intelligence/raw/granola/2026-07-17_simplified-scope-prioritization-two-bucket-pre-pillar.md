# Simplified Scope Prioritisation for Craft-Immature Teams: Two-Bucket Over MoSCoW

## Key Content

When applying scope prioritisation to a team that lacks the craft maturity to navigate MoSCoW's nuanced categories (Must / Should / Could / Won't), simplify to two buckets: "must be in build" and "must not be in build." Everything else is implicitly deferred.

**Why MoSCoW fails pre-pillar:** MoSCoW works best when held against established pillars -- the team needs a shared reference to evaluate "Should" vs "Could." Without locked pillars, Should/Could become opinion-based and teams stall on debate rather than making decisions. The two-bucket system forces binary decisions that the team can actually execute.

**When to graduate to MoSCoW:** once pillars are established and locked, reintroduce MoSCoW as the standard prioritisation framework. The two-bucket phase is a temporary scaffold, not a permanent methodology.

**Couch Heroes VS1 application:** used to cut roughly one-third of VS1 line items (PVP combat, arena, range combat, loot distribution, mount pets, character creation deferred; core item/entitlement system, consumables, equipment inventory retained).

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen (NBI MD / CPO) decided: not to use this method again once pillars are established; MoSCoW is the right long-term tool.
- Pattern: teams without craft maturity or locked design pillars will stall on MoSCoW's middle categories; forcing binary decisions speeds up the process at the cost of nuance, which is acceptable when getting to build is the priority.
- Observation: player progression can be an "illusion of levelling" (1-2 levels) rather than a full progression system for a vertical slice context -- the two-bucket method enabled this distinction.

## Context

Production meeting at Couch Heroes on 17 Jul 2026, reviewing VS1 scope with the NBI MD/CPO and the studio's EP/producer. Pillars are in progress (22-page document being condensed to ~2 pages); scope prioritisation could not wait for pillar lock, hence the simplified method.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio needs to scope-cut for a vertical slice or demo milestone before design pillars are locked -- the two-bucket method gets decisions made without requiring pillar alignment.
Relevant when: a prioritisation session is stalling on Should/Could debates -- reframe as binary (in / out) and commit to revisiting nuance once the baseline scope is established.
Relevant when: advising a team new to structured scope management -- introduce two-bucket first, then graduate to MoSCoW once the team understands how to evaluate trade-offs against a reference framework.
Relevant when: onboarding as a new CPO/advisor to a studio mid-production -- a binary scope cut is often the fastest way to create shared understanding of what the vertical slice actually is.
