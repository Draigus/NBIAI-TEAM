# MMO Game Pillar Design Methodology: Structure, Framing, and Feature Alignment

## Key Content

Pillar count and structure: 5-10 pillars per game. Each pillar requires three components:
1. What it is (positive definition)
2. What it is not (anti-definition -- the Overton window setter)
3. Failure signals (observable indicators the pillar has been violated in a feature or system)

Feature-to-pillar mapping rule: every feature must map to at least one pillar. Features that cannot be mapped are red flags and should be cut or deferred. Features that map to multiple pillars are desirable -- they deliver disproportionate value per unit of scope.

Language risk: reductive "what it is not" language can be over-corrected by teams. Example: "no numbers games" risks being read as "never show numbers" -- the intent is Overton window setting, not a hard prohibition. Pillar documents require careful framing to prevent misapplication.

Steam tag thought experiment for market positioning: feed the game's lore or design document to an AI and ask it to infer Steam tags. The resulting tag list surfaces implied genre positioning and reveals missing signals. Useful early check before market positioning is locked.

Ideation process fix for founder-led studios: centralise unilateral idea distribution into a single shared channel with all creative leads. Prevents each recipient forming different priority impressions from the same idea.

## Decisions / Insights

- CPO and Game Director agreed: 5-10 pillars is the right range; fewer loses nuance, more creates navigation overhead
- CPO and Game Director agreed: "what it is not" framing is required for every pillar -- positive definition alone is insufficient because teams default to maximising the positive without understanding the constraint
- CPO decided: features without pillar alignment are not in the game; pillar alignment is a gate, not a guideline
- Pattern identified: the Steam tag thought experiment is a fast, low-cost market positioning check that surfaces implied genre signals the team may not have intended
- Pattern identified: founder-led studios frequently distribute ideas to multiple individuals without shared context, causing divergent priority models across the leadership team

## Context

Two-hour design alignment session between CPO (Glen) and the Game Director of a 55-70 person MMO studio. Purpose: align on pillar structure before presenting to wider creative leadership. Both parties produced independent pillar documents that showed significant overlap. 2026-07-22.

## Applicability

Relevant when: facilitating a game pillar definition process with a client studio -- the three-component structure (is / is not / failure signals) is directly deployable as a workshop format.
Relevant when: a studio is in scope-creep risk and features are being added without clear alignment gates -- pillar mapping as a feature gate is the structural fix.
Relevant when: advising on market positioning for a game in development -- the Steam tag thought experiment is a 30-minute exercise that surfaces misalignments early.
Relevant when: a founder-led studio is creating misaligned priorities across the leadership team through unilateral idea distribution -- the centralised ideation channel is the operational fix.
Relevant when: reviewing a game design document that uses reductive language in pillar definitions -- flag the over-correction risk and reframe as Overton window setting.
