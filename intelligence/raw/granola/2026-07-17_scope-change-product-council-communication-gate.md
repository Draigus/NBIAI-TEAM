# Scope Change Communication Protocol: Product Council Gate Before Team Announcement

## Key Content

Incident at Couch Heroes: a late-night scope addition (magic abilities for VS1 combat, investor-driven) was announced directly to the team by the Game Director without context. One line item added with no mention of what was being cut to accommodate it. The Art Director's team was blindsided; the Director had no answers for his reports when they came to him with questions.

Root cause: the change was communicated in isolation, framed as mandatory rather than exploratory. Intent was sound (investor request, immediate transparency) but execution bypassed the Product Council layer that exists to gate team-facing announcements.

**Fix agreed:** all scope changes -- additions, cuts, or deferrals -- go through Product Council first. Product Council validates the change, understands the trade-offs, and owns the team-facing communication. No direct team announcements of scope changes outside this gate.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen (NBI MD / CPO) decided: Product Council is the mandatory gate for all scope changes before team communication.
- Art Director observed: receiving a scope change without the cut context is worse than receiving no communication -- it creates uncertainty and distrust simultaneously.
- Pattern: investor-driven scope changes are high-velocity and bypass normal production gates; a named gate (Product Council) with explicit ownership prevents this from becoming a recurring failure.

## Context

1:1 between NBI MD and Couch Heroes Art Director on 17 Jul 2026. Scope change incident occurred the previous day and had already caused team confusion by the time of this meeting. The fix was codified immediately.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio on scope communication protocols -- a named gate (Product Council or equivalent) with explicit authority over team-facing announcements prevents investor urgency from bypassing production coordination.
Relevant when: a scope change must be communicated quickly -- the gate does not need to be slow; a 30-minute Product Council alignment call before team announcement is sufficient to prevent the "one line item with no context" failure.
Relevant when: building a Product Council operating model at a studio -- scope change authority is one of the most critical responsibilities to assign explicitly; ambiguity here creates recurring communication failures.
Relevant when: reviewing a team's response to scope uncertainty -- if the team is getting contradictory or uncontextualised scope information, check whether a Product Council gate is in place and enforced.
