# Junior vs. Senior Mindset Diagnostic in Production Teams

## Key Content

A crisp behavioural diagnostic for distinguishing genuine seniority from title seniority in a production environment, particularly under vertical slice delivery pressure.

**Junior mindset:** "When do I get to see the game?" Waits for systems to be built before engaging. Responds to partial builds with dismissal ("we don't have the world, so it doesn't mean anything").

**Senior mindset:** Reads the roadmap and figures out what to close before the next system lands. Does not need the full context to contribute -- identifies the next constraint and works backwards from the delivery date.

**Practical implication:**
- The response to incomplete vertical slice progress is the diagnostic moment
- A junior-mindset response treats incompleteness as a reason to defer contribution
- A senior-mindset response treats incompleteness as the work to close
- Useful for assessing attitude fit during hiring, probation, or performance reviews

**Coaching implication:** rather than replacing a junior-mindset contributor directly, prefer hiring someone who can model and mentor the senior mindset. Overlap the two during onboarding, then exit the previous person once the new standard is visible to the team.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen observed: the response a team member gives to a partial vertical slice build is a cleaner seniority signal than their title or years of experience.
- Glen concluded: replacing a junior-mindset contributor directly is a blunt tool; mentoring via a senior new hire is the preferred approach when time allows.

## Context

1:1 between senior advisor and Art Producer at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2 2026. Discussion of a team member whose response to vertical slice progress frames the gap between junior and senior production attitudes. Arose in the context of a team undergoing significant turnover and vertical slice delivery pressure.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: assessing a mid-production team member's attitude toward incomplete builds -- the "wait and see" response is the tell.
- Relevant when: a team member with adequate technical skill is not contributing proportionally -- the mindset gap may be the root cause more than the skill gap.
- Relevant when: deciding whether to replace or mentor a junior-mindset contributor -- if timeline allows, hiring a senior to model the mindset first is lower risk than a direct replacement.
- Relevant when: writing interview questions for mid-senior art or production roles -- ask how they would approach a sprint where the game's core systems are not yet integrated.
