# Vertical Slice Velocity Tactics: Animation Scope Reduction and Outsourcing

## Key Content

A ~55-person MMO studio identified environment art and engineering as the two critical-path constraints for a three-month vertical slice. Interventions applied:

**Animation scope reduction:**
- Traversal animations and ACF (Animation Control Flow) animations pulled from the core animator team scope
- Rationale: these categories can be sourced from animation catalog/library assets, freeing senior animators for bespoke character work
- Impact: reduces the number of senior animators needed in the vertical slice window

**Outsourcing prop kit asset modelling:**
- Decision: outsource prop kit assets to a contract team rather than hire additional world builders
- Rationale: faster ramp-up, parallel capacity, avoids permanent headcount for a time-bounded need
- Target model: approximately 12-person contract team over a 4-week sprint
- Action: art director to identify outsource partners and return with a list and pricing

**Baking lights for vertical slice:**
- Multiple senior engineering and art leads independently recommended baking lights
- Estimated compute reduction: approximately 50%
- Trade-off: reduced dynamic lighting flexibility; acceptable for a vertical slice quality bar
- Decision pending: art director to provide a craft-level pros/cons assessment before commitment

**Prioritisation principle:** for vertical slice, definition of done is not launch quality. Leads and seniors must not sandbox to 2x estimates against a launch quality bar.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO decided: pull traversal and ACF animations from animator scope; use animation library assets.
- Studio CPO decided: outsource prop kit asset modelling rather than expand headcount for the vertical slice window.
- Multiple senior leaders recommended: bake lights for vertical slice to reduce compute ~50%; final decision pending craft-level assessment.
- Studio CPO observed: definition of done for vertical slice is not launch quality; estimates sandbagged against a launch quality bar must be corrected.

## Context

Senior production meeting at a ~55-person MMO studio in a three-month vertical slice production window, July 2026. Environment art and engineering identified as the two primary critical-path constraints. The studio has limited runway and cannot rely on headcount additions materialising in time.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio's vertical slice schedule is blocked by environment art velocity -- prop kit outsourcing is the fastest capacity lever; identify partners and cost before committing headcount.
- Relevant when: animation team velocity is a constraint -- audit scope for catalog-eligible work (traversal, ACF, common cycles) before hiring.
- Relevant when: a studio is debating baked vs dynamic lighting for a vertical slice -- baking is the standard recommendation; ask for craft-level trade-off from the art director before committing.
- Relevant when: estimates from leads seem inflated -- challenge them to the vertical slice quality bar, not launch quality; this typically reduces estimates materially.
- Relevant when: a studio cannot hire fast enough to fill a vertical slice gap -- outsource burst capacity for time-bounded, scoped deliverables.
