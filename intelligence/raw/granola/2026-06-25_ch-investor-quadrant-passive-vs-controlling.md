# Investor Targeting Framework: Passive vs. Controlling Quadrant

## Key Content

Game studio raising $120M (live-service MMO; prior comparable raise was $200-240M adjusted ~$190M). Round structure preference: 3-4 larger checks rather than 20 x $500K -- concentration reduces dilution complexity and board coordination overhead.

**Investor targeting quadrant (4 categories):**
- **Ideal -- Passive + Reliable:** family offices, select patient capital funds; want returns, not board seats; provide stability during long development cycles; can absorb milestone delays without forcing intervention
- **Acceptable -- Strategic + Reliable:** gaming-sector funds with genuine operator insight; may want one board observer; add value beyond capital
- **Avoid -- Passive + Unreliable:** fast-money or tourist capital; will not stabilise in a downturn; treat the investment as a financial position, not a product commitment
- **Avoid -- Controlling + Any:** large PE/institutional funds (Redbird, Blackrock-style) seeking full board control; their incentive structure misaligns with long development timelines; will force premature monetisation decisions

**Key design principles:**
- Define the ideal investor profile before outreach -- criteria established upfront allow the studio to decline inappropriate capital early without losing credibility
- The studio has the right to fire investors: a precedent of removing investors who became destructive to the creative process has been established; criteria for "destructive" should be defined pre-raise
- California and New York family office networks are underutilised for gaming -- they provide the patient capital profile without the board control pressure

**Sunk cost framing for the raise narrative:** the existing team investment is a known figure; the raise narrative should target reducing "throwaway" sunk cost (team with wrong skills, poor management) rather than defending the gross spend. The ratio matters more than the total.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio advisor decided: build out the investor quadrant framework with the COO before outreach begins -- criteria must be defined, not improvised.
- Studio advisor concluded: 3-4 large checks are preferable to 20 small ones -- cap table complexity scales non-linearly and board dynamics degrade with too many voices.
- Studio advisor observed: family offices are a better fit than institutional funds for a live-service MMO with a multi-year development horizon -- the incentive structures align.
- Studio advisor noted: VC investors who were "scared a little bit" by a runway conversation are not ideal long-term partners -- investor resilience under bad news is a selection criterion.

## Context

Internal planning session at a ~65-person live-service MMO studio, VDR kickoff 1st July, raise targeting through August. Named investors and specific financial details withheld. Named individuals and specific studio anonymised.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: advising a game studio on fundraising strategy -- present the four-quadrant framework before they start mapping investors.
- Relevant when: a studio is evaluating a term sheet from an institutional PE fund -- apply the board-control screen; long development cycles require patient capital.
- Relevant when: a studio founder has not defined their ideal investor profile -- prompt this conversation before outreach, not during negotiation.
- Relevant when: a studio is building a cap table for a later-stage raise -- model dilution at 3-4 investor concentration vs. 20, then show the board dynamics comparison.
- Relevant when: a studio has received destructive board input in the past -- codify what "destructive" means as a criterion that can be applied in due diligence on the investor.
