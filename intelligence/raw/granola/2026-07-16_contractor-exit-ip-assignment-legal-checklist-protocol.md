# Contractor Exit: IP Assignment as a Separate Legal Step for Multi-Jurisdiction Tenures

## Key Content

For contractors with long tenure (2+ years) who signed contracts across multiple entities or jurisdictions, the standard HR exit document is insufficient. An additional legal step -- an IP assignment agreement -- is required and must be drafted separately by legal counsel, not bundled into the HR exit package.

**Trigger conditions for this additional step:**
- Contractor signed contracts under different entity structures during their tenure (e.g. an operating company plus a holding company or a Cyprus entity alongside a UK entity)
- Different IP clauses apply across those contracts, creating an assignment gap that the standard exit doc does not close

**Process:**
1. Legal drafts the IP assignment agreement (separate from the exit doc)
2. HR receives the complete package (exit doc + IP assignment) before scheduling the exit conversation
3. The exit conversation happens only when both documents are ready -- not before

**Status reporting protocol for complex exits:** leadership needs a binary "green / not green" signal, not a narrative update. Responsible parties (legal, HR) each confirm their workstream in bullets only: what is done, what is pending. This prevents the exit from proceeding on an assumed "we're working on it" that means different things to different people.

## Decisions / Insights

- General counsel decided: IP assignment is a distinct legal deliverable from the exit doc; it cannot be delegated to HR and must be drafted before the closing conversation is scheduled.
- Founder/CPO decided: adopt "green / not green" binary status language for all complex exits; bullet-format updates only -- no narrative; this forces each workstream owner to state their actual position.
- General counsel observed: multi-jurisdiction contract tenure creates IP assignment gaps that are invisible in a standard exit process; the gap only becomes visible when legal reviews the full contract history.

## Context

Legal handoff call on 16 Jul 2026 between an advisory firm MD and the general counsel of a Cyprus-domiciled, UK-operating gaming studio. A long-tenure contractor with contracts across multiple entity structures was being exited that week. The general counsel was preparing a clean handoff of all in-flight workstreams before a two-week leave period.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio on a contractor exit where the individual was employed across entity structures or jurisdictions -- require legal to review the full contract history and draft a separate IP assignment before HR begins the exit conversation.
Relevant when: a studio is managing multiple exits simultaneously -- introduce "green / not green" binary status language per workstream; prevents partial progress being reported as readiness.
Relevant when: a studio's general counsel is going on leave mid-exit -- require all exit documents to be fully drafted and handed to HR before departure; a half-drafted IP assignment is not a handoff.
Relevant when: advising on contractor-to-employee or contractor exit policies -- multi-entity contract histories are common in Cyprus-domiciled studios with UK operations; build the IP assignment step into the standard exit SOP, not as a special case.
