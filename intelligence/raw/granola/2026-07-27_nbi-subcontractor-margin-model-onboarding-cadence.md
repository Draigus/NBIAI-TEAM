# Consultant Subcontracting Margin Model: Specialist Delivery with NBI Oversight Layer

## Key Content

NBI engagement model for situations where a client has a defined budget and needs specialist deliverables (forecasts, pitch decks, go-to-market plans, data rooms) but NBI does not hold that specialist capacity in-house: engage a specialist subcontractor directly, position NBI as the oversight and quality layer, retain a margin.

**Financial structure observed:**
- Client budget: ~$30k to deploy
- Specialist subcontractor engagement: ~$20k
- NBI margin retained: ~$10k
- Client receives: crisp, structured deliverables from the specialist with NBI framing and QA

**Subcontractor selection criteria observed:**
- Must be able to build the specific deliverable type (forecasts, decks, market analysis, data rooms) independently
- Risk flag: intolerance for what the specialist perceives as low-quality direction -- manage this upfront with a direct conversation about client context before any joint sessions
- Subcontractor should be screened for the gap between their comfort with structured delivery and their tolerance for unstructured clients

**Onboarding cadence:**
1. Solo onboarding session with the specialist: ~2 hours (context, client background, deliverable standards)
2. Joint session with the client and specialist together: ~1-1.5 hours (alignment, scope, workflow)
3. Weekly touchpoint cadence thereafter

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: when a client has budget and specific deliverable needs, engage a specialist subcontractor at ~67% of the client fee and retain ~33% as NBI overhead/oversight margin
- Glen observed: a specialist with low tolerance for ambiguity or perceived stupidity must be prepped in a solo session before any client contact -- joint sessions without that prep risk damaging the client relationship
- Glen concluded: a subcontractor who "can't close" (strong on soft skills but weak on tangible deliverables) is not viable regardless of personal fit -- deliverable output is the filter

## Context

NBI internal planning discussion, 2026-07-27. Client has ~$30k in discretionary advisory budget seeking structured deliverables. Two subcontractor candidates evaluated. One ruled out for weak deliverable track record despite strong relationship skills. Second selected for strong forecast/deck/go-to-market capability with a flagged personality risk (intolerance for low-quality direction) requiring upfront expectation-setting.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a client has a defined budget and specific deliverable needs that exceed NBI's in-house specialist capacity -- the subcontract margin model is a viable structure.
Relevant when: onboarding a specialist subcontractor who will have client contact -- the two-step cadence (solo prep then joint session) protects the client relationship from specialist personality risks.
Relevant when: screening advisory subcontractors -- "strong on relationship, weak on deliverables" is a disqualifier; "strong deliverables, personality risk" is manageable with upfront expectation-setting.
Relevant when: pricing NBI engagements where a specialist is required -- the ~67%/33% split (specialist fee vs. NBI margin) is a reference point for subcontracting structures.
