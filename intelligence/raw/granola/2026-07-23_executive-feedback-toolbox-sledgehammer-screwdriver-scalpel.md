# Executive Feedback Toolbox: Sledgehammer / Screwdriver / Scalpel Model

## Key Content

Glen's tiered feedback methodology for coaching studio executives and managing founder behaviour in mixed-company settings:

**The three tools:**
- **Sledgehammer:** direct, private. Used to set the anchor. Without this, public techniques have no foundation to land on.
- **Screwdriver:** adjusting. Applied when calibration is needed, not a full reset.
- **Scalpel:** subtle, in mixed company. Precise and minimal -- effective only because the private sledgehammer already hit.

**Operating principle:** private conversations set the anchor; public techniques only land because the private work already happened. Public correction without prior private alignment is ineffective and damages credibility.

**Escalation ladder:**
1. Give information (share what you observed)
2. Observe capacity (does the person pick it up?)
3. Raise directness only if needed

Nine times out of ten, the escalation ladder does not need to reach full directness. Passive/subtle feedback often fails -- people do not pick up on what the giver expects them to notice.

**Public correction rule:** almost never do it. Reframe instead: "good that we've identified this, let's improve" rather than direct criticism. A scalpel move (stepping in front of someone in an exec call to redirect) is not a public attack -- it is subtle and target-visible, not audience-visible.

**Sticky note coaching tool (supplementary):** three keywords on the left of the monitor (how to show up) and tools on the right. Used by Glen at Dell after a Stephen Covey retreat. Risk: subjects may tunnel-vision on the words and over-index to extremes. Mitigation: when they over-index, ask "what's the other word on your sticky?" Narrative and metaphor framing works well alongside keyword prompts.

**Timing risk:** internalising tools to unconscious habit takes years. Need patience with each individual tool before layering in the next.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen concluded: public criticism should almost never happen -- reframing is the correct public move; sledgehammer conversations are always private
- Glen observed: passive/subtle feedback consistently fails; people do not register what the giver expects -- escalation to directness is required more often than coaches expect
- Glen and Aris agreed: the sticky note framework risks tunnel-vision extremes; the mitigation is prompting the other word when over-indexing is observed
- Pattern identified: founders who attempt to adopt a "standard CEO profile" often become less effective than when they lead as themselves -- coaching goal is to channel native leadership style, not replace it
