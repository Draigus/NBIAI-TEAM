# Studio Culture Articulation in Hiring: Radical Candor and Social Contract Land Strongly

## Key Content

Culture pitch delivered by a studio CEO in a senior design candidate interview, July 2026:

**Radical candor framing:** "I love you, but that's awful" -- the studio explicitly separates critique of the work from judgement of the person. Harm by accident: assume good intent and move on. Harm with intent: different story.

**No blame culture + rapid iteration:** Directness is not optional; it is the studio's operating mode. The team signs an informal "social contract" on joining: directness, no corporate formality, gamer culture norms.

**Candidate response:** The candidate stated it was the first time she had heard this kind of culture articulated clearly in her career. The explicit articulation -- naming the principle and giving an example phrase -- was the differentiating factor. Most studios claim culture; few name the behaviours.

**Remote social fabric elements disclosed:** Slack-heavy communication (CEO disclosed 20-hour Slack days), in-person meetups in Greece, GDC/Gamescom overlap for international team members.

**Candidate's own framework:** She distinguished between "harm by accident" and "harm with intent" as her personal filter for giving and receiving difficult feedback. This mirrored the studio's stated position, which accelerated culture fit assessment.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CEO concluded: naming the specific phrase ("I love you, but that's awful") and the intent/accident distinction is more credible than abstract claims about "no blame culture"
- Studio CEO observed: the social contract (signed informally on joining) creates accountability for directness, not just aspiration
- Studio CPO noted: candidates with strong personal frameworks for feedback reciprocity are faster to assess for culture fit -- their own language tells you whether they have internalised the principle

## Context

Senior UI/UX Designer candidate interview at a ~60-person UK/Cyprus-based MMO studio, July 2026. Interview panel: CEO and CPO. Candidate had ~10-12 years' industry experience, primarily UI art at a major games studio. Culture portion of the interview was approximately 15 minutes.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio on how to pitch culture in hiring -- naming a specific behaviour phrase ("I love you, but that's awful") is more memorable and credible than abstract values statements.
Relevant when: preparing a studio CEO or CPO for candidate culture interviews -- the "harm by accident vs. harm with intent" distinction is a simple, testable frame that candidates can react to and demonstrate alignment with.
Relevant when: a remote studio is struggling to communicate culture to candidates -- making the social fabric concrete (communication tool, in-person cadence, geographic touchpoints) grounds abstract culture claims.
Relevant when: assessing candidate culture fit quickly -- ask for their personal framework for giving or receiving critical feedback; candidates who have internalised a principle will have one; those who haven't will give generic answers.
