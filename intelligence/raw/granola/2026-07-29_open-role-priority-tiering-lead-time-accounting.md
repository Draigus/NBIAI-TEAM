# Open Role Priority Tiering: P1/P2 with Lead-Time Accounting

## Key Content

A role priority rating system that accounts for both criticality and hiring lead time:

**P1:** Role is critical now. Must be hired immediately. Examples at a ~65-person MMO studio: systems designer, environment artists (2-3 needed ahead of a vertical slice milestone).

**P2:** Role is not critical for the current milestone. Not needed for 6-8 months. But if hiring lead time is 3 months, the hiring process must open now to land the hire in time. Example: QA lead rated P2 (not critical until VS2/VS3, ~8 months out) with a 3-month lead time = open immediately.

**The key insight:** P2 does not mean "hire later." It means the role is not urgent but the process is. Conflating role criticality with hiring urgency produces roles that open too late to fill before they are needed.

**Budget transition:** When a studio shifts from "what do you need?" to "what can we cut?", P2 roles are the first scrutiny target. But P2 roles with long lead times should remain open unless explicitly frozen -- the cost of a late hire is higher than the cost of an early one.

**Headcount plan structure:** Separate approved roles from proposed roles in the headcount document. The two categories have different sign-off processes; conflating them creates stakeholder confusion about what is authorised.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO applied: QA lead rated P2 (not critical for 8 months) opened immediately due to 3-month lead time -- P2 criticality rating does not mean P2 hiring urgency
- Studio CPO observed: when burn rate tightens and cuts are needed, P2 roles are scrutinised first -- but roles with long lead times must remain open unless explicitly frozen to avoid a more expensive late-hire problem
- Studio CPO decided: headcount plan must separate approved roles from proposed roles; the two categories require different stakeholder sign-off and must not be combined in a single list

## Context

Leadership sync at a ~65-person MMO studio, July 2026. Headcount plan was nearing finalisation; studio had shifted from growth posture ("what do you need?") to constraint posture ("what can we cut?") as burn rate became a visible concern. P1/P2 framework applied to rationalise the open headcount list ahead of a clean Excel export for stakeholder sign-off.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio on which open roles to hire for first -- apply P1/P2 criticality tiering, then adjust each P2 role for lead time; a P2 role with a 3-month lead time may need to open before some P1 roles with shorter pipelines.
Relevant when: a studio is moving from growth to constraint and needs to rationalise open headcount -- P2 is the first scrutiny target, but lead-time-adjusted roles should survive cuts unless explicitly frozen.
Relevant when: building a headcount plan for a studio -- separate approved vs. proposed roles; the two categories need different sign-off processes and must not be mixed in a single list presented to investors or board.
Relevant when: a production team argues to delay a support hire because the role "isn't needed yet" -- calculate the lead time and show when the process must open to land the hire before the need becomes critical.
