# Game Design Pillar Craft: Writing One-Liner Creative Pillars

## Key Content

A game design leadership session produced a clear methodology for crafting game design pillars -- the short statements that anchor a game's creative direction for all disciplines.

**Structural model:**
- Pillar = a one-liner mic drop; supporting documentation is the "Bible"
- Analogy: Ten Commandments is the line; the Bible is the parables that explain and illustrate it
- Must survive being read once by a new hire with no context; cannot require explanation to land

**Common failure modes in pillar wording:**
- Too broad: "Every adventure should feel different" was read by engineering as implying full procedural generation or multi-combinatorial outcomes
- Word choice shapes implementation: "choice of words is a choice of worlds"
- Language that excludes non-native English speakers ("rote") or requires lore context to interpret (world-specific nouns without context)
- Poetic language ("canvas") that reads differently across roles and disciplines

**Generation method:**
- Write 20 candidate lines per pillar; do not judge in the moment
- "Let them cook" before selecting -- immediate judgement closes off options
- Select candidates by semantic clarity across cultures, roles, and reading levels
- Expect multiple pillar-crafting sessions: "we may need to do this two or three times"

**Cross-pillar check:**
- Each pillar must stand alone and pass the mic-drop test independently
- Pillars should support each other thematically but feel distinct enough not to merge

**Pillar splitting test:**
- If one pillar contains two separable ideas (each covering >10 min of distinct gameplay), consider splitting
- Example: a pillar about "feeling different" potentially hides two distinct pillars -- player choice paths, and surprise/novelty encounters

**Candidates that landed during session:**
- "Crafting the journey is its own reward" -- covered questing, builds, fishing, all player types; strongest reaction
- "Identity isn't chosen, it's practiced" -- for a player identity / reputation pillar
- "Your habits are your hero" -- alternative for the same pillar

## Decisions / Insights

- Creative leadership decided: 20+ candidate lines per pillar is the generation method; selection is a separate stage from generation.
- Creative leadership observed: "every adventure should feel different" was too broad and engineering-misleading; strong candidates emerged from workshopping.
- Creative leadership decided: pillars must work as one-liners; supporting detail goes in documentation, not in the pillar itself.
- Creative leadership observed: "choice of words is a choice of worlds" -- engineering teams interpret wording literally and build systems against it.
- Creative leadership decided: session output is candidate lines only; no final selection in the same session.

## Context

Game direction session at a ~55-person MMO studio, 8 Jul 2026. Attendees: CPO, CEO/Creative Director, and a senior creative lead tasked with generating candidate lines. The studio was two years into development with no formally locked design pillars; this was the beginning of a multi-session pillar definition process.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio needs to define or redefine its game design pillars -- 20-candidate generation followed by cross-role semantic selection is the method.
- Relevant when: a studio's design pillars are too broad and being misinterpreted by engineering -- "choice of words is a choice of worlds" insight applies; workshop 20 candidate lines.
- Relevant when: advising on pillar documentation structure -- one-liner + supporting document (not a long pillar statement) is the right format.
- Relevant when: a studio's pillar appears to contain two separate design ideas -- split test: can each idea stand alone as a mic drop?
- Relevant when: a studio is writing pillars with poetic or jargon-heavy language -- flag words that exclude non-English speakers or require lore context to interpret.
