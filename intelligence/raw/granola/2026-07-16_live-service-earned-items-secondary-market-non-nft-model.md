# Live Service Earned-Item Secondary Market: CS:GO Model Without the NFT Problem

## Key Content

A proposed live service economy model for an MMO in development: players earn items through gameplay and can trade them for virtual currency redeemable across the developer's game ecosystem. Modelled on Counter-Strike skin economy, explicitly not on NFT/crypto. Scarcity and legacy value are the appeal -- not speculative ownership.

**Two-currency architecture:**
- In-game currency: earned through play, no real-world value, used for in-world transactions
- Virtual currency: purchased with real money, used for MTX purchases and secondary market item trades

**Revenue streams from the secondary market:**
- Transaction fee (banker's fee) on all item trades
- Revenue share back to partner games whose items are traded -- creates a strong incentive for ecosystem partnerships; partner games benefit financially from their items circulating in the secondary market

**Critical guardrail:** the model must never be perceived as NFT or crypto. This framing would damage the game's reputation with its target audience. No blockchain, no speculation narrative, no "digital ownership" language. The appeal is CS:GO (skill-based economy, legacy items, community value) not Web3.

**Dependencies:** live service structure and item economy must be designed in alignment with the game's design pillars before the monetisation model is finalised. Economy design that contradicts pillar commitments (e.g. a no-pay-to-win commitment) requires resolution before pitching to investors.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio COO decided: secondary market for earned items is the live service monetisation direction; decision taken in principle, pending live service document to formalise the model.
- Studio COO and CPO agreed: two-currency model (in-game earned + virtual purchased) is the architecture; single-currency models create pay-to-win risk.
- Studio CPO flagged: live service structure must align with game pillars before monetisation model is finalised; pillar-first, then economy.
- Studio COO observed: revenue share back to partner games is the strongest incentive for ecosystem partnerships; this turns the secondary market into a business development tool.

## Context

Strategic planning session on 16 Jul 2026 between studio COO and advisory firm MD at a ~70-person MMO studio. Live service and monetisation design was being drafted ahead of a Gamescom investor pitch. The studio's pitch deck requires a credible revenue model; the secondary market concept was surfaced as the primary live service differentiator.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising an MMO or live service game on monetisation design -- the CS:GO earned-item secondary market model offers a non-predatory revenue stream that appeals to a core audience while generating transaction fee revenue at scale.
Relevant when: a studio needs a partnership incentive structure -- revenue share on secondary market trades from partner game items is a concrete financial reason for other studios to join an ecosystem; more compelling than brand alignment alone.
Relevant when: a studio is designing a virtual economy and worried about pay-to-win perception -- two-currency architecture (earned in-game / purchased virtual) is the standard separation; single-currency risks pay-to-win no matter how it is framed.
