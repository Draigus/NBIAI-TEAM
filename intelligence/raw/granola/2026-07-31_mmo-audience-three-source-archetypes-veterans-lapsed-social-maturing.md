# MMO Audience: Three Source Archetypes for a New Entry

## Key Content

Three source player pools identified for a new MMO game -- not personas, but audience archetypes describing who exists in the market that a new MMO could capture if built correctly.

**Archetype 1 -- Veterans:** Loved MMOs at their peak (rise of WoW era), still love games, cannot commit to a roster or guild schedule, want to play with friends but MMOs demand time investment that no longer fits. Current platform: Old School RuneScape (nostalgic, low-commitment).

**Archetype 2 -- Lapsed social players:** Want to play games with their kids or friends but MMOs do not support casual, drop-in social play. Dropped out of MMOs when life changed. Current platform: Fortnite, Minecraft -- games that accommodate irregular social play.

**Archetype 3 -- Maturing up:** Social players (Animal Crossing), creative players (The Sims), roleplayers (tabletop, single-player RPGs) who have never had an MMO built for them. Not ex-MMO players -- never found an entry point that served their play style.

**Use of archetypes:** these are guardrails ("who we're for vs. who we're not"), not persona documents. They inform feature prioritisation: distinctive and lovable features that serve these three groups first, not breadth of zones or content volume.

**Design implication:** deep player choice (combat-focused or non-combat, both fully supported), growth that is not combat-gated, and a life in the game that belongs to the player are the hooks for all three archetypes.

## Decisions / Insights

- Design lead identified three source archetypes; founding CEO thought two were sufficient -- CPO agreed all three are distinct
- CPO concluded: audience framework informs what to prioritise in next phase -- distinctive and lovable features first, not 10 zones

## Context

Design and narrative positioning session at a ~60-person UK/Cyprus MMO studio, July 2026. Studio in pre-production, pillar development ongoing. Audience framework being developed for pillars deck (Vardy/Robin review target: Tuesday 4 August).

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a new MMO on positioning -- the three archetypes (veterans, lapsed social, maturing up) cover the accessible addressable market for a new entrant without head-on competition for active WoW or FFXIV players.
Relevant when: building an investor pitch for an MMO -- the archetype framework answers "who is this for?" concisely and without claiming to compete with WoW directly.
Relevant when: prioritising features in MMO pre-production -- the archetypes surface which hooks must be present at early access vs. which are breadth features.
Relevant when: assessing MMO studio product-market fit -- test whether the studio's feature list serves at least two of the three archetypes; gap analysis flags positioning risk.
