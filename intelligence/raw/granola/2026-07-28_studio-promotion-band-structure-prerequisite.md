# Studio Promotion Framework: Band Structure as Prerequisite to Individual Promotions

## Key Content

At a ~55-70 person studio, a CPO blocked an individual promotion request pending a studio-wide band structure. The principle: promotions to a level that has no defined examples or criteria are meaningless and create precedent problems.

**Correct sequencing:**
1. Define the band structure first: what does junior, mid, and senior look like in this discipline, with concrete examples?
2. Require multi-department sign-off on each promotion: the direct manager alone cannot promote to senior without validation from adjacent leads who can assess cross-functional impact
3. Limit promotion slots per cycle to prevent grade inflation: not everyone who is "ready" gets promoted in the same cycle

**Why the block matters:** An individual promotion to "senior" without a defined senior standard either sets a precedent (this person becomes the de facto benchmark) or is arbitrary (the title means whatever the manager says it means). Neither outcome serves the studio.

**Multi-department sign-off rationale:** A character artist being promoted to senior affects pipeline dependencies with technical art, rigging, and animation. Those leads have information the direct manager does not. The sign-off requirement surfaces cross-functional readiness signals the manager would otherwise miss.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO decided: block individual promotion to senior until band structure with concrete examples is defined for the relevant discipline
- Studio CPO established: promotions require multi-department sign-off, not just direct manager approval
- Studio CPO established: limited promotion slots per cycle to prevent grade inflation
- Studio CPO observed: promoting without a defined standard sets the promoted individual as the de facto benchmark -- a responsibility they did not consent to and may not warrant

## Context

Org chart review at a ~55-70 person MMO studio, July 2026. A direct manager requested a promotion to senior for a well-regarded individual contributor. CPO pushed back pending a broader band structure exercise. This was the first time the studio had surfaced the need for formal promotion criteria.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio manager requests a promotion and no band structure exists for the relevant level -- block until the standard is defined; the promotion sets the standard whether you intend it to or not.
Relevant when: designing a promotion process for a growing studio -- multi-department sign-off surfaces cross-functional readiness signals that the direct manager is structurally positioned to miss.
Relevant when: advising a studio on grade inflation risk -- limited slots per cycle is a simple structural control; without it, the path of least resistance is to promote everyone who is nominally ready in a given window.
Relevant when: a studio is approaching 50+ headcount -- informal promotion decisions made before this point create precedent that is expensive to unwind; a band structure exercise at this scale is overdue, not premature.
