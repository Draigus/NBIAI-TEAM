# Milestone Close Philosophy: Scope Defines the Close, Not the Calendar

## Key Content

A milestone target date is a focal point, not a hard deadline. The milestone closes when the build is ready to the defined scope -- if it doesn't land by the target, it lands target+1 week, +2, +3 weeks until scope is met, then the team rests before the next milestone begins.

**Framing principle:** Even a late-closing build leaves a solid base for the next milestone. The failure mode is not missing a date; it is leaving a broken build that doesn't compound into the next phase.

**Scope vs. date tension:** When a production lead says "it won't land," the correct response is to trace the impossibility to specific scope items. The correct tool is scope surgery, not calendar extension.

**Crunch context:** Past crunch cycles at a studio felt purposeless because pressure was applied without communicating why the deadline mattered. "Pressure without clear purpose is demoralising" -- the crunch itself is not the problem; the absence of context is. Fix: explicit framing of what success means, what failure means, and what is at stake.

**Historical evidence:** Blizzard's Project Titan MMO was shelved until a 3-month prototype ultimatum was issued with full stakes communicated. A 90% scope cut under that constraint produced Overwatch -- a billion-dollar game. Deadline pressure works when the team understands why the date matters.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO concluded: a milestone target date is a scope-close beacon, not a calendar deadline -- add weeks until the build closes to scope, then rest, then start the next milestone
- Studio Head of People observed: even a missed-date delivery leaves a solid base; the failure mode is a broken build at the close, not a late close
- Studio CPO applied: when a production lead declares a milestone won't land, trace the impossibility to specific scope items; scope surgery before calendar extension
- Studio CPO concluded: past crunch that felt purposeless failed because context was not communicated, not because the crunch was wrong

## Context

1:1 between studio CPO and Head of People at a ~65-person MMO studio, July 2026. Reviewing approach to the next vertical slice milestone and diagnosing why a prior crunch cycle had demoralised the team despite the studio ultimately delivering.

## Applicability

Relevant when: a studio is approaching a milestone date and production is behind -- reframe the milestone as a scope-close event; determine the minimum scope for a "closed" build and extend from there, not from the calendar.
Relevant when: advising on a crunch cycle where the team seems demoralised -- diagnose whether the purpose of the deadline has been communicated; if not, the crunch will feel purposeless regardless of effort.
Relevant when: a production lead declares a milestone "won't land" -- probe what specific scope items make it impossible; the answer is a scope cut, not a slipped date.
Relevant when: a studio needs a forcing function for a scope decision -- a milestone with full stakes communicated (including consequences of missing it) is the cleanest forcing function for cutting scope.
