# Contractor Vacation Policy Restructure and Red-Team Rollout Approach

## Key Content

A methodology for restructuring contractor compensation to reduce misclassification risk while maintaining effective pay, plus a change management model for rolling out the new policy across a large contractor base.

**Policy structure:**
- Pay contractors on a 20-day/month average (lower than actual days worked under current model)
- Annual salary unchanged; monthly invoice varies based on actual days worked
- Tracks actual vacation taken; reduces misclassification risk relative to FTE classification
- Requires tool integration (e.g. HiBob) for day-logging; requires contract amendments for ~40 contractors

**Red-team rollout model:**
1. Identify a small group of trusted contractors across departments (one per department)
2. Walk the policy through with them before studio-wide communication
3. Goals: practise the communication, surface confusion points, build internal ambassadors for the policy
4. Ambassadors then carry the message into their departments alongside formal communication

**Communication ownership:** senior advisor or HR owns the communication package and presentation; department heads are not the right vehicle for policy rollout of this complexity

**Parallel workstream:** HR creates a project plan (with steps, owners, and deadlines) to track the rollout; Finance involved to confirm statutory obligations before any new agreements are signed.

## Decisions / Insights

- Glen decided: red-team the contractor vacation policy through one trusted contractor per department before studio-wide rollout; the goal is communication practice and ambassador-building, not policy testing.
- Glen observed: communicating a pay structure change to ~40 contractors simultaneously without a red-team run risks mass confusion that a single policy document cannot fix in real time.
- Glen concluded: Finance must confirm statutory sick leave obligations before any contract amendments are executed -- sequencing matters.

## Context

1:1 between senior advisor and Head of Production at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2 2026. Studio has ~40 contractors across multiple countries; IR35 misclassification risk is a live concern, particularly for UK-based contractors.

## Applicability

- Relevant when: a studio needs to restructure contractor compensation to address misclassification risk -- the 20-day average billing model is a specific structure that maintains effective pay while reducing the FTE resemblance.
- Relevant when: rolling out a complex HR policy change to a large contractor base -- the red-team approach (small trusted group per department before studio-wide) is a reusable change management model.
- Relevant when: a studio has a large contractor workforce across multiple jurisdictions -- centralising the communication package with HR rather than distributing to department heads prevents inconsistent messaging.
- Relevant when: advising on contractor policy sequencing -- Finance sign-off on statutory obligations before contract amendments is a non-negotiable sequencing step.
