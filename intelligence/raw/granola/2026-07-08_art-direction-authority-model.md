# Art Direction Authority: Establishing Creative Chain of Command

## Key Content

Pattern observed at a ~55-person studio where an Art Director had lost effective creative authority because leads were bypassing him to influence the CEO/Creative Director directly.

**The failure mode:**
- Leads go directly to CEO/Creative Director to get art style decisions overridden
- AD defers to the CEO even on decisions explicitly within his authority
- CEO validates the bypass by agreeing to art direction changes without going through the AD
- Result: AD has a title but not the authority; team reads this as "passive, not directing"
- Key observation: "Whether this reflects reality or perception is irrelevant -- if the team believes it, the effect is the same"

**The correction methodology:**
1. AD calls all art leads together and explicitly states: art direction comes from the AD; going around the chain has consequences
2. AD separates creative spaces (leads own their craft) from direction authority (AD sets the visual bar and approves deviations)
3. Three specific scripts prepared by external CPO:
   - When a lead bypasses AD to CEO: "That's nice input. It's not the decision."
   - When CEO overrides art direction without going through AD: "Are you the creative director or the CEO right now?"
   - When AD is tempted to defer: simply "no" -- not argumentative, just firm
4. CPO offers to attend the boundary-setting meeting as an advocate for AD's authority; backs AD's "no" with CEO when needed
5. CPO makes the offer explicit: "Come get me and I will drop things and go help you" -- not inserting uninvited

**Three-stage escalation model applied to any director:**
1. Clear verbal direction: set expectations, invite pushback, but don't nod and then do the opposite
2. Follow-up if not actioned: "I'm a little disappointed, were we not on the same page?" -- firm, creates accountability
3. PIP if pattern repeats a third time

**Parallel case used as reference:** another director at the same studio was taken offsite, given a direct ultimatum, and now performs; the same approach applies.

## Decisions / Insights

- CPO concluded: a passive Art Director who defers upward on art direction decisions loses effective authority regardless of title.
- CPO observed: team belief in an AD's weakness has the same practical effect as actual weakness -- perception is the reality the team operates within.
- CPO decided: external CPO backs AD's authority directly with CEO when needed; this structural support is what makes the AD's "no" stick.
- CPO observed: the dividing line is "you should be more forceful" (a suggestion) vs "you have to be" (a job requirement).

## Context

1:1 between CPO and Art Director at a ~55-person MMO studio, 8 Jul 2026. Multiple senior art team members had fed back to the CPO that the AD was not making decisions. Concrete incident: a senior artist bypassed the AD to change art style direction with the CEO; CEO agreed, AD pivoted. CPO sent a written follow-up to the AD after the call with specific actions, a framework for the art team meeting, and the escalation model.
