# Animation Team Throughput Benchmarking: Lead Leverage and Resourcing Model

## Key Content

Throughput data from a ~55-70 person MMO studio animation team:

**Observed output:** 14 animations completed since January (~5 months = ~2.8/month = ~0.65/week). One animator driving essentially all output at ~1/week. Team lead spending the majority of time on "lead work" despite managing only one animator and one rigger.

**Target output:** ~6 animations/week -- approximately 9x current run rate.

**Resourcing model to reach target:** 3 animators total producing 2 animations/week each. Current single animator stays; 2 net new hires required.

**Lead leverage diagnosis:** A team lead managing 2 direct reports who spends most time on lead activities produces poor leverage. At that ratio, lead hours are wasted on coordination overhead rather than output. Rule of thumb: a lead-to-IC ratio below 1:4 in a craft discipline is a throughput hazard.

**Replacement trigger:** When significant training investment over multiple months fails to move craft output materially, replacement is the correct response rather than continued coaching. "Good people" is not sufficient for role retention when throughput dependency is high.

## Decisions / Insights

- Studio CPO assessed: 14 animations in 5 months from a 3-person team = ~0.65/week actual output vs 6/week target; the gap is a resourcing and quality problem, not a process problem
- Studio CPO observed: lead spending most time on lead work for only 2 direct reports is a throughput hazard -- lead ratio too low to justify overhead
- Studio CPO decided: target 3 animators at 2/week each; 2 net new hires required
- Studio CPO applied: training investment that has not moved the needle after multiple months is a replacement signal, not a reason to continue

## Context

Production review at a ~55-70 person MMO studio, July 2026. Animation team assessed as a major production bottleneck ahead of a vertical slice milestone. Lead animating less than they could because of coordination overhead. One core animator effectively carrying the team.

## Applicability

Relevant when: assessing animation throughput against a milestone -- divide total animations needed by weeks available to get required weekly rate, then model headcount against 2 animations/animator/week as a benchmark.
Relevant when: a team lead has fewer than 4 direct reports -- the coordination overhead-to-output ratio may be destroying throughput rather than multiplying it.
Relevant when: training spend on a critical craft role hasn't improved output after 3+ months -- this is a replacement signal, not a coaching opportunity.
Relevant when: advising a studio on resourcing a content-intensive discipline (animation, VO, VFX) -- use actual output tracking to diagnose throughput, not team size alone.
