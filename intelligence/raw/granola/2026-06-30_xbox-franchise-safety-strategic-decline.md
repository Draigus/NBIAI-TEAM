# Xbox/Microsoft Strategic Decline: Franchise Safety Over New IP

## Key Content

First-person account from a 27-year industry veteran who departed Xbox in mid-2026 due to values misalignment with the strategic direction under current leadership.

**Strategic shift observed:**
- Microsoft moved Xbox from building new IP toward "safe" franchises (Gears, Halo, Forza)
- Plans handed down without consultation with business leaders who had operational knowledge
- Game Pass strategy not accounting for member-weighted engagement value -- subscriber counts prioritised over depth of engagement

**Root cause as described:**
- Decision-making dominated by financial analysis without game-making judgment
- In one XGS quarterly business review: only 4 of 20+ attendees had ever shipped a game
- Spreadsheet-driven allocation replacing creative judgment about team potential and IP viability
- Result: portfolio narrows to defensible franchises, killing creative risk-taking

**Shared observation from multiple Xbox-era executives:**
- Too few game-makers in decision-making roles is the systemic problem, not individual leadership failures
- Phil Spencer and Matt Booty retain high regard; the structural issue is the layer below
- The same pattern is visible at other large publishers: financial analysts outnumbering game-makers in strategy meetings

**Game Pass weakness noted:**
- Game Pass strategy not modelling member-weighted value (i.e. hours of engagement per title per subscriber)
- Leads to catalogue additions optimised for press coverage rather than engagement depth

## Decisions / Insights

- Industry veteran concluded: Microsoft's shift to franchise-safety over new IP is structural, not a single leadership decision -- it reflects the composition of decision-making rooms, not individual judgment calls.
- Industry veteran observed: Game Pass economics are not being modelled correctly at the strategic level; member-weighted engagement is the right metric, not raw subscriber counts or content additions.
- Industry veterans (multiple, independently) observed: the ratio of game-makers to non-game-makers in publisher QBRs is a leading indicator of strategic creativity -- studios lose creative courage as that ratio falls.
