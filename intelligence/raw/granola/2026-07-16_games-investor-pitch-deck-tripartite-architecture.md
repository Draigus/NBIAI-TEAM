# Games Investor Pitch Deck Tripartite Architecture

## Key Content

A games studio preparing for an investor outreach campaign needs three distinct deck variants, not one. Each serves a different audience and cannot be adapted from the others without compromising effectiveness:

**15-slide deck (games-industry investors):** asset-heavy, visual-first, limited text. Games investors respond to craft signal -- they want to see what the game looks and feels like before reading a business case. Lead with visual identity and gameplay.

**30-slide deck (non-games investors):** data and graph-heavy, business metrics lead. Non-games investors need to understand the market opportunity and business model in terms they recognise. Visuals are secondary; the investment thesis must be explicit.

**50-60 slide due diligence deck:** full financials, headcount breakdown, cash flow projections, taxation structure, corporate structure. Not a pitch tool -- a trust and transparency document provided after initial interest is established.

**Pre-deck prerequisites (must be resolved before deck work begins):**
1. Financials pinned and budget updated
2. Raise amount and valuation agreed internally
3. Spend plan defined (what the money does)
4. Investor target list built with outreach timing
5. Brand identity stabilised -- assets in market must match internal brand; a brand review and consolidation pass before deck work avoids rebuilding slides after brand changes

**Deadline model:** a major games industry event (e.g. Gamescom, GDC) as a fixed pitch-ready deadline is effective for forcing completion; it creates a credible external anchor for investor meeting scheduling.

## Applicability

Relevant when: advising a studio preparing investor outreach -- build three deck variants from the start (15/30/50-slide); a single deck adapted for different audiences always compromises one of them.
Relevant when: a studio's brand identity is in flux -- resolve brand alignment before deck production begins; mid-deck brand changes require a rebuild pass and compress the timeline.
Relevant when: a studio cannot define its raise amount or spend plan -- these are prerequisites, not deck content; the deck cannot be built credibly until the internal investment thesis is agreed.
Relevant when: a studio needs a pitch deadline to force completion -- anchor to a major industry event (Gamescom, GDC, Develop:Brighton) that creates a credible external forcing function for investor meeting scheduling.
Relevant when: pitching to non-games investors for the first time -- the 30-slide data-heavy format must explicitly state the investment thesis in business terms; games-fluent visual shorthand does not translate.
