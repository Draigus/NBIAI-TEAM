# Rob Sandberg (Production Alchemist) -- The External Producer Role: Accountability Without Authority

## Key Content (max 200 words)

An external producer works for a publisher overseeing games built by independent development studios -- the structural bridge between publisher interests and developer capabilities. Key responsibilities span four domains: commercial and strategic (market fit assessment, studio capability evaluation, IP licensing), project management (multi-project production plans, milestone submission oversight, budget forecasting), contract and legal (milestone payment schedule negotiation, scope change documentation, SOW review), and operational coordination (platform certification shepherding, QA bug prioritisation, marketing asset delivery, first-party platform liaison).

The central structural challenge: accountability for outcomes you cannot directly control. Authority flows through contracts, relationships, and judgment quality -- not command. This creates a persistent tension between partnership rapport and contractual enforcement readiness.

The critical skill distinction Sandberg identifies: "The external producers who struggle most default to process. The role requires you to read the room." Process enables work but cannot replace professional judgment about project health.

The role requires prior studio-side experience specifically because without it, producers cannot distinguish between normal project difficulty and genuine trouble -- the core capability required for milestone evaluation and risk identification.

Modern evolution: fewer self-published studios mean fewer traditional external producer positions; remote work requires more deliberate relationship maintenance; live service expertise increasingly valued.

## Decisions / Insights

- The structural tension (accountable without authority) is the defining feature of the role, not a problem to solve -- success comes from building enforcement readiness alongside partnership rapport.
- "Read the room" is the distinguishing capability: producers who default to process when project health is ambiguous are the ones who miss early warning signs.
- Prior studio-side experience is not optional -- it provides the mental models needed to separate normal development pain from structural trouble.
- Remote external production requires more deliberate relationship maintenance, not the same amount as co-located work.
- The role increasingly requires live service operational expertise alongside traditional milestone management.
