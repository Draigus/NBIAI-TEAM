# Larian Studios / Divinity: Original Sin 1 -- Publisher Independence and Early Access as Mass QA

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Larian Studios (Ghent, Belgium) made Divinity: Original Sin with approximately 35-40 people on a total budget of €4.5M -- €3.5M in studio funding plus ~$950K from a 2013 Kickstarter campaign. The project was near-fatal: Larian delayed tax payments and pulled resources from Dragon Commander to fund it. Had DOS1 not succeeded, the company would have failed.

The core production decision was breaking publisher dependency. Every RPG proposal Larian sent to publishers for 15 years was rejected or had multiplayer removed ("every time we proposed, it was refused"). Self-publishing via Kickstarter created what Swen Vincke described as "real room for failure" in creative development -- the absence of a publisher milestone chain that would have required sign-off before iterating on core systems.

Early Access started as pay-to-access. This proved counterproductive. Larian reversed to free access: all 20,000 players willing to test received free access, generating approximately 70,000 total testers who provided continuous QA feedback. Vincke's characterisation of traditional publisher QA: "you send a milestone and you're lucky if someone reads what you're saying." The EA community replaced this with immediate, volume-verified feedback. Digital distribution only: "I would never again do all the boxed stuff" -- physical distribution (localisation, disc printing, shipping) consumed disproportionate resources.

## Decisions / Insights

- Publisher milestone chains are passive oversight -- "send a milestone and you're lucky if someone reads it" -- whereas Early Access produces active, daily player feedback that actually shapes the game
- Pay-to-access Early Access is counterproductive: it filters out casual testers who provide volume feedback in exchange for motivated buyers who are less likely to log bugs
- Free access to all willing testers converts community interest into QA capacity at no incremental cost
- Kickstarter under $1M can buy creative independence from publisher constraints even when total budget is €4-5M -- the value is contractual freedom, not the capital amount
- Digital-only is a structural decision, not just a channel preference: avoiding physical distribution eliminates localisation management, disc manufacturing coordination, and retail logistics
- "Room for failure" in creative iteration requires removing the milestone-approval step -- you cannot genuinely iterate when every major direction change requires external sign-off

## Applicability

Directly usable when advising studios weighing publisher deal vs self-publishing. The calculation is not "can we raise enough from crowdfunding to fund the game" but "does the milestone contract remove our ability to iterate on the game's core systems?" The Early Access QA model -- free access, high volume, community feedback as production input -- is a scalable alternative to contracted QA for studios that cannot afford dedicated QA headcount. The digital-only recommendation applies most clearly to first-time publishers who underestimate physical distribution overhead.
