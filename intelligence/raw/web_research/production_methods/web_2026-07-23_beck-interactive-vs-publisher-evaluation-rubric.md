# Beck Interactive Partners -- Publisher-Grade Vertical Slice Evaluation Rubric (2026)

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Alex Beck, Managing Partner at Beck Interactive Partners (a developer representation firm that ran 500+ publisher pitch processes in 2025), published a structured evaluation framework for vertical slices in February 2026. The piece documents the quality bar now required by publishers in the current market.

**Primary benchmark:** The VS must look, feel, and play like the actual final game -- fully dressed art, polished UI, complete gameplay loops with no or limited placeholders. Self-test: "Would you feel comfortable selling this as 1/10th of your complete game on Steam at a pro-rated price?"

Six evaluation dimensions with pass/fail criteria:

| Category | Requirements | Failure indicators |
|---|---|---|
| UI | Final-form menus, transitions, responsive feedback | Any placeholder elements; must be navigable by an unfamiliar player |
| Art | Environmental assets, characters, lighting, VFX at near-final quality | Unclear tone or theme; not Steam front page viable |
| Gameplay loops | Primary loop functioning end-to-end; secondary loops demonstrated | Incomplete progression chain (e.g. kills → XP → stat spend → character change) |
| Polish | Smooth state transitions; externally playtested; stable | Soft locks; not tested 10+ times internally and with external players |
| Tutorial | In-game instruction that teaches unique mechanics | Player frustration without external assistance |
| Technical | Minimal crashes; stable frame rates; optimised | Tech de-prioritised unless it is the primary USP |

Recommended professional practice: document known issues on a loading screen or accompanying document rather than submitting undisclosed shortcomings.

## Decisions / Insights

- The "1/10th game on Steam" heuristic is the most actionable self-test for VS readiness: if the team would not sell this segment at a pro-rated price, the VS is not ready.
- Beck explicitly states what worked in 2021 no longer works in 2026: the quality bar has risen substantially. Studios using pre-2023 playbooks are under-delivering to a market where publishers are far more selective.
- The six-dimension evaluation table maps to the sections publishers score in parallel during a pitch -- each dimension can fail independently, which means a game with outstanding art and broken UI fails the evaluation.
- Known-issue disclosure (loading screen or doc) is standard professional practice and prevents the perception that shortcomings were hidden, which damages trust more than the issues themselves.
- Meeting baseline criteria achieves consideration only; funded games possess an additional intangible quality beyond checklist compliance (the subject of Part 2 of the series, not accessed).
