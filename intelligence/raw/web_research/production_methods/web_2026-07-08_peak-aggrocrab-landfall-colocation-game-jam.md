# Peak -- Co-location Game Jam as Production Framework: Two Studios, One Month, 2 Million Sales

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Aggro Crab (~10 people) and Landfall (~25-30 people) made co-op climbing game Peak via a month-long retreat in Seoul, South Korea (February 2025). Seven developers total: 3 from Aggro Crab, 4 from Landfall. Budget under $200k (primarily salaries plus accommodation and travel). Total production time: approximately 4 weeks of intensive co-location, followed by a shorter polish phase covering UX and meta-progression. Announced and shipped within 5 days of public announcement. Commercial outcome: 100,000 units in 24 hours, 2 million sales total, break-even within first hours of launch.

Scope management: the fixed delivery date was the scope mechanism. "The game jam IS the game" -- whatever existed at the end of the Seoul month would ship. Post-jam work was pre-scoped to UX polish and meta-progression only, removing the most common scope creep vector. Pre-production Discord discussions created design conflict and misalignment; co-location resolved the same disagreements faster. The specific lesson: "text is evil" -- creative direction disputes resolved more cleanly in person or via synchronous calls than through async text. Landfall had previously used the same co-location methodology for Content Warning (2024), suggesting this is a repeatable production model rather than a one-off.

## Decisions / Insights

- Fixed delivery date is the strongest scope management tool available: when the game jam end date equals the ship date, scope naturally contracts to what exists rather than what was planned
- "Text is evil" for creative coordination: async text misrepresents emotional tone and generates friction that synchronous voice or in-person conversation resolves more quickly and with less residual conflict
- Cross-studio co-location eliminates collaboration overhead: scheduling delays, status updates, and alignment documentation reduce to near-zero when both teams share physical space
- Pre-scoping the post-jam phase (UX, meta-progression only) prevents the most common extension: "just one more feature" after the core is done
- Landfall's prior Content Warning (2024, same methodology) provides evidence this is repeatable -- not a lucky one-off
