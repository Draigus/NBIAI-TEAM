# Ghost Ship Games: Single-Project Focus Model for Studios Running Live Service + New Development

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Ghost Ship Games (~26 people in 2021, ~40 by 2022, 54 in 2026) publicly described how they handle the simultaneous pressure of maintaining a live service title (Deep Rock Galactic) and developing a new game (Rogue Core). Their stated approach: "instead of trying to do a little bit of work on each game every day, we'd rather block out bigger chunks of time where we give one project our full attention -- it's what will allow us to give you the best games we can possibly make."

In practice, this means pausing internal DRG season development when Rogue Core demands concentrated effort. To maintain content flow for the live service during these focus blocks, Ghost Ship partnered with external studio Invisible Walls for Deep Rock Galactic Season 06 -- the first season not developed in-house. Invisible Walls was selected for alignment with DRG's cooperative design values, and Ghost Ship had prior confidence from a similar arrangement with Funday Games on DRG: Survivor.

Separately, Deep Rock Galactic completed 5+ seasons of themed seasonal content (enemy types, equipment, missions, cosmetics) on a roughly 4-6 month cadence since November 2021, all developed in-house with no crunch.

## Decisions / Insights

- "Split attention between two games daily" loses to "block time fully on one game" at the 40-50 person scale -- stated explicitly as a studio conclusion, not an assumption
- Outsourcing content cadence to a trusted external partner is a viable mechanism to maintain live service continuity during a new-title focus block
- External partner selection criteria: shared co-op design values, prior proof-of-concept relationship (lower risk than a cold engagement)
- Studio grew from 6 contractors to 54 people across 10 years without abandoning the no-crunch model, suggesting the constraint applies at every growth stage, not only in the early days
- The seasonal model formalises a live-ops cadence that is sustainable without crunch: 4-6 months per season with a defined content scope (one enemy class, equipment set, mission type, cosmetic tier)

## Context

Primary source: gamesmarket.global interview with Ghost Ship (2025) on DRG future and Invisible Walls partnership. Team size history: 6 founders May 2016 (all ex-Press Play/Microsoft), 12 people by February 2018 (all contractors due to funding uncertainty), ~28 by December 2021 (game director Mikkel Martin Pedersen interview, Game Rant), ~40 by 2022-2023, 54 as of 2026. Deep Rock Galactic: 8M+ copies sold, 2.3M in 2022 alone. Rogue Core entered Early Access May 2026 with stated 18-month build window. ## Applicability

Directly usable when advising any studio that has shipped a live service title and wants to build a new one without abandoning the existing player base. The Ghost Ship model -- block-focus on one project, outsource content maintenance to a vetted partner -- is a concrete alternative to the common failure mode of splitting a small team too thin. The 40-54 person range is squarely within NBI's client target. The external partner selection criteria (values alignment, prior relationship) provide a due diligence framework for similar arrangements.
