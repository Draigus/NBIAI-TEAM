# Coffee Stain Studios -- Satisfactory Early Access Update Cadence and Transparency Model

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Coffee Stain Studios (Skövde, Sweden) developed Satisfactory through over five years of Early Access (March 2019 to September 2024). Studio size: 24 employees in 2018 (pre-launch), growing to 25 employees by August 2019 (Wikipedia, verified). The game shipped eight major updates during Early Access, with Update 8 releasing November 2023, before full 1.0 launch on 10 September 2024.

The production transparency model is publicly documented: a public Trello roadmap; weekly "Friday News" live Q&A streams; a staged Experimental branch that holds each major update for community testing before promotion to Stable. The Experimental branch functions as a structured community feedback loop -- the studio observes player impact on live factory saves and systems before committing the update to the default branch, surfacing edge cases internal QA cannot replicate on a complex open-ended simulation.

Post-1.0, the studio has continued major updates to the live game. The Experimental-to-Stable pipeline separates "feature complete" from "production ready" without requiring a closed beta infrastructure, making it a low-overhead staged release model accessible to studios of similar size.

## Decisions / Insights

- The Experimental-to-Stable pipeline is a two-phase release model that surfaces live-player edge cases that internal QA misses on simulation or systems-driven games; it requires no additional beta infrastructure, only an opt-in branch.
- Weekly community Q&A streams serve a production function as well as a marketing one: they surface player friction early enough to address within the current update cycle rather than creating a new bug backlog.
- A public Trello roadmap commits the studio to transparency about what is in scope, which creates external accountability for scope decisions -- a meaningful structural constraint on scope creep.
- Studios running complex simulation or systems-driven games should model a staged release gate: define explicit promotion criteria (bug threshold, performance baseline) before moving from Experimental to Stable, making the community testing window purposeful rather than ad hoc.
- With 24-25 developers throughout Early Access, Coffee Stain demonstrates that an Experimental branch model is operationally feasible at indie-to-small-studio scale and does not require dedicated QA infrastructure to run.

## Applicability

Most applicable when NBI advises clients entering or managing Early Access, or running live service games with periodic major updates. The Experimental-to-Stable model is directly applicable to any studio shipping to a technically engaged player base willing to opt into testing. The public transparency model (roadmap and regular streams) functions as a scope discipline tool as much as community relations -- the studio that commits publicly to a roadmap creates external pressure on internal scope decisions.
