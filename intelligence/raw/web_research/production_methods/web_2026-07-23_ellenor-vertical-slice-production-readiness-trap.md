# Geoff Ellenor -- Vertical Slice as Production Trap: What Pre-Production Must Also Deliver

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Geoff Ellenor (Creative Director, People Can Fly Montréal -- Outriders, Bulletstorm) published a practitioner warning about what a polished vertical slice can mask. The core risk: teams exit pre-production with a visually impressive VS built with none of the tools, processes, or data structures needed for production, and fall behind schedule from the first day of production.

Three specific failure modes:

**Tool degradation.** Pre-production tools are often "hacks" with minimal investment -- crash buttons positioned near save functions, undocumented workarounds. Bad tools burn money and waste iteration time in production. The VS does not reveal this gap.

**System shortcuts.** Small sample sizes in a VS allow workarounds that do not scale. Teams fake systemic content because the VS requires only one instance, not a hundred.

**Process gaps.** Teams without established feature review meetings, mission approvals, or asset validation processes are not production-ready. The VS does not test approval cadence.

**Primary metric for production readiness:** "The velocity of a team in production is determined by how much _approved_ work they do." Approval speed -- not raw output -- is the KPI that predicts production success.

**What pre-production must also deliver beyond the VS:** robust data structures and content pipelines; functioning approval workflows practised before headcount ramp; designers building systems, not handcrafted samples.

## Decisions / Insights

- A studio can pass the VS milestone and still not be production-ready. The VS tests gameplay and visual quality; it does not test tools, pipelines, approval workflows, or team decision-making cadence.
- Approval velocity is the primary production KPI: if the team cannot move work through approval quickly, raw output speed is irrelevant. Approval meetings should be established and practised during pre-production, not invented on the fly when production starts.
- Pre-production must deliver three things beyond the VS: (1) robust data structures and content pipelines, (2) established approval workflows practised before headcount ramp, (3) designers building systems rather than handcrafted one-off samples that will not scale.
- For NBI clients entering production after a VS: the first diagnostic question should be "How does approved work move through the pipeline?" not "How fast is the team shipping?"
- "Bad tools say to the team: your frustrations are not important to us." Investing in tooling before headcount ramp is a morale and velocity multiplier -- the VS reveals none of this.

## Context

Source: gellenor.medium.com, published November 2014. Author Geoff Ellenor is a Creative Director at People Can Fly Montréal with direct production experience on mid-to-large studio titles. Published 2014 -- predates the current era, but the structural failure modes described are unchanged and this article circulates widely among producers as a counterpoint to VS-centric pre-production frameworks. Content is practitioner-specific, not theoretical.

## Applicability

Applicable to any NBI client approaching or just past their VS milestone. The VS passing is a necessary but not sufficient condition for production readiness. NBI should use this as a second-tier checklist after the VS passes -- specifically to evaluate tool maturity, content pipeline documentation, and approval workflow establishment. Most useful in a post-VS production readiness conversation with studio leadership before headcount ramp: "You have a great VS -- now let us check whether the pipeline behind it can support the scale you are about to add."
