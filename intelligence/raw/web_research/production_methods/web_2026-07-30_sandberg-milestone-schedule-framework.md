# Rob Sandberg (Production Alchemist) -- How to Build a Game Milestone Schedule

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Sandberg's framework treats milestone schedules as communication tools for three audiences simultaneously: teams (what to build and when), leadership (credible delivery tracking), and publishers (money movements). Core methodology: work backwards from ship date to expose conflicts before options close. Identify the critical path -- the subset of work that controls ship timing -- to prevent wasted effort on non-critical activities.

Two estimation failure modes to avoid: inflation-as-protection (padding after past missed deadlines, creating false visibility) and compression-as-optimism (squeezing to fit available time, eroding team confidence). Estimates forecast time needed; commitments come later in the process.

Contingency placement: one protected buffer block late in the project outperforms distributed padding, where Parkinson's Law and Student's Syndrome consume it invisibly.

Probabilistic forecasting: replace fixed dates with confidence ranges. Internal iteration planning: 50% confidence. Publisher and certification commitments: 85%+, using Monte Carlo simulation on historical throughput.

Audience-specific views from a single source of truth: technical teams get granular dependency detail; executives get headline ranges; publishers get certification windows, marketing beats, and a one-page risk summary.

Weekly early warning signals: velocity drop, tasks reopening, late estimates lengthening, QA defect spikes.

## Decisions / Insights

- Work backwards from ship date as the primary scheduling discipline -- forward estimation reveals problems too late to address.
- Milestones serve three distinct audiences; the same document sent to all three is a failure mode, not efficiency.
- A 50%/85% confidence split between internal and publisher-facing schedules is a concrete, immediately adoptable standard.
- Visible, concentrated contingency buffer is harder to erode than distributed padding; make it explicit and defend it actively.
- Weekly velocity tracking is diagnostic, not bureaucratic: four specific signals (reopened tasks, lengthening late estimates, QA spikes, velocity drops) are the producer's early warning system.
