# Rob Sandberg (Production Alchemist) -- Live Service Roadmaps: The Rolling Window Model

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Live service development operates differently from boxed games because planning and operating happen simultaneously -- there is no clean endpoint. Sandberg's core model is a three-tier rolling window:

1. Near-term (6-8 weeks): specific features, firm estimates, clear accountability
2. Mid-term (following quarter): thematic direction with reduced granularity
3. Far-term (beyond 6 months): directional intent only; genuinely undecided specifics

The critical discipline is "hold the near end tightly and the far end loosely."

Four essential distinctions: (1) Confidence levels must be explicit on roadmaps -- items vary from "already in QA" to "aspirational wish," and treating them identically creates downstream problems when marketing campaigns get built on unfinished work. (2) Slipping vs reprioritising are fundamentally different -- slipping means the estimate was wrong; reprioritising means business priorities changed deliberately. Conflating them damages team trust. (3) Fixed calendar dates (seasonal events, Halloween content) cannot slip -- these create hard obligations unlike other roadmap items. (4) Player-facing roadmap commitments require conservatism -- only publish what you would be comfortable having quoted back in the worst possible context six months later.

Roadmap updates are expected; the challenge is distinguishing normal revision from unexpected problems.

## Decisions / Insights

- The rolling window model gives studios a principled answer to "how far out should we plan?" that accounts for live service's inherent uncertainty.
- Slipping vs reprioritising is a distinction most studios collapse -- making it visible restores team trust and creates accurate risk signals.
- Confidence labelling (colour-coded or text-tagged) on roadmap items prevents downstream teams from treating aspirations as confirmed commitments.
- Player-facing roadmaps should pass the "leaked to journalists tomorrow" test before any commitment is published.
- The producer's credibility compounds: accurate early-stage assessments lead to being consulted earlier in future decision cycles rather than informed after decisions are made.

Rob Sandberg, Production Alchemist Substack, Production 101 #11. Written from live service production experience across multiple game types. Sandberg is a working producer, not an academic, and the framework reflects operational lessons rather than theory.
