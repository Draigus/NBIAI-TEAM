# Toño Game Consultants -- Prototype to VS to Production: Five-Stage Sequence

## Key Content (max 200 words)

Toño Jimenez (10 years professional experience: Halo Infinite, Minecraft, ARK Survival Evolved, Pokémon Go) published a paired framework across two articles (September and October 2024-2025) defining the five-stage pre-production-to-production sequence with specific gate criteria at each transition.

**Five-stage sequence:** Prototype Loop → Validation → Story Mapping → Backlog Creation → Vertical Slice or Production

**Gate: Prototype to Validation.** A prototype advances when it demonstrates "repeatable fun across multiple playtests, not just once." Prototypes are intentionally fast, dirty, disposable, and non-scalable -- built only for learning. Critical failure: converting a prototype directly to production foundations creates tech debt and brittle systems. Production must begin with clean architecture.

**Gate: Validation to Story Mapping.** A 1-2 day facilitated session for small/mid-sized indie teams. Outputs: structured user journey, organised backlog, mapped dependencies, aligned priorities, early scope boundaries. Rushing into production before this alignment causes reactive, confused development with extensive rework.

**Vertical slice scope:** 3-5 minutes of polished content; 1-3 months of development; principle: "cut scope, not quality."

**Seven VS steps:** (1) identify core mechanics, (2) select manageable scene representing full game in miniature, (3) polish to near-final, (4) establish minimal narrative context, (5) external playtest with uninvolved participants, (6) document honest feedback, (7) refine iteratively until the experience reliably demonstrates the intended vision.

**Micro-prototyping in production:** Even after VS approval, teams run short micro-prototypes to validate risky features before committing art/animation resources.

## Decisions / Insights

- Prototype and VS serve distinct functions: prototype tests viability, VS proves that viability works at near-final quality. Confusing them loses time and money -- teams that skip the distinction routinely over-invest in prototype polish or under-invest in VS scope.
- "Depending on unsustainable hero-mode effort" during VS misrepresents actual production velocity -- if the VS was built with crunch, it does not prove the team can sustain production at that quality level.
- Story Mapping as a 1-2 day facilitated exercise is the critical alignment gate between prototype success and production commitment -- it is the most commonly skipped step and the most common cause of reactive development.
- The VS audience is the team, leads, investors, and publishers -- not players (a demo serves players). Studios that confuse these over-polish community-facing features and under-polish internal production signals.
- Common VS failure: "They prove visuals, not the core loop." Aesthetic quality without gameplay loop validation passes the wrong test and fools internal stakeholders.

## Applicability

Directly applicable to NBI advisory work with studios in pre-production. The prototype/VS confusion failure pattern applies to any client who has completed prototyping but is uncertain about production readiness. The Story Mapping gate (1-2 days) is a concrete facilitated deliverable NBI can offer.
