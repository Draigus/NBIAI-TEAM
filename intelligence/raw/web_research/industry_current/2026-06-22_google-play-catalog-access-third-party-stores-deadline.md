# Google Play Catalog Access: All Android Games Auto-Enrolled in Third-Party Stores from July 22 Unless Developers Opt Out

**Date:** 2026-06-22
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Platform Policy
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 7 | **Actionability:** 10

## Summary

Google notified all developers on June 22, 2026, that app and game listings (name, icon, description, screenshots, videos) will be made available to enrolled third-party US Android app stores starting July 22, 2026, pursuant to a US court order from the Epic v. Google settlement. All developers are auto-enrolled by default. The opt-out window closes July 22, 2026. Downloads still route through Google Play on Google's standard service fee terms; the distribution change affects listing visibility, not billing. Developers have three settings: allow all third-party stores, manage individually, or block all.

## Key Facts

- Notice issued by Google: June 22, 2026
- Effective date: July 22, 2026 (US market only initially)
- Legal basis: US court order from Epic v. Google settlement (March 2026)
- Default: auto-enrolled -- all developers are IN unless they actively opt out before July 22
- What is shared: app/game name, icon, description, screenshots, promo videos
- What is NOT changed: downloads still route through Google Play; Google's service fee (now 20% per the settlement) applies to transactions even from third-party store traffic
- Opt-out mechanism: Google Play Console settings -- three options: allow all, manage individually, block all
- Programme name: Google Play Catalog Access programme
- This is a separate mechanism from the commission change (also from the Epic settlement) that took effect June 30, 2026

## Strategic Significance

1. The auto-enrolment default means any developer who has not actively reviewed their Google Play Console settings before July 22 is already opted in. The burden of action is on developers to exclude themselves, not to join. This is a meaningful deadline for studios that have not been tracking the Epic settlement rollout closely.

2. Third-party store exposure does not reduce Google's fee (20% post-settlement) -- it potentially increases total distribution without changing unit economics. For studios with strong brand recognition, this may be additive reach. For studios concerned about listing presentation or fragmented support queries, it is an unwanted complication.

3. This is the first operational implementation of court-ordered Android distribution opening in the US. It sets a template for what "open distribution" looks like in practice: Google maintains billing and fee collection while accepting court-mandated listing sharing. Apple is watching; regulatory pressure for equivalent moves on iOS will likely follow.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Time-critical: every NBI client with an Android game published on Google Play needs to review their Console settings before July 22, 2026. This is not optional -- inaction equals auto-enrolment.
- For clients with games in sensitive categories (children's titles, content rated for older audiences) or with strict brand standards: opting out of all third-party stores is likely the right default until the landscape of enrolled stores is understood.
- NBI should include a Google Play Catalog Access audit as a standard checklist item in any mobile studio engagement for the remainder of 2026.
