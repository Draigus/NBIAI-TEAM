# EA $55B PIF Buyout: EU Antitrust Verdict July 22 -- Largest Gaming M&A Ever Reaches Regulatory Finish Line

**Date:** 2026-07-02
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** M&A / Regulatory
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 6 | **Actionability:** 7

## Summary

The $55 billion take-private of Electronic Arts by a Saudi PIF-led consortium (with Silver Lake, Affinity Partners, and Jared Kushner's SPAC vehicle) has cleared US HSR antitrust review and is now days from its EU decision. The European Commission is due to issue its initial antitrust ruling on 22 July 2026, with a separate Foreign Subsidies Regulation decision on 30 July. CFIUS review remains the final outstanding condition. The outside date for closing has been extended to September 28, 2026. EA is simultaneously running its third round of workforce reductions this calendar year, targeting trust and safety, customer support, and IT teams ahead of the deal close.

## Key Facts

- Deal: $55B all-cash buyout of EA by Saudi PIF (lead), Silver Lake, Affinity Partners, others -- largest leveraged buyout in history
- EA shareholders approved the deal December 22, 2025
- US HSR antitrust: cleared
- EU antitrust (DG Comp) decision: July 22, 2026
- EU Foreign Subsidies Regulation decision: July 30, 2026
- CFIUS: outstanding; contractual outside date extended to September 28, 2026
- Merger-linked bond tender offer extended to settle on or about July 20, 2026 -- consortium positioning for H2 2026 close
- EA's third 2026 layoff round: trust & safety, customer support, IT -- putting multiplayer moderation at risk; no headcount disclosed
- Scale context: EA generates approximately $7-8B in annual revenue; the deal values it at ~7x revenue

## Strategic Significance

1. **July 22 EU decision is the pivotal moment.** The EU antitrust clearance (or conditions attached) will determine whether the deal closes in Q3 or slips to late 2026. DG Comp has shown willingness to impose behavioural remedies on gaming M&A (the Activision deal required cloud streaming commitments); a similar conditions package would affect EA's platform relationships post-close.

2. **Saudi sovereign capital is now the controlling force in two of the five largest western gaming publishers.** Saudi PIF owns a significant stake in Nintendo, controls Savvy Games Group (MLBB owner Moonton, $6B deal in March 2026), and -- if this clears -- will effectively control EA. The concentration of Saudi sovereign capital across platform-level gaming assets is a structural shift in who funds and controls the industry.

3. **Trust and safety cuts ahead of deal close create live-service risk.** EA's multiplayer portfolio (Battlefield 6, FIFA/EA Sports FC, Apex Legends) depends on functional moderation. Pre-close cuts to trust and safety are a red flag for multiplayer game quality post-acquisition. Studios competing against EA titles have a window of quality advantage.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- For client pitch and publisher selection advisory: EA is not a stable partner during this transition period; deals signed now will be inherited by a new ownership structure with different priorities
- For investment advisory work: Saudi PIF's continued accumulation of gaming assets is the most important capital flow in the sector; any studio seeking strategic investment should understand PIF's gaming thesis
- For regulatory advisory: the EU FSR (Foreign Subsidies Regulation) proceeding is new territory for gaming M&A -- this is the first gaming deal subject to this regulation; it sets precedent for any future non-EU sovereign capital acquisition of a gaming company
- July 22 is actionable: monitor the EC decision for any conditions attached that reshape EA's platform or distribution commitments
