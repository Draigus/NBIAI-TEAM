# Google Play Catalog Interoperability Goes Live 22 July 2026: Android Developers Must Opt In or Out

**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Platform Policy
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 8 | **Actionability:** 8

## Summary

On 22 July 2026, Google will activate Play Catalog Access -- a programme allowing approved third-party Android app stores in the US to list and distribute any developer's app by default, unless the developer explicitly opts out before the deadline. The mechanism is a direct consequence of the Epic v. Google antitrust settlement (completed March 2026): Google's standard commission drops to 20% for new installs (subscriptions 10%), with a further 5% fee if developers use Google's payment system. The original permanent injunction from October 2024 remains in force while Judge Donato weighs the settlement.

## Key Facts

- Effective date: 22 July 2026 (US Android only initially)
- Developer opt-out deadline: 22 July 2026 (Google notified developers 22 June 2026)
- Third-party stores can access the Play Catalog by signing up to the new programme
- Commission structure from Epic v. Google settlement: 20% standard (down from 30%), 10% subscriptions, 5% additional if using Google Play Billing
- Settlement not yet court-approved; original October 2024 permanent injunction still in effect
- Developers can now promote cheaper external deals and link to external downloads without fee
- FTC weighed in on proposed modifications in January 2026, signalling continued federal oversight

## Strategic Significance

1. **This is the most significant structural change to Android distribution since the Play Store launched.** The commission reduction from 30% to 20% is immediately material to mobile game margin.

2. **The opt-out-by-default mechanism means many developers will be distributed through third-party stores without actively choosing to be.** Distribution implications -- brand control, support obligations, regional availability -- need to be assessed before the 22 July deadline.

3. **If the settlement receives final court approval, the fee structure becomes permanent and resets pricing benchmarks across the entire mobile tier.** The 20% commission is now the reference rate for Android; iOS's 30% is under renewed pressure.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Mobile-first clients need an immediate decision on the opt-out before 22 July -- time-sensitive
- NBI can frame the analysis: third-party store distribution benefits (lower commission, broader reach) versus risks (fragmented installs, player support complexity, brand dilution)
- The commission reduction changes unit economics on mobile F2P models and may affect pricing and bundle strategy
- For clients currently negotiating platform deals, 20% is now the Android reference floor; relevant anchor for any terms discussion
