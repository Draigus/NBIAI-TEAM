# Bungie Confirms 292 Layoffs via WARN Notice; Destiny 2 Ending After Nine Years, Studio Head Resigns

**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Industry Structure
**Relevance:** 9 | **Novelty:** 8 | **Actionability:** 7

## Summary

Sony Interactive Entertainment confirmed on June 25, 2026, that Bungie is cutting 292 employees, formalised via Washington State WARN notice with an effective date of July 9, 2026. Destiny 2 will end after nearly nine years of live-service operation. Studio head Justin Truman resigned and is replaced by 11-year Bungie veteran Poria Torkan. The cuts affect most of the Destiny team and portions of the Marathon team. This is Sony's second major PlayStation Studios closure event of 2026, following Bluepoint Games (approximately 70 employees, February 2026).

## Key Facts

- WARN notice: 292 full-time employees, effective July 9, 2026 (Washington State)
- Destiny 2: officially ending after approximately nine years of continuous live-service operation
- Studio head Justin Truman resigned; replaced by Poria Torkan (11-year Bungie tenure)
- Cuts confirmed to affect "most of the Destiny team" and portions of the Marathon team
- Sony PlayStation Studios context: this follows Bluepoint Games closure in February 2026 (~70 employees)
- Sony acquired Bungie in January 2023 for $3.6 billion; the acquisition is now widely cited as a strategic failure
- Marathon (extraction shooter in development) reportedly still active but team composition significantly reduced

## Strategic Significance

1. Bungie is the canonical case study for the risks of a live-service studio being acquired by a platform holder. The Sony/Bungie outcome -- acquisition for $3.6 billion followed by gutting of the Destiny team within three years -- establishes a clear data point on what "platform holder controls live-service IP" means for the studio that built it. This is now a reference case, not a warning.

2. The death of Destiny 2 is a live-service milestone: a nine-year-old title with a dedicated playerbase being shut down by the acquiring platform rather than by player abandonment. This signals that Sony's calculus is financial margin, not community obligation -- relevant for any studio in live-service negotiations with a platform.

3. Sony's second major first-party closure in four months (Bluepoint + Bungie) confirms a structural pattern, not an isolated event. PlayStation Studios is contracting, not growing.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Studios considering platform exclusivity deals or acquisition conversations with Sony should treat Bungie/Bluepoint as live evidence of what platform acquisitions look like in a margin-pressure environment.
- Couch Heroes: any live-service or GaaS ambition should include explicit contractual protections around studio autonomy and title continuity if a platform or publisher acquisition scenario becomes realistic.
- The July 9 WARN effective date releases experienced Destiny-era developers (live-service systems, economies, community management) into the market; relevant for any NBI client in active hiring for live-service roles.
