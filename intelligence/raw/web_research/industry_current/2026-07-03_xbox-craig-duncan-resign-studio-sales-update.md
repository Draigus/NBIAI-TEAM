# Xbox Head of Game Studios Craig Duncan Resigns; Compulsion, Double Fine, Undead Labs in Sale Talks

**Date:** 2026-07-03
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Layoffs / M&A
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 7 | **Actionability:** 7

## Summary

Craig Duncan, Head of Xbox Game Studios, has resigned as the July layoff wave executes. Three studios confirmed in last cycle's round-up as "in negotiations" have now been given more specific public details: Double Fine (Psychonauts) and Compulsion Games (South of Midnight) are actively in management buyout or third-party sale negotiations; Undead Labs (State of Decay series) faces an undetermined future weeks after a State of Decay 3 reveal at the Xbox Games Showcase. This update extends the 2026-07-02 extract with the leadership departure and studio-level resolution signals.

## Key Facts

- Craig Duncan, Head of Xbox Game Studios, has resigned (timing: concurrent with July layoff wave)
- Double Fine (San Francisco, ~170 staff, Psychonauts): in negotiations for sale or management buyout
- Compulsion Games (Montreal, South of Midnight): in negotiations for sale or management buyout
- Undead Labs (Kirkland WA, State of Decay series): status unclear; State of Decay 3 was revealed at Xbox Games Showcase just weeks before the Reset execution
- Microsoft has not publicly confirmed closure or sale for any of the three studios
- Private investment in gaming fell approximately 55% in 2025 (per search result; treat as indicative, not audited)
- Background: Craig Duncan previously led Rare (Sea of Thieves) before heading Xbox Game Studios

## Strategic Significance

1. **The loss of the head of game studios during the execution of a restructuring is a significant leadership signal.** Duncan oversaw the internal studio portfolio. His departure removes institutional knowledge about the studios in play and reduces the likelihood of a nuanced outcome (e.g., independence with ongoing support). Decisions are now likely being taken at the Asha Sharma / Matt Booty level with less advocacy for studio preservation.

2. **State of Decay 3 reveal followed immediately by Undead Labs uncertainty is a rare sequencing failure.** Announcing a sequel at a major showcase and then placing the developer in restructuring limbo within weeks creates severe credibility damage for Xbox's publishing communications. It also leaves the State of Decay 3 project in a state where no publisher commitment is confirmed.

3. **MBO structure for Double Fine or Compulsion is constrained by the capital environment.** Gaming private investment declining approximately 55% in 2025 means fewer buyers with appetite for distressed studio acquisitions. MBOs require debt financing that is more expensive in the current environment. The realistic outcome pool is: third-party acquirer (existing publisher), independence with minimal support, or closure.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Craig Duncan's departure removes the most likely internal advocate for studio survival outcomes; any client tracking these studios as acquisition targets should recalibrate expectations toward closure or external sale rather than Xbox-supported independence
- Double Fine and Compulsion are credible acquisition targets for publishers seeking established IP (Psychonauts, South of Midnight) without a development pipeline cost; relevant for any NBI client doing M&A advisory in the mid-tier studio space
- State of Decay 3 as a stranded franchise (revealed without a confirmed developer/publisher future) is an unusual asset class; could be acquired separately from Undead Labs
- For talent advisory: both studios have senior developers with AAA RPG, adventure, and zombie-survival expertise; market timing matters for recruitment windows
