# EU Commission Rejects Stop Killing Games Legislation; Proposes Voluntary Industry Code Instead

**Date:** 2026-06-16
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Regulation
**Relevance:** 7 | **Novelty:** 7 | **Actionability:** 6

## Summary

On June 16, 2026, the European Commission adopted its formal Communication responding to the "Stop Destroying Videogames" European Citizens' Initiative (popularly known as Stop Killing Games). The initiative gathered 1,294,188 verified signatures calling for legislation requiring publishers to keep discontinued games playable after commercial end-of-life. The Commission declined to propose legislation, citing the complexity of imposing such obligations across diverse game formats and business models. Instead, the Commission committed to facilitating a voluntary industry code of conduct on game end-of-life transparency and preservation, to be developed by end of 2026.

## Key Facts

- Commission Communication date: June 16, 2026
- Initiative: "Stop Destroying Videogames" (European Citizens' Initiative)
- Verified signatures: 1,294,188
- European Parliament plenary debate: May 21, 2026
- Outcome: no legislative proposal; mandatory game preservation requirement rejected
- Alternative: voluntary industry code of conduct, developed by end of 2026 with industry and consumer representatives
- Supporting measure: Commission to work with national consumer authorities on awareness of existing end-of-life consumer rights
- Games affected: online-dependent titles that become unplayable upon server shutdown

## Strategic Significance

1. Publishers retain full discretion to shut down online-only games with no legal preservation obligation in EU law; the "games as a service" model faces no mandated end-of-life requirement as a result of this process.
2. The voluntary code creates a medium-term reputational dynamic: studios and publishers that engage constructively in code drafting can shape the outcome and signal consumer-friendly positioning; those that resist or stall the process will be visible to the 1.3 million signatories who backed the initiative.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- For Couch Heroes and NBI clients building online-dependent or live-service titles: no new legal obligation created, but the code process is worth monitoring -- voluntary today may become mandatory if the Commission reviews the code's uptake in 2028-2030 (standard Commission pattern for voluntary frameworks).
- NBI's legal advisory context (Saybrook Legal): absence of legislation means contractual end-of-life clauses in publishing agreements remain the only consumer-facing protection; this is relevant when reviewing any IP licensing or publishing contracts that govern online-only titles.
- The Commission's rejection is the definitive answer to the question of whether EU game preservation law was coming in this legislative cycle -- it is not.
