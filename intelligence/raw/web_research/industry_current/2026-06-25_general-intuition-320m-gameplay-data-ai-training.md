# General Intuition Raises $320M at $2.3B Valuation: Gameplay Footage as Tier-1 AI Training Data

**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Funding
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 9 | **Actionability:** 7

## Summary

General Intuition, spun out of Medal (the gameplay clip platform), raised $320 million in a new funding round on June 25, 2026, bringing its total disclosed funding to $454 million and valuation to $2.3 billion. Khosla Ventures led the round, joined by General Catalyst, Jeff Bezos, Eric Schmidt, and researchers from Google DeepMind and MIT. The company trains AI agents on hundreds of millions of hours of gameplay footage to develop spatial-temporal reasoning, with the same model demonstrated playing Fortnite and controlling a quadruped robot. The round is the clearest institutional signal yet that gameplay telemetry and replay data is a tier-1 AI training asset with a defined commercial value.

## Key Facts

- Funding: $320 million new round; total disclosed funding now $454 million (follows $134 million seed, October 2025)
- Valuation: $2.3 billion
- Lead investor: Khosla Ventures
- Other investors: General Catalyst, Jeff Bezos, Eric Schmidt, researchers from Google DeepMind and MIT
- CEO: Pim de Witte (31), founder of Medal (gameplay clip platform, the underlying data source)
- Training data: hundreds of millions of hours of gameplay footage sourced from Medal's user base
- Demonstrated capability: the same model plays Fortnite and controls a physical quadruped robot (spatial-temporal reasoning transfer from game to real world)
- Planned product: API launch by end of summer 2026; Nerve platform (gamer data labelling marketplace)
- Use of funds: majority allocated to compute scaling via CoreWeave partnership
- Source: TechCrunch, June 25, 2026

## Strategic Significance

1. At a $2.3 billion valuation, Khosla and General Catalyst have priced gameplay data as a tier-1 AI training asset. This is not a games-adjacent investment -- it is an AI infrastructure investment that happens to use gaming data. The implication is that studios sitting on significant replay or telemetry data possess an asset with a defined institutional valuation model, separate from any game IP.

2. The real-world transfer capability (Fortnite → quadruped robot) is notable because it suggests the value proposition extends beyond gaming applications. If proven at scale, this positions game studios -- particularly those with large active fanbases generating continuous replay data -- as inadvertent contributors to general AI capability infrastructure.

3. The Nerve platform (gamer data labelling marketplace) creates a revenue model that monetises player behaviour without requiring IP licensing. This is a structural innovation: players as a labour pool for AI training, separate from the studio that made the game.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Any NBI client with significant replay/telemetry data (particularly live-service titles with large daily active userbases) should now evaluate that data as a separate commercial asset, not just a product analytics input. General Intuition's valuation provides an external reference for what that asset might be worth to an AI company.
- For new client engagements: NBI should include a standard data-asset audit question alongside the IP and team assessments. Studios often do not know the volume or quality of replay data they hold.
- PlaySage product context: if PlaySage captures project/workflow data at scale, the Nerve model is a reference point for how that data could be monetised beyond the core SaaS offering.
