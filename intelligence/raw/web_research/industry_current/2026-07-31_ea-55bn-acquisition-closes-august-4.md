# EA Goes Private: $55 Billion PIF-Led Acquisition Closes August 4, 2026

**Date:** 2026-07-31
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** M&A
**Relevance:** 9 | **Novelty:** 7 | **Actionability:** 8

## Summary

Electronic Arts will become a privately held company on August 4, 2026, when a consortium led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) closes a $55 billion all-cash acquisition. All regulatory approvals -- including CFIUS and the European Commission -- were obtained by July 30, 2026. PIF will hold 93.4% of the company; Silver Lake Partners and Affinity Partners (Jared Kushner's PE firm) hold the remainder. The deal was originally announced September 29, 2025 at $210 per share; the August 4 close is the culmination of a 10-month regulatory process.

## Key Facts

- **Deal value:** $55 billion enterprise value; $210 per share (all-cash)
- **Buyer consortium:** PIF (93.4% post-close, including roll-over of existing 9.9% stake); Silver Lake Partners; Affinity Partners
- **Close date:** On or about the close of trading on August 4, 2026
- **Regulatory clearance completed:** July 30, 2026 (EU Commission final approval; CFIUS cleared earlier)
- **Original announcement:** September 29, 2025
- **EA IP portfolio in scope:** Apex Legends, FIFA (EA Sports FC), The Sims, Battlefield, Dragon Age, Mass Effect
- **Context:** The deal is the second-largest video game acquisition by enterprise value (behind Microsoft's $68.7bn Activision Blizzard close in 2023); it removes EA from public markets entirely

## Strategic Significance

1. **The publisher map changes structurally** EA's exit from public markets removes one of three major Western multiplatform publishers (alongside Activision/Blizzard/King and Ubisoft) from listed equity. PIF's 93.4% stake extends Saudi sovereign capital's reach across Activision, Nintendo (indirect), ESL/FACEIT, SNK, and now EA -- the largest concentration of gaming IP under a single sovereign-wealth vehicle in history.

2. **Private ownership unlocks long-cycle decisions** Publicly-listed EA faced quarterly earnings pressure that shaped release cadence, franchise risk tolerance, and studio consolidation. Under PIF/Silver Lake, EA can take extended development timelines and absorb restructuring costs without public market reaction. Expect fewer annual sports titles and more premium bets on GaaS extensions.

3. **Licensing and IP deal velocity likely to increase** Silver Lake brings operational PE discipline; Affinity Partners adds access to US political and financial networks (Kushner's GCC relationships). NBI clients building in EA's IP orbit (FIFA licensing, Origin platform, Frostbite middleware) should expect faster commercial decisions but potentially stricter terms.
