# India PROG Rules 2026 Come into Force -- Online Money Games Banned, Gaming Authority Established

**Date:** 2026-07-02
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Regulation
**Relevance:** 6 | **Novelty:** 5 | **Actionability:** 6

## Summary

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules, 2026 -- the implementing subordinate legislation under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 -- came into force on 1 July 2026. The Rules operationalise a three-tier classification system for online games in India and establish the Online Gaming Authority of India (OGAI) as the unified regulator under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. Critically, the Rules introduce a complete ban on "online money games" -- defined broadly to include games of chance, skill, or a combination -- covering their operation, advertisement, and associated financial transactions. This is separate from the Act's announcement (captured May 2026); the Rules are the enforcement instrument.

## Key Facts

- Effective date: 1 July 2026
- Regulatory body: Online Gaming Authority of India (OGAI), under MeitY
- Three-tier game classification:
  - E-Sports: recognised as legitimate skill-based competitive sport
  - Online Social Games: skill-based entertainment and social interaction, permitted with registration
  - Online Money Games: financial stakes involved -- completely banned, including advertising and payment processing
- "Online money game" definition is broad: covers chance-based, skill-based, and hybrid games involving financial stakes
- Mandatory compliance for registered categories: age verification, parental controls, time limits, grievance redressal, counselling support
- Financial intermediaries must verify OGAI registration status before processing transactions for e-sports and social games
- International developers: must comply with registration requirements for e-sports and any social games the Central Government designates
- Data localisation: data retention on Indian servers mandated for registered game categories
- Penalties: OGAI may suspend/cancel registrations, impose financial penalties (credited to India's Consolidated Fund), and prohibit non-compliant games

## Strategic Significance

1. **India's online money game ban is the broadest skills-game prohibition enacted by a major gaming market.** Unlike the UK ASA monitoring framework or EU PEGI 16 loot box floor (which regulate mechanics), India's framework prohibits the entire category, including advertising and payment processing. Studios with IAP mechanics that could be classified as "financial stakes" -- including loot boxes, gacha pulls, and paid randomised drops -- face classification risk under OGAI review.

2. **India is a growth market with 500M+ mobile gamers and the enforcement environment has now hardened.** Prior to these Rules, online gaming operated in a regulatory grey zone. The OGAI now has powers to block apps, prohibit financial transactions, and impose penalties. For studios distributing in India, this is a compliance environment change, not an advisory.

3. **The regulatory model -- a unified digital-first authority with classification powers -- is one other Asian governments are watching.** India's OGAI is structurally similar to South Korea's Game Rating and Administration Committee (GRAC) and Thailand's proposed gaming regulator. A pattern is forming: large Asian gaming markets moving from ad-hoc enforcement to purpose-built regulatory bodies with classification authority.
