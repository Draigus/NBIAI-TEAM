# Google Play Catalog Interoperability Goes Live 22 July -- Third-Party Stores Get US App Listings

**Date:** 2026-07-15
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Platform Policy
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 6 | **Actionability:** 8

## Summary

Google began notifying developers on 15 July 2026 that the Play Catalog Access programme launches 22 July, automatically sharing US app and game listings -- name, icon, description, screenshots, video -- with enrolled third-party Android stores unless developers explicitly opt out. This is the first court-ordered catalogue interoperability mandate enforced on a major US app platform, stemming from Epic v. Google (October 2024 order, March 2026 settlement collapse). Third-party stores must pay $5,000 per year and maintain a sub-1% malware rate to qualify.

## Key Facts

- Live date: 22 July 2026 (US market only)
- Developers auto-enrolled by default; must actively opt out before 22 July to exclude their listings
- Three developer options: full participation across all enrolled stores; per-store management; full opt-out
- Third-party store annual access fee: $5,000; must meet Google malware thresholds and publish non-discriminatory policies
- Potential qualifying stores include Epic Games Store and Amazon Appstore (neither confirmed enrolled as of 15 July)
- Mandate derives from the October 2024 Epic v. Google court order; the March 2026 Epic-Google settlement withdrawal reverted to the full original order
- Original settlement ran until June 2032; replaced by court order which has no stated sunset
- First wave of enrolment data from third-party stores expected after 22 July

## Strategic Significance

1. **First US platform compelled to distribute competitors' catalogues** No US court had previously forced a platform to actively list rival stores' catalogues. If upheld on appeal, this sets a precedent for Apple App Store litigation and any future state or federal app store regulatory action.
2. **Structural advantage for alternative Android distribution** Alternative stores previously required users to sideload or navigate obscure settings. Catalogue discoverability inside the Play Store dramatically reduces the friction barrier -- the first time a game appears in an alternative store without the developer doing any distribution work.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Clients with mobile games on Android should have decided their opt-in/opt-out position before 22 July; NBI conversations with mobile studio clients should flag this immediately if not already addressed
- Epic Games Store's potential Android enrolment is strategically significant: Epic has been actively courting developers with lower revenue share (12%) -- any client already on Epic PC store should evaluate whether their mobile catalogue auto-populates an Epic mobile presence
- Relevant benchmark for any future platform strategy or market access analysis NBI produces for mobile-first clients
- Note: the Google Play commission reduction (30% to 20% on qualifying titles) is a separate but concurrent development; both affect the same client conversations
