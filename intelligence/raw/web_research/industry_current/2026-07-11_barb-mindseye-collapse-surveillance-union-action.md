# Build a Rocket Boy: MindsEye Collapse, Surveillance Software, and IWGB Legal Action

**Date:** 2026-07-11
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Layoffs / Studio Governance
**Relevance:** 6 | **Novelty:** 6 | **Actionability:** 6

## Summary

Build A Rocket Boy (BARB), the Edinburgh studio led by ex-GTA producer Leslie Benzies, has confirmed a second round of layoffs in 2026, is alleging "organised corporate sabotage" as the explanation for MindsEye's failed launch, and faces IWGB union protests outside its offices on 11 July 2026 alongside imminent legal action over the covert installation of monitoring software on employee machines. The case is emerging as a significant cautionary example of studio governance failure following a high-budget, high-profile launch.

## Key Facts

- MindsEye launched June 2025 to widespread critical and commercial failure
- First layoff round: June 2025, two weeks after MindsEye launched; headcount undisclosed
- Second layoff round: confirmed 2026; studio attributed MindsEye's poor reception to "organised espionage and corporate sabotage" (no substantiation provided)
- Surveillance: Teramind monitoring software installed on employee machines without consent; workers discovered it in January 2025 when it slowed game development tools including Maya
- IWGB union protests: 11 July 2026 outside Edinburgh studio, coinciding with a company-funded fan playtest day the union described as a misuse of funds
- IWGB legal action filed: separate claims for data privacy violations (Teramind) and mishandled redundancy process
- IOI Partners (10% stakeholder) publicly stated it "no longer wants anything to do with MindsEye"
- Senior executives departed one week before MindsEye launched; post-mortems cite "knee-jerk" leadership decisions throughout development

## Strategic Significance

1. **Corporate sabotage framing is a governance red flag** Attributing a product failure to saboteurs rather than product quality and leadership decisions signals dysfunction to future employees, investors, and partners. It validates nothing, inflames existing staff, and creates a public record that will follow executives into future roles. The credibility cost to Benzies and BARB is material and ongoing.

2. **Surveillance software as a governance failure pattern** Covert installation of Teramind constitutes a GDPR violation and an employment law risk under UK law. More fundamentally, it represents a management response to distrust that amplified the distrust it was trying to address. The IWGB legal action may set a precedent for how unionised creative workers challenge non-consensual monitoring. Any UK studio considering similar tools should treat this case as a live liability example.

3. **Post-launch layoff speed signals absent contingency planning** The two-week interval between MindsEye's launch and the first layoff round suggests either no contingency plan for a weak launch or that the layoffs were pre-planned irrespective of performance. Either reading is damaging to the leadership narrative and suggests the studio was financially over-extended relative to launch risk.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Cautionary case study for any NBI client managing a high-stakes launch: communication strategy, contingency planning, and staff trust are as important as product quality
- Directly relevant to Couch Heroes governance discussions: the covert surveillance pattern is something NBI should actively advise against in any studio policy framework
- The IWGB legal action is worth tracking for any UK studio NBI advises: it demonstrates union willingness to escalate data privacy violations to employment tribunal level, not just industrial action
