# PEGI Introduces Mandatory PEGI 16 Rating for All Games with Paid Loot Boxes from June 2026

**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Regulation
**Relevance:** 9 | **Novelty:** 8 | **Actionability:** 9

## Summary

From June 2026, PEGI's revised classification framework automatically assigns a minimum PEGI 16 rating to any new game submission containing paid random items -- loot boxes, card packs, gacha mechanics, or similar. The rule applies to all new submissions; existing classified titles are unaffected unless updated content triggers reclassification. EA Sports FC is the most prominently affected franchise: currently rated PEGI 3, it faces a PEGI 16 rating if it retains paid loot mechanics in Ultimate Team, forcing a commercial choice between the age label and the monetisation model.

## Key Facts

- Effective June 2026 for all new PEGI submissions across 38 European territories
- Mechanism: paid randomised rewards (loot boxes, card packs, gacha) automatically trigger PEGI 16 floor
- Existing classified titles not re-rated unless the game is updated
- EA Sports FC: current rating PEGI 3, would become PEGI 16 if Ultimate Team loot mechanics retained
- PEGI's new 'interactive risk categories' framework integrates monetisation mechanics, communication features, and engagement design into age classification for the first time
- EU Digital Fairness Act (expected 2027) likely to impose outright ban on loot boxes for minors; PEGI change is a forerunner rather than the final regulation

## Strategic Significance

1. **This is the single most consequential near-term regulatory event for live service games with Western release ambitions.** The PEGI 16 floor does not ban loot boxes but imposes a retail and marketing cost that is commercially material for family-facing titles.

2. **Publishers with cross-demographic appeal face a binary choice: strip the randomised monetisation or accept a restrictive age label.** Sports games, platformers, and family games are the most exposed categories.

3. **The EU Digital Fairness Act arriving likely in 2027 will be the hard stop.** Publishers that do not redesign monetisation now will face a forced redesign under a tighter deadline. The PEGI change is the warning shot.
