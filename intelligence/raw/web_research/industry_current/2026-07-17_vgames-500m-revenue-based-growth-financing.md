# Vgames Raises $500M to Launch Revenue-Based Growth Financing for Gaming

**Date:** 2026-07-17
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Funding / Market Structure
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 8 | **Actionability:** 7

## Summary

Israeli gaming investment fund vgames has raised $500 million backed by Phoenix Holdings to launch a revenue-based growth financing platform for gaming and consumer companies. The model links repayment to the actual revenue performance of user cohorts funded through the investment, removing traditional debt pressure while protecting founder equity. vgames and General Catalyst had previously deployed more than $350M through a prototype of this structure before the public platform launch.

## Key Facts

- Announced approximately 17 July 2026; reported by PocketGamer.biz, Ctech, and InvestGame
- $500M raised from Phoenix Holdings; vgames plans to deploy more than $500M additionally through the platform in coming years
- Model: repayments tied directly to revenue generated by ad-campaign user cohorts, not to fixed payment schedules
- Removes equity dilution for studios needing growth capital; removes fixed-debt risk
- Prior co-deployment with General Catalyst: more than $350M already deployed through similar structures before the public launch
- Targets gaming and consumer companies at growth stage, specifically user acquisition and expansion

## Strategic Significance

1. **Non-dilutive at scale** Historically, game studios seeking user acquisition capital faced a binary choice: dilutive equity rounds or revenue-advance debt with punishing fixed terms. This platform creates a third path. Cohort-performance-linked repayment means studios pay back proportionally only if the funded user group delivers. At $500M planned deployment, this is a serious alternative to traditional VC or publisher advance structures.

2. **Institutional backing signals durability** vgames has a track record across three gaming funds totalling over $500M in equity investments. The new platform is backed by Phoenix, a major Israeli insurer, suggesting the structure could attract further institutional LPs looking for gaming exposure without individual deal risk. This is not a one-off arrangement.

3. **Competitive pressure on publisher deal structures** Publisher development deals often bundle UA commitments as a control lever. A non-dilutive, non-IP-encumbering UA financing alternative directly reduces developer dependence on publisher funding, which shifts negotiation dynamics in favour of studios.
