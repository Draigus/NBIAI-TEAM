# GTA 6 Pre-Orders Open: $79.99 Standard, $99.99 Ultimate, No Disc, November 19 Launch Confirmed

**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Domain:** industry_current
**Category:** Market Data
**Relevance:** 8 | **Novelty:** 7 | **Actionability:** 7

## Summary

Rockstar Games opened Grand Theft Auto VI pre-orders on June 25, 2026, with the Rockstar Newswire and Take-Two IR confirming a $79.99 standard edition, a $99.99 Ultimate Edition, and a November 19, 2026 release on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Critically, Rockstar confirmed there is no disc version at launch -- physical retail units contain a download code only. No PC date was announced. This moves the event from anticipated to confirmed: price, format, and date are now locked.

## Key Facts

- Pre-orders open: June 25, 2026 (all major storefronts)
- Standard Edition: $79.99 USD
- Ultimate Edition: $99.99 USD
- Release date: November 19, 2026 (PS5 and Xbox Series X|S only)
- No disc at launch -- physical copies are code-in-box
- Pre-order bonus (all buyers before November 20): Vintage Vice City Pack
- Digital pre-order bonus: 1 month GTA+
- PC version: no date announced; follows precedent of GTA 5 and RDR2 (12-18 months post-console)
- No mobile release at launch

## Strategic Significance

1. The $79.99 standard price moves the AAA floor from $70 to $80 at the highest-profile title of the generation. Publishers and studios pricing 2026-2027 releases will face direct consumer comparison to this anchor; any premium game priced at $60-70 will require a positioning rationale.
2. The code-in-box physical format at this scale eliminates the secondary resale market for what is expected to be the largest single entertainment launch by revenue in history. This is the most commercially significant test yet of the industry's shift away from disc -- outcome will influence publisher decisions for 2027 and beyond.

## NBI Advisory Relevance

- Any NBI client with a Q4 2026 or early Q1 2027 launch window must now treat the six to eight weeks around November 19 as severely compressed for consumer attention and discretionary spend. This is not optional context -- it is a planning constraint.
