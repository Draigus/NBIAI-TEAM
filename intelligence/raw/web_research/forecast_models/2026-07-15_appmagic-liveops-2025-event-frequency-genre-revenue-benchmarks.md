# AppMagic Live Ops 2025: Event Frequency Benchmarks and Genre Revenue Splits

## Key Content

**Source:** AppMagic annual live ops report, summarised by Dmitriy Byshonkov via GameDev Reports Substack (gamedevreports.substack.com). Based on AppMagic's platform data across the top-grossing mobile titles in 2025.

**What this extract IS:** Market benchmark data for calibrating live ops planning and revenue forecasting. It tells you what the top-grossing games actually run, not how to model the revenue.

**What this extract IS NOT:** A replicable forecasting model. Use it to calibrate inputs to live ops revenue forecasting (event frequency assumptions, genre revenue weights), not as a standalone methodology.

### Event Frequency Benchmarks

Among top-grossing mobile games in 2025:
- **Average events per game per month:** Started at 73 at the beginning of 2025, grew to 89 by year-end.
- This represents roughly 2–3 live events per day on average for a top-performing game.

**By genre:**
- Casual: 0.7 new live ops events added per month on average (slowest growth)
- Midcore: 23% growth in event density year-over-year (fastest growth)
- Hybrid casual/hypercasual: 19% growth, averaging ~64 events per month (lower density)

**Seasonal patterns:**
- New mechanic introductions peak in spring, decline in winter.
- Casual games spike during New Year holiday periods.
- October–December shows sharp event density increases in hybrid casual/hypercasual titles (Halloween, Christmas).

### Event Type Adoption Rates

**Fastest-growing event types in 2025:**
| Event Type | Growth Rate | Genre |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| Stamp events | +96% YoY | Casual |
| Login calendars | +93% YoY | Midcore |
| Win Streak / Lava Quest | >50% | All |
| Cooperative events | +36% | Midcore |
| Competitive events | +24% | Casual & All |

**Highest adoption (% of titles with feature):**
- Collections: Present in 80% of analysed titles (most universal)
- Gacha: >80% implementation rate once adopted (very sticky once added)
- Expeditions: >80% usage frequency once added (also sticky despite being adopted by only 1% of titles)

**Underutilised mechanics with high implementation stickiness:** Expeditions are adopted by only 1% of titles but retained at 80%+ usage frequency -- suggesting low awareness, not low effectiveness.

### Cooperative vs Competitive Event Trend

Cooperative events are growing faster than competitive events in midcore (+36% vs +24%). This is a structural shift: the market is moving from competition-driven engagement (tournaments, leaderboards, PvP events) toward cooperation-driven retention (clan events, co-op challenges, guild mechanics). For NBI clients planning live service event calendars, prioritising cooperative infrastructure in midcore is aligned with the direction of the market.

### Revenue Splits by Genre (2025 actuals)

**Total mobile game revenue breakdown:**
- Midcore: 65% of total mobile IAP revenue
- Casual: 34% of total mobile IAP revenue
- Hypercasual: 1% of total mobile IAP revenue

**Midcore sub-genre:**
- RPG: 37% of midcore revenue (down 15.3% YoY -- structural decline)
- Strategy: Growing fastest, up 17.9% YoY

**Casual sub-genre:**
- Casino: 43% of casual revenue (dominant within casual)

**Fastest-growing segment:**
- Hybrid casual: $788 million revenue in 2025, +75.1% YoY -- the fastest-growing segment in mobile gaming

**Total mobile revenue growth:** Only +0.7% YoY in 2025 -- a near-flat market overall. All growth is concentrated in strategy and hybrid casual; RPG and pure casual are in structural decline.

### Lifecycle Revenue Distribution

**Games released 2015–2020: 49% of all mobile game revenue.**
**Games released 2023–2025: Only 22% of all mobile game revenue.**

The implication: the majority of mobile game revenue is generated by titles 5–10 years old. A 5-year-old game with strong live ops is more valuable than a new game with comparable metrics. This directly validates the economics of lifecycle extension investments -- live ops spending on an established title has a measurable return because the title's revenue contribution is disproportionate to its age.

### Monetisation Focus of Events

70% of live ops events in top-grossing games have a clear paying-user focus. Events are not primarily free engagement tools -- they are structured monetisation touchpoints. The 30% without a direct monetisation focus serve retention and re-engagement functions that support LTV indirectly.

## Decisions / Insights

- **Benchmark event frequency:** A top-performing mobile game in 2025 ran 73–89 events per month. NBI clients running 20–30 events per month are operating at roughly 25–35% of the competitive live ops density for their tier. When advising on live ops scaling, this is the external benchmark to reference.
- **Cooperative events should be in the roadmap for midcore clients.** The +36% growth rate versus +24% for competitive events is a strong market signal. Clients investing in PvP-only events are building into a declining share of the event mix.
- **Collections are table stakes.** 80% adoption means a midcore or casual title without a collections mechanic is missing the most universally implemented live ops feature.
- **RPG revenue is in structural decline (-15.3% YoY).** NBI clients with RPG-adjacent games should be forecasting flat or declining revenue without aggressive live ops investment. Strategy is the growth category in midcore.
- **The lifecycle data is a retention advisory.** When a client asks whether to invest in live ops for a 4-year-old game, the data says yes: games of that vintage account for nearly half of all mobile revenue. The investment has proven industry precedent.

## Context

AppMagic is a mobile game analytics platform with broad data coverage. The report is based on platform data, not survey data. Genre revenue splits and event frequency data are derived from real monetisation signals. The summary is filtered through a Substack analyst's interpretation -- primary source is the full AppMagic report (access required).

## Applicability

**Direct NBI use:** Use event frequency benchmarks to set client expectations on competitive live ops posture. Use genre revenue splits to frame market opportunity sizing and revenue forecasting for clients in each genre. Use lifecycle data as supporting evidence for lifecycle extension advisory.

**Minimum viable implementation:** No platform access required to use these benchmarks. They are inputs to planning conversations, not analytical tools.

**Limitation:** Data covers the top-grossing mobile titles -- the upper tier of the market. NBI clients at indie-to-mid-tier scale will have lower absolute event frequency than these benchmarks. The benchmarks are competitive targets, not baseline requirements. Hypercasual benchmarks are particularly inappropriate for non-hypercasual clients.
