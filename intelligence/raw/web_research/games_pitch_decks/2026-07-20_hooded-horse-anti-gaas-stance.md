# Hooded Horse CEO: Explicit Rejection of Live Service as the Publishing Default

## Key Content

**Context:** Manor Lords (solo dev, medieval city-builder) sold over 2 million copies across all stores in under three weeks after Early Access launch on April 26, 2024. External critics argued that declining concurrent player counts after launch meant the game was "failing." Hooded Horse CEO Tim Bender pushed back publicly.

**The core statement:** "Not every game should be aimed at becoming some live-service boom or bust. And a release should not begin an ever-accelerating treadmill on which devs are forced to run until their mental or physical health breaks down."

**The second argument:** "Success should not create an ever-raising bar of new growth expectations." Bender explicitly decoupled success from player count trajectory, measuring against creator aspirations rather than absolute market share numbers.

**The structural argument:** The "distorted endless growth" expectation has caused "so much trouble" in the industry. The boom-or-bust framing of live service imposes an all-or-nothing operational model that destroys studios that cannot sustain the required content cadence.

**What this means for how Hooded Horse evaluates pitches:**

Hooded Horse specialises in tactical, strategy, and simulation games. Published portfolio includes: Manor Lords, Against the Storm, Menace, Terra Nil, Songs of Conquest, Beyond All Reason.

Their published portfolio contains zero live service titles requiring ongoing battle pass / content pipeline operations. This is not absence of opportunity -- it is deliberate positioning.

The CEO's public statement constitutes an implicit submission filter: games framed as live service requiring post-launch growth acceleration are not Hooded Horse territory.

**Market context -- other publishers with similar implicit filters:**

- Devolver Digital: satirical "MaxPass+" (2021 showcase) explicitly mocked GaaS monetisation. Portfolio reflects this (Hotline Miami, Disco Elysium, Cult of the Lamb -- no battle-pass driven titles).
- Raw Fury: pitch criteria say nothing explicitly against live service, but portfolio (Into the Breach, Citizen Sleeper, Norco, Kingdom series) contains no GaaS-model games.

All three publishers have built brand identity that functions as an implicit submission filter against GaaS pitches.

## Decisions / Insights

Directly actionable for NBI when advising clients on publisher targeting.

Studios pitching a live service / GaaS game to Hooded Horse, Devolver, or Raw Fury are misaligning with publishers whose entire brand is built on rejecting the GaaS model. The lost time and relationship capital from a misaligned pitch is significant.

Conversely, for clients who want to build a sustainable multiplayer game without the live service overhead: Hooded Horse's model (sell a great game, let it settle, measure against initial projections not against Fortnite) is a legitimate counter-strategy NBI can recommend. Manor Lords proves the market exists for premium games without perpetual content pipelines.

**The advisory use case:** when a client has a multiplayer game that could be scoped as either "live service with ongoing content pipeline" or "premium multiplayer with defined feature set," the choice of publisher determines which framing is possible. Hooded Horse/Devolver/Raw Fury = premium framing. a16z/BITKRAFT/DECA = GaaS framing. The same game may require entirely different pitch documents for these two audiences.
