# Spill Games (Bengaluru) -- $3.1M Seed, Portfolio-Not-Single-Hit Pitch, April 2026

## Key Content

Spill Games (Bengaluru, India), founded 2024 by Om Misra (CEO), Tapan Ranjan, and Harsh Garg, raised a **$3.1M seed round** co-led by **Centre Court Capital** and **PeerCapital**, announced April 23, 2026.

The studio has five live titles at time of raise: Cozy Finds, Sticker by Number, and Zen Math Crossword among them. Three of the five have **positive unit economics**.

### The Pitch Narrative (Founder-Stated)

Co-founder Om Misra explicitly rejected the dominant mobile studio framing in the announcement:

> "In mobile gaming, most studios are chasing one breakout hit, which is inherently uncertain. We took a different view from day one: build the underlying system first."

And: "hit-making, done properly, is less about magic and more about method."

**What investors were buying:** A production system, not a specific game. The pitch argument is that the studio's internal tooling and process discipline reduce per-title cost, enabling more prototypes per dollar deployed, which improves the portfolio's hit probability even at seed scale.

**Specific pitch evidence at raise:**
- 5 live titles (portfolio diversification at seed stage)
- 3 of 5 with positive unit economics (execution proof)
- 20+ prototypes planned in next 18 months (throughput credibility)
- Capital earmarked for deepening the internal technology stack (process moat investment)

**Monetisation direction:** Expanding from pure ad monetisation toward hybrid-casual and in-app purchases.

## Decisions / Insights

The Spill Games pitch inverts the standard mobile studio pitch. The standard pitch is: "We have a great game, fund it." Spill's pitch is: "We have a system that produces profitable games, fund the system."

This inversion matters for investors because it changes what they are evaluating:
- Standard pitch: evaluate the specific game (market size, genre trends, team taste)
- Spill pitch: evaluate the production system (throughput, cost per prototype, unit economics rate across portfolio)

At seed stage, the evidence supporting the system argument is concrete: 3 of 5 titles at positive unit economics is a 60% strike rate. In a genre where 1-in-100 games is the industry average hit rate for hypercasual, 3-in-5 is a credible signal that the portfolio approach is working.

The 20+ prototypes in 18 months commitment is a falsifiable claim -- investors can track it. It functions as a credibility anchor in the pitch because it is specific and measurable.

**Geographic context:** Bengaluru is a cost-advantaged development location relative to London, Istanbul, or Helsinki. The unit economics argument is partly enabled by lower cost structures. For an NBI client in a higher-cost location, the same pitch requires higher per-title revenue thresholds to demonstrate equivalent capital efficiency.

## Context

Spill Games is comparable to NBI's client range (10-person founding team, pre-Series A, sub-$5M raise). The production system pitch is distinctive -- most mobile studios at this stage pitch a single game or a single game with vague "pipeline" language.

Centre Court Capital and PeerCapital are not widely known investors in the UK gaming VC landscape. This is an India-centric early-stage funding story.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** The production-system framing is applicable to any mobile studio client that has shipped more than one title. Advise clients to count and document: how many titles live, how many with positive unit economics, what is the prototype throughput, what is the internal tooling that creates the advantage. This reframes the pitch from "fund our next game" to "fund our production capability."

The 3-of-5 unit economics evidence is the minimum threshold that makes the system argument credible. A studio with 1 profitable title out of 5 cannot make this pitch with conviction.
