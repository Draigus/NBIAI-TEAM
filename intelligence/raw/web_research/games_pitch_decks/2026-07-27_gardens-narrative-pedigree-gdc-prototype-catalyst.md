# Gardens -- Journey / Sky Narrative Pedigree, GDC 2023 Prototype, $31.3M Series A (Lightspeed + Krafton co-lead), July 2023

## Key Content

Gardens secured $31.3 million in a Series A round in July 2023, co-led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Krafton, with participation from Dreamhaven, TIRTA, Rendered VC, Transcend Fund, FunPlus, and individual investors including Nate Mitchell (Oculus co-founder), Stephen "Slim" Lim (Valorant co-creator), and Seth Sivak (World of Warcraft VP of development).

**Founders:**
- Chris Bell (game director) -- designer on *Journey*, *What Remains of Edith Finch*, *Sky: Children of the Light*
- Lexie Dostal (technical director) -- decade-plus game development experience; co-creator of *Dustforce*
- Stephen Bell (narrative director) -- credits include *Blaseball*, *What Remains of Edith Finch*, *Sky: Children of the Light*

**Advisory board assembled at close:** John W. Thompson (former Microsoft Chairman), Jack Tretton (former Sony Computer Entertainment America President/CEO), Ben Feder (former Tencent President / Take-Two CEO). This simultaneously signals platform access to Nintendo (Yamauchi No.10 fund also participated), Sony, and Microsoft.

**Studio structure:** Fully remote, founded 2022.

**Game:** An "ever-evolving online PvPvE fantasy adventure sandbox ARPG" -- multiplayer with cooperative and betrayal mechanics, described as blending the emotional register of Journey and Sky with higher-stakes online RPG play.

### The GDC 2023 Prototype Catalyst

The exceptional fundraising outcome was driven by a playable prototype demonstrated at GDC 2023. According to Lightspeed, the prototype generated **term sheets and even acquisition offers within days** of the GDC demo, giving Gardens the unusual position of choosing investors rather than pursuing them. The founders used that leverage to select investors aligned with their creative independence values rather than accepting the highest-valuation or most-prominent-name offer.

### Krafton's Dual Role

Krafton (PUBG / Subnautica 2 publisher) invested as a co-lead alongside Lightspeed VC. Krafton's corporate development head Maria Park joined Gardens' board of directors. While the explicit publishing deal structure is not confirmed in open sources, Krafton is both a venture investor and a publisher, and their co-lead position alongside a VC reflects the hybrid VC+publisher co-investment model described by Robin Guo as the most effective Series A structure for online games: a VC providing financial capital, a publisher providing distribution and platform access, and both acting as counterbalancing forces in the studio's interest.

## Decisions / Insights

Gardens demonstrates a distinct path to formation-stage funding for narrative game designers (Journey, thatgamecompany pedigree) -- one that does not require AAA corporate pedigree (Riot VP, Blizzard co-founder) but instead relies on a body of work that commands emotional trust from investors.

Three structural features combine to explain the exceptional outcome:

1. **Prototype quality, not pedigree alone, created term sheets.** The GDC demo produced "acquisition offers within days." Prototype quality that triggers acquisition interest signals that investors see strategic value, not just investment potential.

2. **The advisory board maps to all major platform holders.** Microsoft (Thompson), Sony (Tretton), and Nintendo (Yamauchi No.10) are all represented, removing platform access risk from investor due diligence. This is deliberate syndicate construction -- the founders actively assembled cross-platform signalling.

3. **Creative independence was the filter for investor selection, not capital size.** Gardens chose investors aligned with their values rather than highest valuation. Dreamhaven (Mike Morhaime) participating as co-investor signals the same creative-first philosophy.

The structural contrast with The Believer Company ($55M) is instructive: Believer used corporate pedigree (VP/co-founder Riot) to raise on a vision pitch with no prototype. Gardens used design-pedigree (critically-acclaimed narrative games) and a working prototype to raise at comparable size without equivalent corporate credentials. Both paths work -- the prototype path is more accessible to studios without C-suite gaming industry backgrounds.

## Context

Robin Guo's "State of Game Studio Financing" article cites Gardens as an example of the "combo VC-publisher" model he proposes as the structural solution to the indie-to-live-service funding cliff -- where VCs seek exits and publishers seek content, and a co-lead structure aligns both incentives while giving founders term negotiation leverage.

Gardens is still in development as of mid-2026. No game title has been announced. The studio's fully remote structure makes them an interesting test case for remote-first live service development at 30-50 person scale.

## Applicability

The advisory board construction (platform holders, former corporate executives) is a deliberate strategy that any studio can adopt.
