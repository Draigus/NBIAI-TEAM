# VC Investment Criteria for GaaS / Live Service Game Studios -- Stage-Gated Framework (2023-2025)

## Key Content

### a16z Games Fund One Thesis

a16z's stated GaaS framing: successful games-as-a-service function as "rich, interactive social networks, with players making in-game friendships that are just as meaningful as those made in-person." Three investment categories: (1) game studios operating as continually-updated online services; (2) games x consumer apps; (3) infrastructure.

Jon Lai (General Partner, led Mountaintop rounds): previously led North America games investments at Tencent and was a developer at Riot. Stated rationale: "the next social network will be a game." No hard quantitative entry criteria disclosed by a16z; rationale is thesis-alignment plus team pedigree ("core gamers themselves").

### Stage-by-Stage Requirements (Robin Guo synthesis, 2024)

**Seed stage -- what gets funded:**
- Team quality and demonstrated ability to raise sufficient capital for market launch
- Differentiated pitch with clear market tailwinds
- Evidence of company-building thinking (product, engineering, go-to-market, talent, BD) -- not just game design
- No hard traction metrics required at this stage

**Series A -- what gets funded:**
- Demonstrated production velocity and shipped content
- Clear path to open beta with the funding tranche
- Product quality comparable to top 1% releases that year
- Proof the core thesis remains intact

**Series B -- what gets funded (hard metrics required):**
- D1/D7/D30 retention (commonly cited strong ranges for competitive multiplayer: D1 40-50%, D7 20-30%, D30 10-15% -- directional benchmarks, not certified thresholds)
- DAU/MAU stickiness (30%+ DAU/MAU correlates with organic growth at competitive scale)
- LTV:CAC ratio, CPI
- ARPU / ARPDAU
- Clear $1B+ franchise potential path
- Studio scaling strategy across multiple titles

### What VCs fund vs. what they reject

VCs fund **companies**, not individual games. The pitch must demonstrate a studio with capacity to become a multi-game publisher with its own distribution moat.

Moats evaluated for live service: user-generated content (two-sided marketplace), superior LTV over competitors (UA moat), creator communities and persistent social bonds, game-specific switching costs (guild relationships, progression systems, knowledge barriers).

Common rejection factors: game concept lacking $1B+ outcome path; insufficient proof of execution at Series A; business model misaligned with VC timelines; genre mashups without unique insight.

### Market context

VC games financing dropped for 9 consecutive quarters before a first uptick in 2024 (Konvoy data). Higher interest rates shifted preference toward lower-risk assets, making long-development-timeline games structurally less attractive to financial VCs. Continued conviction signalled by: a16z Games Fund Two ($600M), BITKRAFT Fund III ($275M), PlayVentures Fund Three ($78M).

Budget compression: studios were reducing multiplayer features from pitches in 2024 to bring budget asks down -- e.g. $3M asks compressed to $1.5M maximum, with multiplayer dropped to achieve the reduction (Little Polygon research, prior cycle).

## Decisions / Insights

The stage-gating framework is directly usable by NBI when assessing where a client studio sits and what their pitch needs to prove at each stage.

Critical advisory implication: studios pitching live service at seed/Series A are evaluated on team and thesis, not metrics. Metrics are the Series B gate. A seed pitch is fundamentally about the founding team's track record and the clarity of their social/community differentiation thesis.

The $1B+ franchise potential requirement at Series B eliminates most indie-scale GaaS from the VC market entirely. Studios with $1-10M revenue outcomes should be targeting publishers or hybrid deals (VC+publisher co-investment), not pure VC equity. The Gardens $31M VC+publisher co-investment structure (Robin Guo) is cited as a structural innovation resolving this gap -- warranting a future extract.
