# Focus Entertainment Publisher Pitch Criteria -- 2025 Standards (3,000-4,000 Annual Submissions)

## Key Content

Focus Entertainment receives **3,000-4,000 pitches per year**. The majority are rejected for category mismatch before pitch quality is evaluated. Chief Content Officer Yves Le Yaouanq stated explicitly that Focus rejects three categories on sight: **mobile games, NFT/blockchain projects, and pixel-art titles**. None of these fit Focus's portfolio focus.

Focus positions itself as the publisher for indie, double-A, and "triple-I" games -- a category they define as independent studios with AAA-level ambition and design discipline. Their stated stance: they do not chase Metaverse fantasies or bloated battle royales. Since 2021, they have expanded focus while other publishers contracted, backing unique, story-driven games (A Plague Tale series, Space Marine 2, Warhammer 40,000: Darktide are in their portfolio).

### Two Criteria That Must Both Be Present

Le Yaouanq frames the evaluation on two axes:

**Identity** -- the game must know what makes it special. Not trend-chasing. Distinctive art, gameplay, or narrative that is specific to this game and not generic to a genre.

**Maturity** -- the developer must understand industry fundamentals: funding models, platform economics, publisher expectations, and realistic financial projections. Pitches that show 100% revenue retention (ignoring platform fees, VAT, currency conversion) are disqualifying red flags.

### Prototype Is Now Required

The era of PowerPoint-only pitches has ended at Focus (and at most publishers at this tier). A pitching studio must present:
- A **playable prototype** demonstrating core mechanics
- A polished visual demonstration -- specifically described as a **"beauty corner"** (a small area of the game showing the intended visual quality at full production polish)

Some publishers now will not engage until a project reaches vertical slice or post-alpha. Focus will engage earlier, but only with a working prototype.

### How Deals Actually Get Made

**"Focus has never signed a game based solely on a cold email."** Most deals originate at in-person events: GDC, Gamescom, and regional conferences. Mass-email outreach to 200+ publishers simultaneously is identified as a disqualifying signal -- it communicates a lack of strategic targeting and diligence about fit.

The article notes this is industry normalisation post-COVID, not crisis: tighter funding and risk aversion, but studios with strong identity and working prototypes are still getting deals.

## Decisions / Insights

The 3,000-4,000 figure means roughly 95%+ of pitches never get past category screening. Knowing the explicit rejection categories (mobile, NFT, pixel art) eliminates a targeting error that wastes both sides' time.

The identity + maturity framework functions as a two-axis gate:
- Identity without maturity = a creative team that cannot execute commercially
- Maturity without identity = a competent team pitching a forgettable game

The "beauty corner" requirement is the publisher equivalent of Tim Cain's Beautiful Corner concept in production methodology -- a small, fully-polished proof of visual intent that is cheaper than a vertical slice but proves the game can look as good as the pitch promises.

The in-person deal origin point is critical for NBI clients: attending GDC, Gamescom, or regional equivalents (not just exhibiting -- networking specifically) is not optional for studios seeking a publishing deal at Focus's tier.

## Context

Focus Entertainment rebranded from Focus Home Interactive and took the 4th position in Metacritic's 2023 Global Publisher Rankings. They have a documented track record of supporting "triple-I" games from studios of 20-100 people -- directly in NBI's client range. They are a viable target publisher for NBI's clients.

The IndieGameBusiness state of pitching article surveyed multiple publishers. The Focus-specific criteria are attributed to Le Yaouanq directly. Other publishers referenced similar playable prototype requirements but did not have as specific a category rejection list.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** Primary use is pitch readiness assessment. Before any NBI client pitches to Focus (or any comparable European publisher), run the two-axis check: does the pitch communicate identity (what is specific to this game) and maturity (do the financials reflect real platform economics)?
