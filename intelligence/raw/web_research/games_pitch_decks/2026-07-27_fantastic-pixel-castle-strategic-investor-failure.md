# Fantastic Pixel Castle -- Ex-WoW Lead, NetEase Wholly-Owned, Closed November 2025 (Two Years, Zero Shipped Games)

## Key Content

Fantastic Pixel Castle was established by NetEase in **November 2023** as a **wholly-owned internal studio** -- not an independently-incorporated company with a diversified investor base. NetEase funded 100% of the studio and held 100% of equity. The studio was led by Greg Street, former World of Warcraft lead systems designer and League of Legends design director, developing a triple-A MMORPG codenamed **Ghost**.

**Studio head:** Greg Street -- 20+ years in games; WoW lead systems designer; LoL design director (Riot Games)  
**Parent/sole investor:** NetEase (Chinese gaming conglomerate)  
**Project:** Ghost -- triple-A MMORPG in pre-production/early development  
**Formation:** November 2023  
**Closure:** November 17, 2025 -- **less than two years after formation, no game shipped**

### How the Closure Happened

NetEase changed its global strategy in **late 2024** and began systematically withdrawing from international studio investments. The stated reasons combined financial and geopolitical rationale: reducing costs, shortening development cycles, and mitigating risk from US-China policy changes. NetEase concluded Ghost was not financially viable under the new strategy.

Greg Street's public statement: "Very few deals are getting made for any game, let alone something on the scale of an MMORPG." He framed the closure as the result of a market problem, not a quality or execution problem.

NetEase formally "supported Fantastic Pixel Castle in its efforts to find publishing and investment partners" -- language indicating the studio could survive as an independent entity if it secured alternative capital before the closure date. It could not.

Street acknowledged the possibility of a "Fantastic Pixel Castle 2.0" as an independent studio if the team remained intact and secured subsequent funding. No announcement of this had been made by mid-2026.

### NetEase's Pattern: 12+ Studio Closures in 18 Months

Fantastic Pixel Castle was not an isolated case. In an 18-month period from November 2024 to mid-2026, NetEase closed or effectively closed:
- **Worlds Untold** (Mac Walters, Mass Effect writer -- paused November 2024)
- **Jar of Sparks** (paused January 2025)
- **T-Minus Zero Entertainment** (Rich Vogel, Star Wars: The Old Republic producer -- closed 2025)
- **Fantastic Pixel Castle** (Greg Street -- closed November 17, 2025)
- **Nagoshi Studio** (Toshihiro Nagoshi, Yakuza creator -- closed June 2026)
- And more: reports indicated "more than a dozen" studios potentially affected

Each of these studios was led by a recognisable AAA industry veteran and had been announced as part of NetEase's international expansion strategy. All closed without releasing a game.

## Decisions / Insights

Fantastic Pixel Castle is the clearest available case study for the most dangerous formation-stage decision: **accepting wholly-owned funding from a single strategic corporate investor**.

The pattern is structurally different from accepting a lead VC investment with co-investors. Key distinctions:

**Single investor = single point of failure.** When NetEase's strategy changed, there was no syndicate to buy out NetEase's stake, bridge the studio, or continue operations. A diversified cap table (two or more institutional investors) would have provided structural redundancy.

**Wholly-owned = no independent governance.** As a wholly-owned NetEase studio, Fantastic Pixel Castle had no independent board, no investor protection clauses, and no capital raise rights. When NetEase decided to close, the studio had no mechanism to resist or delay.

**Strategic investor risk compounds with geopolitical risk.** NetEase is a Chinese conglomerate operating in an environment of US-China policy tension. The studios it closed were predominantly US/Western operations. This is a form of risk that does not appear in a standard VC term sheet analysis but is directly analogous to the foreign sovereign fund concentration risk seen in Q1 2026 gaming M&A data (Savvy Games Group accounting for $7B+ of the $7.7B Q1 total).

**The investor's exit is a development dependency.** VC investors (Lightspeed, a16z, Griffin) exit at liquidity events (acquisition, IPO, secondary). Strategic corporate investors (NetEase, Tencent, Sony) may also exit when their parent company's strategy changes -- and that change can happen faster and less predictably than a VC's exit horizon.

The contrast with The Believer Company is instructive: Believer's $55M Series A syndicate included Lightspeed, a16z, BITKRAFT, Riot Games, and three individual investors. No single investor held more than a minority stake. A change of strategy by Riot Games as a co-investor would not have closed the studio.

## Context

Greg Street's pedigree was comparable to the alumnus profiles in the Believer Company and emptyvessel extracts. He had founding-generation-equivalent credentials at both Blizzard (WoW) and Riot (LoL). By normal alumni-pedigree analysis, his formation raise should have been structurally solid.

The failure occurred not because of the pitch narrative or founder credentials but because of the **capital structure choice**: a single parent company instead of an independent studio with a diversified investor base.

The Ghost MMO may or may not survive as an independent project. The Fantastic Pixel Castle team's dissolution represents a significant talent dispersal -- potential for alumni formation pitches in 2026-2027 from this network.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** This extract is the primary counter-argument to use when a client asks whether to accept a fully-funded wholly-owned structure from a single corporate investor (publisher, conglomerate, or strategic partner). The answer is: not without a detailed analysis of geopolitical exposure, the investor's capital allocation stability, and whether an independent governance structure can be negotiated.

The specific question to ask: "If this investor's strategy changes in 18-24 months, what is our fallback?" If the answer is nothing, the structure is too exposed.
