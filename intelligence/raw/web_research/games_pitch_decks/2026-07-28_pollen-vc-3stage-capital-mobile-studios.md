# Pollen VC 3-Stage Capital Model -- Mobile Game Studio Funding Stack (2026)

## Key Content

Martin Macmillan, writing for Gamigion, sets out a three-stage capital model that maps the full mobile game studio funding journey from idea to UA scaling. The model is notable for naming explicit entry thresholds per stage, including hard metric gates that define when a studio is ready for each capital type.

### Stage 1 -- Seed Capital (Discovery Equity)

- **Fund profile:** $25-50M total fund, 50-100 investments, gaming-expert operators
- **Check size:** $500K initial checks
- **What they want:** Soft launch completed, retention proof-of-concept
- **Type:** Dilutive equity
- **Goal:** Reach soft launch and demonstrate initial retention signal

### Stage 2 -- Grey Zone Capital (Build-Out Convertible Debt)

**Entry criteria (both must be met):**
- Day 30 retention: **8-10%**
- Revenue: **$50K/month minimum**

**Terms:**
- Facility size: $1-3M, deployed in tranches
- Interest: 12-15% PIK (payment in kind, compounding)
- Equity component: ~5% warrant or conversion optionality
- Senior liquidation preference for downside protection

**Why "Grey Zone":** Macmillan describes this as the biggest structural gap in gaming finance. Studios with early signal (retention data, initial revenue) are past seed risk but are not yet at VC-scale unit economics. Traditional VCs cannot price this stage. The Grey Zone capital addresses the gap.

### Stage 3 -- UA Scaling Debt (Proven Economics)

- **Entry criteria:** LTV > CAC proven, cohort economics measurable
- **Structure:** Cohort financing (longer payback cycle) or accounts receivable factoring (shorter payback cycle)
- **Type:** Non-dilutive UA fuel
- **Goal:** Scale user acquisition once unit economics are validated

### Illustrative Funding Path

- Year 0-1: $500K seed capital → soft launch → $50K MRR
- Year 1-2: $1.5M Grey Zone → build-out phase → $300K MRR
- Year 2-3: Cohort debt → scale UA → multi-million revenue

## Decisions / Insights

The Day 30 retention threshold (8-10%) and $50K/month revenue minimum are the most operationally specific gates in this research set. They define exactly where a studio sits on the capital stack and what it must achieve to unlock the next stage of funding.

The Grey Zone framing is useful for NBI client conversations: it explains why studios with genuine early traction (some retention, some revenue) still cannot access institutional VC. The capital instrument they need -- convertible debt with warrant component -- exists but is not commonly known among indie studio operators.

The 12-15% PIK coupon is expensive capital. A studio should understand that $1.5M Grey Zone at 12-15% PIK is meaningfully dilutive over a 12-18 month period if the game does not accelerate fast enough to repay. The upside: it is cheaper dilution than giving up equity at a low valuation at this stage.

## Context

Pollen VC is a revenue-based financing firm specifically for mobile game studios. Macmillan's framing represents both the capital model Pollen VC deploys and his diagnostic for where studios are in their maturity. The article does not name specific Pollen VC portfolio companies.

The three-stage model assumes a studio is building a mobile game with ad monetisation and/or IAP that generates measurable day 30 retention. It does not apply to premium mobile, PC/console, or subscription-model games.
