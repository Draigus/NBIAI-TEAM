# Achren -- "Medium Game First, Big Game Second" -- Mobile F2P Series A Framework (June 2025)

## Key Content

Joakim Achren, writing in the Elite Game Developers Substack (June 6, 2025), articulates the dominant mobile VC pitch framework for studios seeking Series A: **launch a profitable hybrid-casual or arcade title first, prove execution, then pitch the ambitious game.**

### The Framework

**Phase 1 -- Medium Game (Pre-Seed, $2-4M, 4-6 months):**
Build and ship a hybrid-casual or arcade game quickly. The goal is not the game itself but the proof it generates.

**What to demonstrate before approaching Series A:**
- The team ships
- The game generates "tens of thousands in daily revenue" through aggressive UA scaling
- The business provides downside protection: meaningful revenue continues while the studio pursues the larger opportunity

**Phase 2 -- Series A Pitch (the ambitious game):**
The pitch for the bigger, riskier game becomes credible only because the medium game exists. The argument is: "We can execute (proven), we have revenue continuity (de-risked), now we are going after a game with billion-dollar potential."

### Why This Structure Works for VCs

Achren frames the appeal explicitly as a **risk/reward balance**. The medium game provides downside protection (the VC does not lose everything if the ambitious game fails) while preserving upside optionality. The target outcome for the ambitious second game is "over $100 million annually." The implied Series A valuation ceiling via the portfolio approach: $50-80M.

### The Most Common Pitch Failure

Studios that pitch a single ambitious game without any shipped product face **bridge round dependency**: they cannot demonstrate execution, so each funding round requires more thesis conviction from the investor, not less. Each bridge round dilutes founders without the corresponding proof that reduces risk.

## Decisions / Insights

The "medium game first" framework functions as a pre-Series-A readiness diagnostic:
- Has the studio shipped a live product? If no: not Series A ready.
- Does that product generate meaningful daily revenue through UA? If no: the business case for the next round has no foundation.
- Is the team proposing to raise for the medium game or the ambitious game? If the medium game has not shipped, they are raising for the wrong thing.

The $2-4M pre-seed range is the budget envelope for the medium game. This is the fundraise that NBI clients can actually approach before they need institutional Series A capital.

## Context

The Achren framework appeared in June 2025 and describes conditions as of that date. The hybrid-casual / arcade category is the current genre context: these are the genres where a small team can reach "tens of thousands in daily revenue" within the 4-6 month window Achren describes.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** Pitch readiness gate for any mobile studio client. Before advising a client to approach Series A investors, run the diagnostic: have they shipped a medium game and does it generate daily revenue? If not, the first advisory conversation is about Phase 1 capital ($2-4M pre-seed), not Series A.
