# Raw Fury -- Published Pitch Requirements + Public Contract -- PC/Console Indie Publisher Submission Framework

## Key Content

Raw Fury (Swedish indie publisher; portfolio includes Norco, Dome Keeper, Kingdom series, Citizen Sleeper, Cassette Beasts) has published their full pitch requirements and made their publishing agreement publicly available -- a rare degree of transparency from a top-tier PC/console indie publisher.

**Four submission requirements:**

1. **Playable PC build.** Must capture the game's essence in a way outsiders can understand. Practical requirements for the build: skippable cutscenes if playtime exceeds 10 minutes; loadable checkpoints so the publisher can jump to different sections; English subtitles if the game is not in English; documentation of the control scheme. Raw Fury commits to playing it once -- the build needs to make the experience clear on first contact.

2. **Budget.** Defined as burn rate (USD) multiplied by remaining months of development, covering only the cost to ship one complete PC build. Marketing, porting, localisation, and PR are explicitly excluded. This figure is development-only.

3. **Pitch deck.** Must answer six fundamental questions. Raw Fury's stated questions: "What are you doing, Why are you doing it, Who are you, Where do you want to go, What do you need to get there, When will you be there?" The deck can literally be six slides, one per question. No prescribed length beyond covering the six points.

4. **Supplementary materials.** GIFs, videos, design documents, concept art, story materials -- anything that adds clarity to the vision. Optional but welcome.

**Key contract terms (publicly available in 9 languages including English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, French, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Korean, Croatian):**

- Raw Fury explicitly excludes internal staff time from recoupable development costs: "we also don't include internal work hours from our team into these budgets (our work is our risk to carry, only stuff that comes with an invoice from an external party goes into these budgets)." This is a material distinction from standard publishing agreements, where publishers often mark up internal work as developer-side recoupable expenses.
- Any developer in active contract talks with Raw Fury receives a $500 USD budget to hire a lawyer of their choosing to review the agreement. This provision is unconditional -- Raw Fury pays, the developer chooses the lawyer, no strings attached.
- The full publishing agreement is available publicly on Raw Fury's developer resources page -- accessible to any developer regardless of whether they are in a pitch process.

## Decisions / Insights

- The six-question framework ("What, Why, Who, Where, What, When") is the most minimal credible deck structure on record in this bank. Decks that fail to answer these six in plain language fail at the first filter, regardless of polish.
- Build-first submission changes the pitch dynamic: the game speaks before the founder does. Cold outreach without a playable build is structurally filtered before first contact at Raw Fury.
- The $500 legal budget provision is a trust signal, not a significant financial commitment for a publisher. Developers who don't know to ask for legal review time are disadvantaged; Raw Fury removes this structural asymmetry by default.
- Raw Fury's internal-costs-absorbed model means the recoupable budget is genuinely development-only -- a benchmark for evaluating other publishers' contract terms. Standard publisher agreements can mark up all internal coordination, marketing-adjacent work, and platform relations as developer-side costs.

## Context

Raw Fury publishes approximately 5-10 games per year with a focus on PC-first premium titles. Their portfolio tilts toward unusual genre hybrids and narrative-rich games. Unlike Focus Entertainment or Devolver Digital, they publish the deal terms openly; the published agreement is the same agreement presented in actual negotiations. The $500 legal budget has been in place since at least 2021, when the contract was first published publicly. Developer resources page also includes marketing templates and sales projection templates.
