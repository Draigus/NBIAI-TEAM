# Grand Games (Istanbul) -- $70M Series B, Balderton -- Hybrid Casual Puzzle, Moon Active Alumni (May 2026)

## Key Content

Grand Games (Istanbul) raised a **$70M Series B** led by Balderton Capital Growth Fund, with Bek Ventures and Laton Ventures as co-investors, announced May 2026. Total funding to date is **$103M** across seed, Series A (undisclosed amount, January 2025), and Series B.

The studio was founded in early 2024 by alumni who had previously built **Zen Match** at Moon Active. This team provenance is the most explicit pitch signal in the publicly available information.

### Metrics at Series A (January 2025)

Two games -- Magic Sort! and Car Match -- were generating **combined gross app revenue of $4M per month**. Magic Sort! had broken into **Apple's top casual-gaming charts within six months** of release.

The Series A triggered Balderton's initial conviction. The $4M/month combined figure and the chart presence of a single title within 6 months of release are the evidenced thresholds.

### Series B Rationale (May 2026)

Balderton doubled down: the Series B represents Balderton continuing to lead (seed through Series B). The stated use of funds is conventional -- hiring, additional title development, and UA spend in high-leverage mobile channels.

The studio is working on titles in the pouring-puzzle and car-customisation genres (hybrid-casual meta + puzzle core).

### What the Pitch Narrative Signals

From the available investor commentary, the pitch pillars are reconstructable as:

1. **Team provenance:** Moon Active is a top-tier hyper-casual and hybrid-casual studio (Zen Match is one of its marquee titles). Alumni with demonstrated hits at a proven studio reduce execution risk in VC diligence.

2. **Multi-title performance:** At Series A, two different titles were both generating meaningful revenue. Balderton was not buying conviction in one game -- they were buying the studio's ability to repeat.

3. **Chart position as proxy for organic traction:** Magic Sort! reaching Apple's top casual-gaming charts within 6 months is a metric that demonstrates the game was not purely UA-driven -- some organic component was present.

4. **Balderton continuity:** The fact that Balderton led from initial investment through Series B suggests the studio matched or exceeded internal growth assumptions at each stage.

## Decisions / Insights

The Grand Games case confirms two pitch patterns:

**Pattern 1 -- Multi-hit proof before Series A.** The studio was not pitching a single game. At Series A, two titles were live and generating revenue. This mirrors the Achren framework (medium game first) but executed simultaneously by a team with prior industry pedigree.

**Pattern 2 -- Team provenance as the seed-stage unlock.** Moon Active alumni building in the same genre (puzzle/casual) is an investor signal that the team has direct operational experience with the exact audience and monetisation model they are pitching. This is not general gaming experience -- it is genre-specific execution proof.

The Istanbul / Turkey gaming ecosystem is a recurring pattern in the data: Grand Games, Dream Games (Royal Match), Rollic, and Gram Games are all Istanbul-based studios that have raised significant capital in 2022-2026. The talent concentration from Peak Games (acquired by Zynga for $1.8B, 2020) and Moon Active created a dense network of operators with exit pedigree. For a pitch, Istanbul team lineage functions like Y Combinator alumni status -- it signals access to a network and operational context that VCs value.

## Context

Grand Games is a hybrid-casual puzzle studio, ~2 years old at Series B. The scale ($103M total) is above NBI's typical client range, but the formation narrative -- Moon Active alumni team, two-title strategy, chart evidence before Series A -- is directly applicable to smaller studios pitching to mobile VCs at seed and Series A stage.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** The multi-title-before-Series-A pattern is actionable advice: a mobile studio client pitching with only one live title is weaker than one pitching with two. If a client has a second title even at soft launch, it changes the pitch from "we have a game" to "we have a studio that makes games."

The Moon Active / Istanbul alumni framing is also instructive: clients should explicitly anchor team bios to prior studio hit titles (not just studios), with download/revenue context where possible.
