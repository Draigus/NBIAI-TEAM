# Double Fine Productions + Compulsion Games -- Management-Led Independence From Xbox -- PC/Console AA Studios -- Investment Received -- July 2026

## Key Content

In July 2026, Microsoft's Xbox division announced a major restructure involving approximately 3,200 job losses across the fiscal year. As part of this restructure, two first-party game studios -- Double Fine Productions (San Francisco; Tim Schafer, studio head) and Compulsion Games (Montreal; Guillaume Provost, studio head) -- returned to independent status under their founding leadership rather than being closed or sold to third parties.

**Deal structure (as publicly confirmed):**

- Both studios returned to independence under their existing founding leaders (Schafer for Double Fine, Provost for Compulsion).
- Full IP retention: both studios retain their complete back catalogues, including titles developed during the Microsoft acquisition period. Double Fine's catalogue includes Psychonauts 2 (2021) and the Amnesia Fortnight prototypes. Compulsion's catalogue includes We Happy Few (2018), Contrast (2013), and South of Midnight (2024).
- Future catalog revenue: both studios retain ongoing revenue from their existing game catalogues going forward.
- Investment received: both studios received investment as part of the independence arrangement (amounts not publicly disclosed).
- Double Fine's Schafer had explored a management-led buyout when closure discussions began -- this outcome represents the successful conclusion of those negotiations rather than a forced sale.

**Broader Xbox divestiture context (same restructure):**

- Ninja Theory (Cambridge; Senua's Saga: Hellblade II): sold to a new owner (undisclosed); Microsoft funds completion and growth of their in-development title.
- Undead Labs (Seattle; State of Decay series): same structure as Ninja Theory -- new owner, Microsoft-funded title completion.
- Arkane Lyon (Paris; Blade project): under strategic review, outcome pending (French labour law consultation required).

## Decisions / Insights

- Double Fine and Compulsion re-enter the independent pitch market in mid-2026 with an unusually strong starting position: established studio identity, retained IP and catalogue revenue, bridge investment already secured, and founding leadership in place. Their pitch to future investors or publishing partners is structurally different from greenfield studio formation -- this is a "studio restart" with decades of track record and ongoing revenue.
- The management-led buyout mechanism (MBO) as a studio independence path is now documented in the gaming sector. MBOs are standard in private equity but rare in game development. Schafer's success sets a precedent: studio heads can negotiate for studio IP and independence rather than accepting closure or forced sale terms.
- Catalogue revenue as a negotiating lever: studios with owned IP generating ongoing Steam/console revenue have an asset that generalist buyers undervalue. Retaining catalogue revenue changes the pitch narrative from "we need funding to survive" to "we have a revenue base and we need funding to grow."
- Platform holders divesting studios creates a documented supply of experienced, IP-retaining, mid-size studios entering the independent market. This may compress publisher advance expectations for publishers who previously competed for exclusive access to these studios.

## Context

Double Fine was acquired by Microsoft in 2019 for an undisclosed sum. Compulsion Games was acquired in 2018. Both operated as Xbox Game Studios first-party studios until the 2026 restructure. The Xbox "reset" was triggered by declining Xbox revenue against a $20 billion investment base, with new Xbox CEO Asha Sharma overseeing the restructure.

As of July 2026, both studios are independently seeking their next development partners, publishers, or investors for new titles. This is the first major documented instance since the 2021 acquisition wave of established AA PC/console studios re-entering the indie pitch market from a position of track record, retained IP, and pre-existing revenue.
