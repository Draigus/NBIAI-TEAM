# TaleMonster Games -- Hybrid Casual Mobile (Match Valley) -- Seed + Series A -- Raised $37M total from a16z + Arcadia Gaming Partners

## Key Content

TaleMonster Games was founded in Istanbul in 2024 by five former Peak Games colleagues. Peak Games was the Turkish mobile studio behind Toon Blast and Toy Blast -- two of the most downloaded casual puzzle games globally -- acquired by Zynga for $1.8B in 2020. The TaleMonster founding team held senior product and engineering roles at Peak before and through that exit.

Their debut title Match Valley combines match-3 puzzle mechanics with hero and tower defence elements -- a hybrid casual format that adds progression depth without sacrificing the accessibility of casual puzzle. The game launched globally in autumn 2024. At the seed round (approximately May 2025, institutional investors including Point72 Ventures and General Catalyst), the headline engagement metric was 52-minute average daily playtime alongside a 50% D1 retention rate.

By the Series A (January 2026, co-led by Arcadia Gaming Partners and Andreessen Horowitz), average daily playtime had grown to 100 minutes -- nearly double the seed-stage figure. The round also included Point72 Ventures and General Catalyst (seed investors following on). The $7M seed and $30M Series A bring total raised to $37M.

Arcadia's Akin Babayigit (former Toon Blast product leader, directly familiar with the founding team's prior work) stated: "Companies at this stage typically excel in one or two metrics. TaleMonster demonstrates strength across several -- metrics I've never seen in 15 years of gaming. The founders achieved their stated goal of combining strong monetisation with durable retention."

Josh Lu (a16z Games) stated: "Most teams in this category follow the same playbook. TaleMonster chose a different path with real courage, building an original product and culture unafraid to challenge industry conventions."

The pitch thesis was a direct critique of the casual market's optimisation culture: casual games have become overly optimised and uninspiring, mobile players who have been gaming for years now seek depth, and the market is not supplying it. The founders positioned Match Valley as "evergreen" -- a title designed to evolve with players rather than chase viral installs. The tower defence layer provides persistent progression that match-3 alone cannot deliver, and the team argue this is the structural reason their engagement metrics outlast standard casual titles.

How a16z made contact: Josh Lu had posted publicly on LinkedIn about his interest in new game concepts. TaleMonster's CEO sent a direct message referencing Lu's stated investment interest. After reviewing the prototype, a16z joined the seed round. This cold-to-warm outreach via a VC's published thesis is documented and replicable.

## Decisions / Insights

- Simultaneous strength across monetisation and retention is the differentiator Arcadia named explicitly. Most early-stage studios show strength in one metric; multi-metric outperformance is what moved Arcadia from interested to leading.
- 100-minute average daily playtime versus casual genre norms of 15-20 minutes is not a projection -- it was a live measurement at the Series A stage. The pitch was metrics-led, not thesis-led.
- The category-critique pitch ("casual gaming is broken") landed because the founders had category credentials (Peak Games / Toon Blast / Toy Blast). Without that pedigree, the critique would read as a first-timer's complaint rather than a practitioner's diagnosis.
- Cold LinkedIn outreach citing a VC's published thesis is documented to work with a16z specifically. Researching what the investor has publicly said they want, then connecting the studio's product to that stated interest, is more effective than a warm intro in the right context.

## Context

TaleMonster is actively scaling Match Valley globally as of January 2026 with Series A capital. A second title is in development. The studio is Istanbul-based with 32 employees at the Series A. The Turkish mobile ecosystem (Peak Games, Dream Games, Good Job Games, Grand Games, Bigger Games) has produced a cluster of well-funded studios from overlapping alumni networks, improving institutional investor confidence in Turkish-pedigreed founding teams specifically.
