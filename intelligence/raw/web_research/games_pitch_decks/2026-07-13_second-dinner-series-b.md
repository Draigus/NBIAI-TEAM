# Second Dinner -- Mobile CCG (Marvel Snap) -- Series A + Series B -- Raised $130M total from NetEase + Griffin Gaming Partners

## Key Content

Second Dinner was founded in 2018 by Ben Brode (former Game Director, Hearthstone at Blizzard), Hamilton Chu (former Executive Producer, Hearthstone), and three other Blizzard veterans. The studio's initial pitch to NetEase in January 2019 secured a $30M Series A minority investment and a Marvel IP licence. The pitch at Series A was pure pedigree: the Hearthstone team building a mobile card game with Marvel's IP, in a genre the founders defined at commercial scale. NetEase provided both capital and a Chinese distribution relationship for a future global title.

The studio launched Marvel Snap in October 2022 on mobile and PC. The game reached 22M+ downloads and $200M+ in cumulative IAP revenue within its first year. Monetisation was structurally distinct from genre competitors: no card packs, no pay-to-win card acquisition. Revenue derived from Battle Pass, cosmetics, and card variants only. This was a deliberate design and investment thesis -- Brode has stated publicly that Hearthstone's card pack model felt exploitative and he built Marvel Snap's cosmetic-only monetisation as something he personally wanted to spend money on. The result was a high-DAU, high-retention model that prioritised long-term player engagement over short-term extraction.

The $100M Series B round was announced January 2024, led by Griffin Gaming Partners as a new minority investor with NetEase retaining its position. Griffin Managing Director Nick Tuosto stated: "Second Dinner has assembled one of the strongest creative teams that we have ever come across. MARVEL SNAP adds to the management team's incredible track record of creating hit titles, who previously led the creation of Hearthstone." The investment rationale explicitly named pedigree-to-output: the team that built Hearthstone built a mobile hit, proving execution on a new platform.

Use of Series B funds: expand live operations on Marvel Snap, develop new IP titles, incubate original properties. The studio employs approximately 100 people as of mid-2026, with a second unannounced F2P title in development.

The design origin of the mobile format is documented: Brode became a father in 2014 and could no longer play multi-hour Hearthstone sessions. He studied Clash Royale's 5-minute match format as a model. The resulting design decisions -- 12-card decks, 6-turn games, 3-minute matches, one card type, location randomness -- emerged from a specific personal founder-market fit insight: build a complete game experience for people who no longer have hours to play.

## Decisions / Insights

- Pedigree pitch works at Series A when the founding team has a proven hit in an adjacent format. Blizzard veterans pitching a mobile card game in 2019 required no prototype: the team's track record was the product.
- Differentiated monetisation is an investable thesis. Cosmetic-only in a genre dominated by card pack revenue was a deliberate positioning statement, not just a design choice. Investors heard "we will win on retention where category incumbents win on extraction."
- Griffin's Series B rationale cited "track record" explicitly alongside live game metrics. For Series B rounds, investor attention shifts to team credibility as primary value driver, not game metrics alone.
- Authentic founder-market fit stories are more evaluable than demographic TAM arguments. The "why mobile" narrative was personal and specific (Brode became his own target user post-fatherhood).

## Context

Second Dinner is an active live-service studio as of mid-2026. Marvel Snap continues in live service. A second title is in active development under Series B funding. The studio is approximately 100 employees, based in Irvine, California.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** Use this case when advising a studio with identifiable AAA pedigree building a mobile game in a proven genre. The Second Dinner story is the canonical pedigree-forward pitching case: the pitch is "the team that made X is building Y." It is also the clearest example of monetisation philosophy as investment narrative -- articulating not just how you make money but why your approach is better for players and why that produces superior long-term LTV.

**Applicable when:** NBI client is an ex-Activision, ex-EA, ex-Ubisoft, or ex-King senior leader founding a mobile F2P studio; or any studio that has a demonstrable design rationale for why their monetisation model outperforms incumbent approaches. Also directly applicable when client asks "can we raise without a prototype?" -- the answer is yes if pedigree + IP partner combination is strong enough.
