# Griffin Gaming Partners Special Opportunities Fund -- $100M Revenue-Based Indie Financing, May 2026

## Key Content

Griffin Gaming Partners (GGP, $1.5B AUM) launched the **Special Opportunities Fund** on 6 May 2026, a $100M project-based financing vehicle providing capital to indie and double-A game developers in exchange for a **share of the game's revenue** rather than equity.

**Fund size:** $100 million  
**Investment model:** Revenue-based financing -- percentage of game revenue, not equity  
**Check sizes:** Hundreds of thousands to several million dollars per deal  
**Portfolio to date:** 15 projects committed at launch

### Investment Model Mechanics

This is not VC equity. GGP co-founder Nick Tuosto: "We get paid when the developer gets paid." The fund is designed specifically for studios that are not equipped for or interested in traditional equity fundraising -- studios that want capital to finish or scale a game without giving up ownership.

The revenue share structure means:
- No dilution of founder equity
- No board seat or investor control rights
- Repayment tied to commercial performance rather than a fixed schedule
- GGP aligned with developer success rather than exit valuation

### Studio and Game Criteria

Target developers are **indie and double-A studios** -- "relatively small teams" with "extraordinary vision for product." This is specifically **not** formation-stage financing: GGP evaluates actual game content, not vision pitches. Criteria include:

- **Working demo or prototype** that generates Steam wishlist traction and gameplay video engagement
- **Steam-first distribution** -- all 15 current portfolio titles target Steam as primary platform
- **Core market genres:** turn-based RPGs, survival horror, extraction games, grand strategy, physics-based action. No VR, no mobile-exclusive, no social/casual
- **Transmedia franchise potential** as a selection accelerator: GGP has assembled a transmedia advisory board (film/TV producer Dylan Clark, brand steward Russell Binder of Five Nights at Freddy's / Dead by Daylight) to develop IP into film, TV, and merchandise alongside the game release

### Discovery Mechanism

GGP sources portfolio games organically via **Steam wishlists and gameplay videos** rather than pitch submissions. Tuosto: "Steam's extraordinarily robust, organic discovery" allows assessment of whether "people feel passionate about this vision" before any capital conversation. A studio does not need to know GGP to be considered -- organic Steam traction surfaces them.

### Named Portfolio Studios

- **Overhype Studios** (Battle Brothers developer) -- next project *Menace*
- **Darkwood 2** sequel
- Additional projects: *Kinstrife*, *Highland Keep*, *Expedition: Into Darkness*
- Six further projects undisclosed at launch

## Decisions / Insights

The Special Opportunities Fund creates a new instrument in the game financing stack between traditional VC equity (Lightspeed, a16z) and publisher advances (Raw Fury, Focus Entertainment). Its specific niche is studios with working content that want to scale without dilution:

- Too large for bootstrapping (needs $500K-$3M to finish)
- Too small for VC (pre-revenue, pre-launch, unclear franchise potential)
- Reluctant to give publishers marketing control or revenue share at the backend

The revenue-share structure solves the "indie funding dead zone" ($500K-$3M) identified in prior bank research (Little Polygon postmortem extract) without the control implications of publisher deals.

The Steam-first, core market focus means this fund is irrelevant for mobile, social, or casual studios -- GGP is explicitly not deploying here. The transmedia angle (film/TV) is a genuine selection criterion, not marketing language: GGP has assembled a professional transmedia infrastructure to act on it.

The organic discovery mechanism (Steam traction → GGP outreach) inverts the normal pitch dynamic: studios with strong Steam wishlists may receive inbound interest from GGP without ever pitching. Building Steam wishlist traction before seeking financing is therefore a dual-purpose activity.

## Context

GGP is one of the largest gaming-specialist VCs ($1.5B AUM). The Special Opportunities Fund is a separate vehicle from their main venture fund, specifically designed for non-equity project financing. Their other portfolio includes infrastructure and platform companies alongside content studios.

The May 2026 launch makes this the most recent financing innovation in the bank.

## Applicability

**For NBI:** Two applications. First, studios with working prototypes and Steam presence should be aware this fund exists and that organic Steam traction can trigger inbound interest. Second, for studios reluctant to give up equity at an early stage, this is a concrete non-dilutive instrument to include in the financing menu when advising on capital structure.
