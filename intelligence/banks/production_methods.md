# Production Methods

## Executive Summary

This bank covers how game studios in the 10-100 person range organise production: milestone frameworks, sprint cadence, live-ops scheduling, pre-production gates, org design, estimation methodology, remote communication, creative documentation, and meeting structures. Primary evidence is a deep NBI engagement with a ~55-70 person remote MMO studio (April-July 2026), supplemented by published frameworks (Tim Cain, Rami Ismail, Supergiant, Ghost Ship) and NBI's consulting standards. The bank is strongest on the 40-70 person studio navigating the prototype-to-production transition; live ops cadence remains mobile-benchmark-heavy with limited PC/console primary data. The recurring finding across 131 extracts: studios almost universally believe they are further along in production than they are -- documentation gaps, not working code, define production maturity; and live service commercial models require explicit leadership alignment from day one. Key additions through 2026-07-06 include: forced art direction lock (forcing mechanism when chronic drift exists); VS proxy-to-finished ratio (~1:1.8, skunkworks icon building parallel track); adaptive FTU tutorial design (telemetry-driven prompts, investor vs player walkthrough distinction); concept art as support function not pipeline gatekeeper; named ownership "who has the ball" protocol; red/pink list framework for incoming studio leaders; UK contractor compliance common failure points; structured UE plugin evaluation methodology (3-day standalone process); plugin governance approval gate with manifest R/Y/G system; GDD-first feature pipeline sequence with R&D Confluence documentation requirement. ## By Team Scale

### 10-25 People

Informal communication handles most coordination at this scale. The risk: informal channels become load-bearing and fail past ~20. Supergiant Games (17 people) operated with a monthly cadence -- code-open, code-locked, polish, and ship phases per month. Scope control: the "ripple effect" test -- does this work cascade across departments? If yes, it waits. Unlimited PTO underperformed compared to mandatory minimum (20 days); invisible pressure accumulated without enforced floors. [source: web_2026-05-27_supergiant-hades-monthly-milestone-cadence]

Rami Ismail's LTPF is the most appropriate formal structure: Research/Prototyping, Pre-Production (Vertical Slice), Production (Feature Complete through Content Complete), Wrap-Up. Vertical Slice can consume up to one-quarter of total development time. Content Complete 8-12 weeks before launch (4 weeks absolute minimum). [source: web_2026-05-27_rami-ismail-ltpf-milestone-framework]

### 25-50 People

The transition zone where informal coordination fails. Ghost Ship (~40-54 people) resolved this by preserving low-ceremony coordination: information-sharing stand-ups (not task-assignment) and in-office presence. For remote studios this size, equivalent process investment is required. [source: web_2026-05-27_ghost-ship-open-development-live-ops]

Single-producer configurations become a serious risk at this scale. Remediation: an Executive Producer overseeing four discipline tracks (QA, Audio, Art, Design), each with an embedded producer. Agilefall becomes appropriate at 30+: gates provide approval checkpoints; sprints enable iterative delivery. [source: chatgpt_69034e5d, chatgpt_68fb7b4a]

**Comparative Scope Benchmarking (Eremite / Against the Storm):** Eremite Games (Against the Storm) benchmarked against studios of genuinely similar scale -- Lion Shield (Kingdoms and Castles) and others at comparable headcount and budget -- rather than against aspirational targets. Key insight from the studio: "There's no trick we can pull off, and no 'main character' advantage we have." Feature prioritisation criterion: value to experience vs time to implement. Non-negotiable 25% safety buffer built into all schedules. Steam Next Fest demo used as an external milestone gate -- the public commitment creates production pressure equivalent to a publisher deadline without ceding creative control. NBI advisory implication: studios frequently benchmark against aspirational comparables (large studios that succeeded) rather than peer comparables (studios of the same size and resource level); the Eremite model forces honest calibration. [source: web_2026-07-16_eremite-against-storm-scope-benchmarking-spreadsheet]

### 50-100 People

NBI's primary reference engagement is a ~55-70 person remote MMO studio. Key findings apply broadly.

**Production maturity is almost always lower than the team believes.** Art significantly ahead of core systems creates an "illusion of a full game." Working code without documentation is not production-ready. [source: granola_50612dd7, granola_5fdd8c18]

**Feature estimates by discipline (MMO-lite, calibration):** Player progression to MVP: 60d designer/20d eng/20d UI. Skill system: 90d designer/30d eng/25d UI. Partner portal: 60d engineering. New creature: 45d total. Capacity baseline: 20 workdays/month per person. [source: granola_ae650223, granola_5fdd8c18]

**Governance:** Creative Director signals intent > Design designs > Team feeds back > Game Director considers > three-party approval (Game Director + Executive Producer + Creative Director) before any commitment. Bypassed approval chains are the most common source of rework at this size. [source: slack_production-council_2026-05-25_process]

## By Working Model

### Fully Remote

Remote-first production requires more formal async communication. Three estimation spreadsheets maintained independently at a ~55-person remote studio required manual merge before structured sessions were introduced. Written decision records are load-bearing -- a running decisions log prevents re-litigation of approved work; without it, stealth branches proliferate. Anonymised feedback channels (per-department Google Forms) surface negative sentiment before escalation. No-meeting days (one per week) protect deep work. [source: chatgpt_69034e5d, granola_080a19f8]

Chain-of-command bypass is a critical remote failure mode. Slack messages are discoverable via DSAR in UK employment contexts -- informal HR commentary is fully retrievable in an employment dispute. HoDs require explicit briefing that Slack is not a private communication tool. [source: not_4nWBkRC4r7TVRQ_dsar]

**Remote communication patterns (battle-tested):** 7-message rule -- the 8th message triggers a Zoom, automatically. Dual-layer mood tracking: team health and project confidence as separate signals. Two-standup model for distributed teams: two time-zone-spanning standups, each with 10-15 minutes of open chat before agenda. Binary retrospective: bi-weekly, yes/no responses only, groups of 3-5 to prevent groupthink. [source: not_ZLLEyCfuFCgGaT]

**Junior hire support in remote studios:** Ambient learning is absent remotely -- osmosis, overhearing, and watching seniors work all fail. Every learning pathway that happens organically in an office must be engineered deliberately. Minimum viable support per junior hire: dedicated scheduled training hours/week + separate mentoring hours (1:1 with a senior) + senior buddy for ad hoc questions + director check-ins every 3 weeks. Target team shape: ~3 veterans, 2 mids, 1 junior. [source: 2026-06-22_junior-hire-policy-remote-studio]

**Remote production as documentation infrastructure problem (Rob Sandberg #26):** The core constraint of remote game development is not timezone coverage or tool access -- it is documentation infrastructure. Sync vs async decision criteria: sync is reserved for debate, emotional attunement, and relationship-building; async is used for status updates and single-decision records. Decision record format: what was decided / why / who owns it / what alternatives were considered -- approximately 10 minutes to write post-meeting; longer records are not read. Timezone burden is actively rotated rather than defaulted to the majority timezone; fixed "burdenless" timezones for one group entrench inequality and degrade remote culture. Studios that maintained distributed-work practices after a return-to-office showed improved "project memory" -- the documentation discipline built for remote work proved more durable than face-to-face institutional memory, which disappears when staff turn over. NBI advisory implication: remote production improvements are almost always documentation infrastructure problems in disguise; before proposing new tooling, audit whether decisions are being recorded with why, who, and alternatives -- not just what. [source: web_2026-07-16_production-alchemist-remote-async-documentation-infrastructure]

### Hybrid and Co-located

Ghost Ship's model (in-office with WFH Fridays and two additional WFH days/month) preserves cross-department communication density their "develop by doing" methodology requires. Not portable to remote without structural compensators (written briefs, decision logs, structured estimation). [source: web_2026-05-27_ghost-ship-open-development-live-ops]

**Motion Twin flat co-op ceiling:** Motion Twin deliberately kept below 15, with equal base salary and full-team consensus on strategic decisions. Dead Cells shipped after 18 months early access (planned 12). The flat model has a documented upper ceiling of approximately 10-15 people; beyond that, consensus-based decision-making degrades production velocity faster than cultural benefits compensate. When Dead Cells handed off to Evil Empire (70+ people, traditional hierarchy), structured organisation generated approximately 9x more commercial volume on the same IP. [source: web_2026-06-24_motion-twin-flat-coop-production-ceiling]

**Cross-studio co-location game jam model:** Aggro Crab (~10 people) and Landfall (~25-30 people) developed co-op climbing game Peak via a one-month retreat in Seoul (February 2025) with 7 developers total, budget under $200K. Shipped within 5 days of announcement; 2 million sales. Scope mechanism: the fixed delivery date was the only scope management tool needed -- "the game jam IS the game." Whatever existed at end of the month would ship; post-jam work was pre-scoped to UX polish and meta-progression only, removing the most common scope extension vector. Key lesson from pre-production async communication: "text is evil" -- creative direction disputes resolved faster in person or via synchronous voice than through async text; pre-production Discord discussions created the same misalignments that co-location resolved within hours. Cross-studio co-location eliminates collaboration overhead: scheduling delays, status updates, and alignment documentation reduce to near-zero when both teams share physical space. Landfall used the same methodology for Content Warning (2024), providing evidence this is repeatable. NBI advisory implication: creative coordination requires synchronous channels even in otherwise async distributed teams. [source: web_2026-07-08_peak-aggrocrab-landfall-colocation-game-jam]

## Sprint / Cycle Length Evidence

**Monthly cadence (Supergiant, 17 people):** Public Early Access commitment forced production discipline that internal deadlines alone did not. [source: web_2026-05-27_supergiant-hades-monthly-milestone-cadence]

**Two-horizon planning (Agilefall / 30+ people):** Quarterly PI planning (8-12 weeks) defines roadmap; sprint planning (1-2 weeks) handles execution. Weekly ceremony overhead: daily standup 15 min, backlog refinement 60-90 min, sprint planning 2-4h, sprint review 60-120 min, retrospective 60 min. [source: chatgpt_68fb7b4a]

**Estimation method (blind affinity planning):** Feature estimates use blind affinity planning with min/mid/max ranges, cross-validated by a second expert, with structured discussion for discrepancies greater than 5 days. Tooling (Jira) configured only after structure and estimates are confirmed. [source: granola_8b912e8e]

**Min+20% corrective for max-padded estimates:** When estimates are systematically padded to maximum, take the minimum realistic estimate and add 20%. Max-based planning is a credibility failure: experienced leads recognise inflated numbers and stop trusting the exercise. Wide min/max gaps are documentation gaps, not estimation errors -- ask the estimator to narrate what fills the space. A 2-day to 400-day gap means the estimate set is worthless and requires facilitated re-estimation. Plans must explicitly distinguish prototype kits from production kits; prototype kits materially reduce downstream estimates. [source: not_zBxoXexM2abxz9, not_Vn1AdPFNDQgWTj]

**Engineering is always the long pole.** In every NBI estimation engagement, engineering delivers estimates last and these are the largest relative to original assumptions. [source: slack_production-council_2026-05-25_process]

**Estimate inflation control -- "shenanigans" culture and scope capitulation prevention:** Estimates balloon when unchallenged -- one lead's vertical slice estimate went from 1,600 days to 800 days to approximately 3 months when challenged in sequence. Art estimates default to "fully shipped" quality rather than prototype tiers, inflating all downstream planning. Fix: codify tier language (e.g. tier 2/3/4) agreed across all disciplines so "done" means the same thing everywhere. "Shenanigans" adopted as the call-out word for inflated claims -- a non-confrontational signal that forces justification without personal conflict. [source: 2026-06-24_estimate-challenge-scope-discipline]

**Vertical slice staffing model -- efficiency ratings and estimation calibration:** A ~65-person studio built a staffing model for a VS milestone. Model structure: hours per role ÷ 20 (working days/month) ÷ effective headcount = months required or additional hires needed. Efficiency ratings assigned by leads per person (0-100%). Common calibration problems: (1) leads set ratings optimistically for people with demonstrably low output; (2) estimates made before a definition of done existed -- teams defaulted to estimating for full launch quality; (3) wide estimate ranges (e.g. 155 days for a zone, suspected 20-30% padded) may halve with one additional hire; (4) QA estimates at full scale reflect one-person modelling, not a contracted team. Scope framing rule: present to leadership as "how much of the VS can we close this month?" not a fixed end date -- fixed dates inflate team behaviour. [source: 2026-06-26_ch-vs-staffing-efficiency-ratings]

**Hours + complexity matrix (replacing story points):** A ~55-person studio abandoned story points in favour of a two-axis estimation system after years of Fibonacci-card gaming. Axis 1 -- Size: actual estimated hours, not abstract points. Axis 2 -- Complexity: simple / easy / medium / difficult / impossible. Buffer heuristics: complexity 8/10 → automatic 20% buffer; complexity 9/10 → 25%+ or double ("you really don't know what it is"); XL + high complexity → do not size at all, run a research-only sprint with no deliverables expected. Key patterns: small + high complexity is hardest to estimate; large + high complexity gets easier as you move through it; the last 15% and polish phase carry effectively infinite complexity and must be treated as a separate phase. Engineering tasks often cannot be decomposed before a research sprint; art and design tasks almost always can be. Story-point failure mode is structural: Fibonacci cards invite abstraction over genuine estimation; fewer, clearer parameters resist gaming. Implementation timing: build the new system into Jira architecture now, but do not switch it on mid-vertical-slice to avoid approximately 3 weeks of re-estimation overhead. [source: 2026-07-08_task-estimation-hours-complexity]

**OKR threshold calibration:** 1 week late = green; 4 months late = red. Two-layer status model: internal plan uses a buffer; external reporting only flags when buffer is being consumed. A dedicated PM role owns loop-closing and OKR dashboard. [source: not_Ua643ajeN9C1f7]

**Milestone as production pressure mechanism:** Conference-anchored milestones allow teams to rationalise "not quite ready." Pressure-anchored milestones -- internal deadlines with no external excuse -- force the studio to estimate, build, and ship or face a clear internal failure. Once a studio ships to a pressure milestone it has evidence it can execute -- investor readiness follows from production discipline, not the other way around. [source: 2026-06-22_milestone-purpose-pressure-not-conference]

**VS estimation commit protocol -- T4 floor, buffer, crunch accountability:** For a vertical slice milestone commit, the quality floor is T4 proxy-kit quality. Estimates must be drawn from at least 6 weeks of actual production data. Accepted buffer: ±10%. Any estimate framed as "production problem" or subject to external-caveat qualification is absorbed by leadership. Crunch: if crunch is required, EP and GP carry the accountability (not team leads or individual contributors); crunch must have a defined start and end date before it begins -- open-ended crunch is not permitted. Post-lock changes require a formal Change Request process. [source: 2026-07-01_vs-estimation-commit-protocol]

**VS scope lock -- non-negotiable/possible-to-cut triage (a ~70-person MMO studio):** Every VS feature is classified as either non-negotiable or possible-to-cut before sprint commitments are made. Deferred items receive a named target milestone -- never "later" -- so the deferred list remains a real production artifact rather than a bin. Scripted encounters using existing assets can satisfy investor experience goals without building a full system; this prevents over-engineering features for the VS that will be replaced in production. Core demo loop gaps -- including loot distribution mechanics and the partner/co-op loop -- are identified explicitly at scope lock; these are the features most commonly forgotten until the first full playthrough. Feature minimums: four enemy types with distinct combat behaviours is the stated floor for a combat MMO VS -- below this, reviewers cannot evaluate combat depth. Magic systems can be demonstrated using existing visual assets where the underlying system is already built; the VS does not require purpose-built magic effects. Large GDD documents (e.g. 50+ pages) compressed to 3-4 pages for in-session scope-lock use; the full document is preserved and referenced for post-lock detail, but the working session operates from the compressed version. [source: 2026-07-16_vs1-scope-lock-feature-enemy-magic-decisions]

**Sprint cadence for AAA indie -- tech debt budgeting, alternating doc reviews, VS1 roadmap format:** For a ~65-person studio entering vertical slice production: tech debt is logged as Jira tickets and prioritised alongside features each sprint; the committed allocation range is 10-30% of sprint capacity. GDD and TDD reviews alternate every other sprint (~1 hour per session). The primary VS1 communication artefact is a PowerPoint roadmap showing features, priority order, and timeline -- more accessible to non-production stakeholders than a Jira board. VS1 scope lock process: senior advisor pre-separates core path vs non-core path features; non-core goes to a review list; then a 3-4 hour session with game director and creative director to finalise scope. [source: 2026-07-01_sprint-cadence-tech-debt-framework]

## Pre-Production to Production Transitions

**Diagnostic signals of false production belief:** Working prototype code exists but GDDs/TDDs are absent (60% GDD/TDD coverage at a studio that believed itself in production); art impressive but core systems undocumented; design approval routinely bypassed; features built for investor demos that need redesign for scalability; first structured estimate reveals scope 2-3x larger than assumed. [source: granola_4005eb22, granola_5fdd8c18, granola_f41b006d, granola_ae650223]

**Live service vs box game mindset gap:** The most critical and underdetected misalignment in early-stage studios: leadership plans and builds as if shipping a contained product while the game requires live service architecture from day one. Signal: the head of development has perfected the base product but has no plan for post-launch content, live operations, or a player economy. Fix: establish a hard cutoff date for base game systems; everything unfinished by cutoff ships as live content post-launch; align the head of development explicitly on the two-phase model before producers arrive. [source: 2026-06-22_live-service-vs-box-game-mindset-gap]

**Pre-production exit gate / early production entry requirements (NBI standard):**
1. Solid prototype / vertical slice completed and validated
2. All core teams established
3. All design docs in preparation (actively in progress, not complete)
4. All TDDs in development [source: granola_d977d66a]

**The Beautiful Corner (Tim Cain):** A small non-playable area built to final visual quality proves art pipeline feasibility before committing full production resources. **The Horizontal Slice:** All game areas playable but unfinished; tests structural integrity and total playtime. **Vertical Slice: definition and dual purpose.** Not a demo. A pipeline validation exercise determining whether production at intended quality is viable -- consuming up to one-quarter of total development time. VS serves four purposes: (1) studio demo for internal alignment; (2) pipeline validation; (3) investor material; (4) proof to the studio itself that it can build a game. **VS "building the real game" anxiety pattern:** Studios in vertical slice phase often develop a specific anxiety -- the team suspects they are building another pitch deck rather than their actual game. Fix: explicit mandate that every VS asset is a piece of the real game; unconfirmed features banned from pipeline meetings until confirmed; leadership repeats this framing consistently. [source: 2026-06-23_vertical-slice-real-game-framing]

**VS art quality floor -- proxy kit:** Proxy kit + proxy props is the correct VS1 quality standard; mid-poly bake is too high for general VS work and applies only to feature pillars. Set dressing is required on the critical path even at proxy stage. **Art style lock for milestone:** When a studio has two or more coexisting visual aesthetics, each must be formally locked before sprint commitments are made. The lock process: Art Director, Game Director, and studio lead review all existing assets per aesthetic direction; select one clear direction per zone/context; pass to engineering for a performance check; lock the style decision for the milestone duration; document in the knowledge base. "Lock" means: decisions made in 1:1 conversations are no longer authoritative -- the locked document is. [source: 2026-06-26_ch-art-style-lock-milestone]

**Forced art direction lock (when drift is chronic):** When a studio has been iterating on art direction for multiple milestone cycles without locking -- the diagnostic signal is that any senior stakeholder can rename the style in a passing meeting comment and the name sticks -- the fix is a forced lock session, not more feedback cycles. Process: (1) convene all decision-makers in one session; (2) present options side by side; (3) force a binary decision in the room; (4) issue a documented lock with one named authority who can reopen it; (5) future proposals must be submitted as formal alternatives for a new decision -- the room is not reopened for debate. Root cause of chronic drift: absent governance where no single person holds unlock authority; every senior voice becomes a de facto art director in every meeting. Four years of iteration with approximately six restarts driven by direction changes (not personnel failure) is the documented scale of this pattern. [source: 2026-07-02_art-style-lock-forcing-mechanism]

**VS proxy-to-finished ratio (~1:1.8) and skunkworks icon model:** When scoping a vertical slice, the proxy/finished split matters for timeline accuracy. Tier 1 (proxy state with base colour): minimum viable fidelity for spatial layout and rough aesthetic -- the correct VS1 target. Tier 2 (fully finished set dressing): complete texture, props, lighting, detail pass. Estimated ratio: if proxy = 100 hours, fully finished ≈ 180-220 hours (1:1.8 to 1:2.2). Studios treating "vertical slice" and "production-ready" as synonymous approximately double the actual VS timeline requirement. Skunkworks icon model: commission one simple building as a parallel pitch track -- near-complete fidelity in isolation without blocking the tier-1 pipeline; fallback is an AI-enhanced render of the proxy kit if production speed is the constraint. Tier-1 coverage across the full slice is the primary goal; the skunkworks icon is the only permitted exception to single-focus discipline. [source: 2026-07-02_vertical-slice-proxy-vs-finished-ratio]

**"Polished playable" vs MVP vocabulary, and C-level-first leadership training sequence:** A ~55-person MMO studio replaced "MVP" language with "polished playable" across all milestone communications. "MVP" carries a market association with half-finished shipped products that erodes internal quality expectations when applied to a vertical slice; "polished playable" sets the correct bar. Studio's first VS framed explicitly as a "game simulator" -- a diagnostic exercise exposing what the studio can and cannot do -- and pre-communicated as "likely to feel boring internally" to prevent the team reading it as a failure signal. Design freeze in force during VS: new ideas route to a locked document (categorised as locked/unlocked), not into sprint. C-level-first training sequence: do not cascade agile or leadership training to managers and leads until C-level expectations are explicitly defined. Proposed structure: half to full day aligning at C-level → teach leading, directing, and managing within an agile framework in that order → user stories written to a specific agreed DoD as the training completion criterion. Rationale: cascading training before top-down alignment produces inconsistent implementation at every layer below. [source: 2026-07-10_polished-playable-vs-mvp-studio-priority]

**VS velocity tactics -- animation scope reduction and environment art outsourcing:** For a ~55-person studio with environment art and engineering as the two critical-path constraints on a three-month VS window: (1) traversal and ACF animations are catalog-eligible -- sourcing from animation libraries frees senior animators for bespoke character work; (2) prop kit asset modelling outsourced to a ~12-person contract team for a 4-week sprint rather than expanding permanent headcount; (3) baking lights recommended independently by multiple senior engineering and art leads (~50% compute reduction); final decision pending a craft-level art director trade-off assessment. Prioritisation principle: VS definition of done is not launch quality -- lead estimates sandbagged against a launch quality bar must be corrected. [source: 2026-07-09_vertical-slice-outsource-animation-tactics]

**Publisher independence and Early Access as mass QA (Larian Studios):** Larian (35-40 people, €4.5M total budget including ~$950K Kickstarter) spent 15 years pitching RPGs to publishers who rejected or stripped out multiplayer. Outcome: 500K+ units year one, enabling DOS2 (team tripled, "first time we had sufficient resources to do everything well"). [source: web_2026-07-09_larian-divinity-os1-self-publishing-early-access-qa]

**Managing scope fear:** When a studio first sees its full VS scope, a predictable fear response triggers scope-cut proposals. **Game design hierarchy: Pillars → Player Promises → Value Creation:** (1) Pillars -- core design principles, CEO-approved, then locked; (2) Player Promises -- what the player feels as a result of each pillar; (3) Value Creation -- how each promise translates to measurable outcomes. Every contributor should trace their task through this chain. [source: 2026-06-19_pillar-promise-value-creation-framework]

**Audience-first game design -- for/against statements before pillars:** Audience definition must precede pillars and feature lists. Format: 15-20 statements maximum describing experiences the game is deliberately for or against. NOT granular persona archetypes (no Myers-Briggs, Bartle types) -- these are too complex to operationalise in daily decisions. Pillars are the "how" in service of this definition -- not the starting point. [source: 2026-06-30_audience-first-game-design-methodology]

**Vision pillar format -- headline, subheading, story, is/is-not, red-team:** Effective vision pillars use a specific 3-part format: a headline (short, memorable), a subheading (expansion), and a story (brief narrative of a player experiencing the pillar). Each pillar also requires an explicit is/is-not clause. Usability test: a pillar must have a meaningful opposite -- "good level design" has no meaningful opposite and is therefore useless as a filter. Red-team mandate: before any studio-wide rollout, pillars must be red-teamed by leadership for misinterpretation vectors and ambiguity. Pillars must be locked before the studio AMA; presenting unlocked language to the full team generates contradictory design decisions that are expensive to unwind. [source: 2026-07-01_ch-game-vision-pillar-framework]

**Pillars vs Value Creations (operative razors):** Design pillars are frequently too aspirational to function as daily decision-making tools. Two-layer framework: (1) Pillars = aspirations (high-level statements of what the game should feel like); (2) Value Creations = razors (operative constraints that limit how each pillar is delivered). Each pillar requires at least one corresponding value creation. The two most commonly missing value creations in early-stage vision documents: commercial viability (the studio must still exist and be funded in 5 years) and delivery constraint (a playable game ships within approximately 2.5-3 years). Stress-testing method: ask how the team will misinterpret or over-extend each pillar -- red-team against the people who use the pillars day-to-day, not just leadership. The person who writes pillars and the person who closes work against them are often different; the more operationally-minded closer is usually better at defining value creations. [source: 2026-07-07_pillar-value-creation-framework]

**Pillar alignment session -- multi-author consolidation and red-team process:** When multiple senior stakeholders have each produced their own design pillar documents, a structured session is needed to lock a shared version. Pre-session: consolidate all existing documents into a single hybrid draft; do not share the draft before the session -- each principal should bring their raw position, not a pre-aligned one; identify specific divergences for time-boxed resolution. Session format: attend with all principals whose buy-in is required; time-box each pillar; mark each "ready" or "not ready"; red-team the double-clicks ("what does this mean?") until alignment is concrete, not aspirational -- "the world remembers differences" sounds aligned until asked what it means for each principal in practice; items not requiring immediate resolution go on a deferred list, explicitly parked not dropped. Locking: once locked, the pillar document becomes the reference for art direction, systems design, and design briefs; future decisions are checked against the pillars, not re-opened for each feature debate. Diagnostic from prior red-team failures: misalignment at the detail level is common even when headline agreement exists; aspirational language can mask two opposite mental models; the red-team must reach concrete examples, not just head-nods to the headline. [source: 2026-07-14_pillar-alignment-session-process (anonymised)]

**Is/is-not definition technique for scope creep prevention:** When aspirational language in design documents (e.g. "persistent world") is triggering scope creep, the fix is a formal is/is-not definition for the concept. Format: a bulleted "IS" list of concrete, achievable manifestations, and a separate "IS NOT" list of explicitly ruled-out interpretations. The list must be written and distributed before the term continues to appear in design documents. [source: 2026-07-01_mmo-persistence-is-not-definition]

**Adaptive telemetry-driven tutorial design -- investor VS and player FTU as distinct operational modes:** A tutorial or first-time-user (FTU) zone requires two distinct modes that must not be conflated. Player mode: organic, telemetry-triggered prompts fire on observed behaviour (e.g. player pauses at a gap for 12 seconds, then receives a prompt); experienced players must not be blocked by rigid click-through flows. Investor mode: the creative director walks through the vertical slice live -- discoverability and organic flow are not the concern. Conflating the two modes produces zones that are neither good investor demos nor good player introductions. Structural design drivers: instanced single-player start area through to portal/world entry (multiplayer begins after); contrast as a spatial driver (enclosed/tense/dark interior vs open expanse outside); named locations throughout feeding UI and map systems; art must-haves locked before handoff (rock sets, stylised water, era-specific props, breakable walls). Scope note for instanced zones: breakable walls do not require regrowth logic in instanced start areas -- this is a valid scope reduction without sacrificing player experience. Handoff readiness requirements: Miro board with sign-off frames where stakeholders formally approve before handoff begins; all narrative, art, and audio docs consolidated into a single folder; dedicated Confluence page per zone logging changes with rationale. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-tutorial-ftu-adaptive-design]

## Build Stability, Merge Cadence, and Creative Documentation

**Weekly build as the primary visibility mechanism:** "I don't care what's in Jira or Perforce. If I can't play the game, it's not there." Weekly playable build is the foundation of studio state; Jira and tracking tools only become meaningful once anchored to a stable build. Biweekly showcase-style demos are false confidence. [source: 2026-06-19_weekly-build-visibility-design-doc-discipline]

**Design document discipline for engineering:** Engineers must not implement features without a properly detailed design spec, even for "known" features. Rule: if engineering cannot receive an adequate design spec, push it back. Creates accountability between design and engineering and surfaces when design is the bottleneck. [source: 2026-06-19_weekly-build-visibility-design-doc-discipline]

**Design brief why-first structure:** When a design team is producing work that does not match intent, the diagnostic is whether the brief contains a why section. Brief structure: (1) Why -- the reason the feature exists, what player problem or experience it addresses, what pillar or promise it fulfils; (2) Context -- what the feature connects to, how players encounter it, what systems it interacts with, what would break if absent; (3) Features -- what to build. Process: lead writes the why and context sections first; sits with the team, takes questions, refines the top two sections based on what the team does not understand; once solid, hands the features section to designers to fill out themselves. Why it changes team dynamics: coaching mode (asking questions, framing problems, then handing off) produces better engagement and ownership than directing mode (telling designers what to build); misunderstandings surface in the brief review, not mid-build when expensive to fix; designers who fill out their own features section have ownership and will flag misalignments before building starts. Intervention for a studio with accumulated ambiguity: why-first briefs give the team a concrete stake in the ground to push back against or build from, breaking the ambiguity pattern. Decision: all new briefs to lead with why; feature lists without why are not accepted brief format. [source: 2026-07-14_design-brief-why-first-structure (anonymised)]

**Design-to-engineering direct feedback protocol -- evaluation vs integration communication fix:** When a studio is experiencing repeated system rebuilds in the same area, the diagnostic is whether designers have explicitly stated what the existing system cannot do, or proxied the request through a plugin or external reference. Root cause pattern: "evaluate this plugin" is almost always a disguised statement of "the existing system doesn't do X"; absent a direct communication path, the cycle repeats -- designer finds workaround, engineer reads it as rejection, system gets rebuilt. Agreed protocol: (1) designer finds something useful in a plugin or reference game, sends a screenshot directly to the relevant engineer; (2) engineer responds "good / bad / already built / backlog" -- a single-line answer is sufficient; (3) engineers treat designer requests as requirements statements, not challenges to existing code; (4) designers state pain points explicitly ("the current system cannot do X") rather than proxying through plugin requests. Channel discipline: engineering should not be excluded from design exploration channels where integration topics are discussed -- cross-functional issues resolve faster in open channels than via siloed handover. Reported context: a seventh combat system rebuild at a ~55-person MMO studio was traced to this structural gap; engineering had already built approximately half the requested plugin functionality and designers were unaware. [source: 2026-07-14_design-engineering-direct-feedback-protocol (anonymised)]

**GDD-first feature pipeline with R&D Confluence documentation:** Fixed feature pipeline sequence: (1) Creative Director vision -- what kind of game this is; (2) Game Director direction -- how this feature fits the game; (3) design document (GDD entry or feature spec); (4) Technical Design Document (TDD); (5) build. Exception: small, obvious single-step requests only -- new systems and features always require documentation. Escalation path: if engineering is pushed to build without a GDD, escalate to senior advisor / CPO, not resolve it internally. R&D documentation requirement: all plugin and technology evaluations go to a dedicated Confluence section with findings, pros/cons, decision made, and action points -- prevents repeated evaluation of the same tool (documented case: one plugin evaluated 4-5+ times with no recorded decision). Communication: announce the GDD requirement directly from senior level; routing through leads does not achieve adoption. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-gdd-first-engineering-gate]

**Nightly stable build protocol:** Two-environment setup: (1) Stable -- nightly 4am build from main; QA lead runs smoke test each morning and posts to studio channel; always one sprint behind main. (2) Verification -- per-PR build gates merges; not for general access. Without a defined stable build, biweekly showcases become the only visibility point, creating false confidence. [source: 2026-06-19_nightly-stable-build-protocol]

**Build-to-close quarterly delivery cadence:** For studios with no history of regular delivery, a quarterly feature-closure cadence is the entry point for building delivery discipline. Core principle: build to close, not build to progress -- quarterly releases must represent formal feature closures (things formally closed on the roadmap), not progress updates. Daily builds + continuous smoke testing make quarterly releases predictable and boring; issues surface in daily builds rather than at the major release point. Tracking layer: maintain an external view alongside Jira showing closed features separately from in-progress features -- the "closed" vs "in progress" distinction is the key metric. Framing: "build to close, build to close, build to close" as a cultural anchor. Quarterly cadence is chosen for rhythm-building, not milestone alignment -- decoupled from investor or publisher schedules. [source: 2026-07-08_build-to-close-quarterly-cadence]

**MMO branch architecture model and sprint-branch governance:** Two complementary patterns from the same studio at different phases.

*Four-tier architecture (foundational):* Main (merge target only; all merges approved by branch manager), Dev (sandbox; breaking dev acceptable), Feature branches (isolated per system), Stable (one sprint behind main). Branch manager role: dedicated person owning merge approval into main. [source: 2026-06-19_mmo-branch-architecture-model]

*Sprint-branch governance for large-team VS (69 features in scope):* Sprint branch → QA branch → main (last known good). Daily build = current sprint build. Sprint branch merges back into dev at end of sprint; clean items promoted to main. Nothing merges to main until QA team approves. Feature branches were rejected at this scale because cross-team contributors would need to track 3-4 active feature branches simultaneously -- feature branches are only viable in a true feature-team model. Governance rule: new branches require Product Council sign-off. Backend changes that break last known good must notify the senior producer before merging. Engine version locked at UE5.8; UE6 rejected due to C++ deprecation and blueprint removal risk. [source: 2026-07-02_mmo-sprint-branch-governance]

**Engine tool rollout -- staging branch protocol for major tool updates:** When a major engine tool or studio-wide tooling package rolls out to a multi-team studio, the failure mode is merging all accumulated work studio-wide without per-team verification first. Corrected protocol: (1) staging branch first -- roll out to the staging branch before main; (2) per-team lead verification -- each discipline lead verifies the tool functions correctly for their team's context before the studio-wide push; (3) issues resolved before studio-wide merge -- do not push through unresolved issues on the expectation they will be sorted post-merge; (4) VFX lead required for any merge touching shaders, particles, or rendering systems -- VFX breakage is the most common high-visibility failure point in engine tool rollouts. Engine version discipline: stay on the current dot-release track; major engine generation upgrades (e.g. UE6) constitute a full codebase rewrite, not a viable migration during active production. Failure evidence: a one-month backlog of accumulated work was merged studio-wide without per-team verification, causing widespread breakage requiring emergency remediation. [source: 2026-07-14_engine-tool-rollout-staging-branch-protocol (anonymised)]

**Bug management cadence -- three-workstream sprint plus data-driven bug bash:** Three parallel workstreams per sprint as the base structure: feature work, bug prioritisation, tech debt. Bugs are never deferred to "later" -- they enter the next sprint or are explicitly deprioritised with a reason. Every ~3 sprints (data-driven, not calendar-fixed): dedicated bug bash week clearing accumulated backlog and addressing approximately 20% of outstanding tech debt. The tech director monitors bug load to determine when the bash is needed. Evidence: deferring bugs in a similar MMO project resulted in 845,000+ items to close in the final six months. The hockey-stick end-of-project bug pile is the predictable result of treating bugs as deferrable during active feature development. [source: 2026-07-02_bug-management-sprint-bash-cadence]

**Plugin evaluation -- carve assets, do not replace architecture:** When existing systems already cover a functional domain, adopting a plugin wholesale scraps invested work and creates new technical debt from the migration. The correct question: "What can we extract from this plugin without replacing our architecture?" Identify the specific assets with value (animations, rigs, traversal logic); layer them onto existing systems; reject the plugin as a system. Evaluation red flags: a plugin reviewed and rejected multiple times is a strong prior against adoption -- recurring rejection is not an evaluation failure, it is the correct outcome confirming the architectural boundary. UE version upgrade decisions require joint sign-off from art and technology leads before proceeding -- unilateral discipline decisions are a process failure. [source: 2026-07-02_plugin-evaluation-carve-not-replace]

**Structured UE plugin evaluation methodology (3-day standalone process):** When a design team requests a third-party UE plugin -- often a proxy for "the existing system doesn't meet our needs" rather than a genuine plugin need -- the structured process: (1) install in a blank standalone project, no game dependencies; (2) design team (2 designers) produces feature-by-feature like/dislike breakdown with video footage of desired features; (3) engineering maps each feature to already-have / don't-have / can-harvest-code; (4) network and replication scale assessed explicitly by the network engineer (most plugins assume 2-32 players; MMO context requires 200+); (5) Game Director and Creative Director review separately; total time budget ~3 days. Animation harvesting: plugin example projects often include animations retargetable in ~1 hour per asset with a tech animator; harvest as VS placeholders, flag all for post-VS replacement; confirm licence redistribution terms before any asset enters production. Diagnostic: if the design team is requesting a plugin without having given candid feedback on the existing system, force that feedback session first -- it is almost always the real issue. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-unreal-plugin-eval-methodology]

**Plugin governance approval gate and UE5/UE6 lock rationale:** For studios building complex multiplayer games on UE5: (1) no large systemic plugin enters the game without explicit senior advisor (CPO/CTO) approval; engineering redirects all requests with a named response; (2) UE5 to UE6 migration is not viable mid-production -- UE6 eliminates Blueprints and C++ in favour of Verse, making an existing codebase a full rewrite, not an upgrade; plugin developers stop maintaining UE5-compatible versions once UE6 ships, creating structural end-of-life risk for all systemic plugins; (3) full plugin manifest required: R (breaks on next UE update) / Y (risk to monitor) / G (low fragility); red and yellow items feed a backlog of custom code replacements; (4) when plugin pressure originates from a specific team member routing requests through other leads, treat it as a process failure -- the approval gate addresses the structural issue; the people conversation is separate. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-plugin-governance-ue5-lock]

**Marketplace asset IP risk in investor data rooms:** At a $10M+ investment level, data room licence audits surface every third-party file in a shipped or demo build. Marketplace assets (e.g. Unreal Marketplace, Unity Asset Store) intended as temporary placeholders frequently become permanent due to the cost and time of replacement. Mitigation protocol: classify all Marketplace content as placeholder from the moment of integration; compile an asset registry tracking every Marketplace item by integration date and sprint count; any Marketplace asset present for more than one sprint without a formal replacement plan is treated as permanent for IP audit purposes. VDR preparation requirement: Marketplace content must be formally flagged in investor materials -- not disclosed post-due-diligence. EP owns the production gate: no Marketplace content reaches a VDR-preparation milestone without a tracked replacement plan and timeline. Strategic implication: investors and their legal teams can identify third-party components; "temporary" assets that ship create undisclosed IP encumbrances that become negotiating liability during due diligence. [source: 2026-07-14_marketplace-asset-investor-data-room-risk (anonymised)]

**Third-party plugin VS1 exception gate and investor documentation requirements:** At studios with a no-systemic-plugin policy, a VS1 exception protocol handles cases where a third-party plugin is the only viable path to vertical slice velocity. Exception criteria: the plugin must be the only way to hit production velocity and quality -- alternatives must be exhausted first; decision escalates to EP, CPO, Game Director, and CEO -- not a unilateral engineering or design call; quality threshold is external player experience, not code quality (which will be replaced anyway); intent to kill and replace with original code before launch is a required part of the approval. Investor documentation: at $10M+ investment level, data room licence audits include all third-party components in a shipped or demo build; all plugin-sourced content must be formally flagged as placeholder immediately on integration; EP owns the production gate and tracks all plugin-sourced content for replacement; no plugin content reaches launch without a tracked replacement plan; undisclosed plugin usage in a VDR creates due diligence exposure even when originally intended as temporary. Plugin philosophy: plugins are valid as ideation tools (install in blank projects, take screenshots, share ideas); plugins are not valid as shipped components (dependency, update, and licence risks are too high). [source: 2026-07-14_plugin-vs1-exception-investor-documentation (anonymised)]

**UE5.8 pipeline efficiency -- practitioner estimates and adoption path:** Estimated overall pipeline time savings from full UE5.8 feature adoption at a ~55-person MMO studio: 10-15% across the board; animation pipeline specifically approximately 40% time reduction; layout tools and blueprint tools also assessed as material. These are potential gains from adoption, not measured savings -- the studio had not completed the adoption work at time of estimate. Adoption path: CPO compiles UE5.8 feature candidate list; Head of Tech reviews and assesses applicability; surfaced to leads as suggestions not directives. The key distinction: UE feature adoption is a voluntary uptake process managed through suggestion chains, not a tool rollout managed through sprint mandates. Context for estimate: combined with a 15-person outsource contract team for prop kit modelling, these efficiency gains provide the underlying math for a compressed VS timeline without headcount expansion. [source: 2026-07-14_ue58-pipeline-time-savings-estimates (internal)]

**Art Bible as mandatory creative source of truth:** Small art direction changes communicated informally cause studio-wide confusion when they reach people outside the original conversation. Fix: the Art Director owns the Art Bible; the Creative Director signs off. Every art direction decision -- even minor environmental style changes -- must be logged in the Art Bible before or simultaneously with being communicated. [source: 2026-06-23_art-bible-creative-direction-source-of-truth]

**Concept art as support function not pipeline gatekeeper:** When a concept art team self-organises into a gating role (requiring sign-off before environment artists can build), the root cause is almost always exclusion from the process earlier, not a genuine quality control mandate. Response: (1) clarify that concept is a support function -- assets already approved by the relevant lead or director are not sent back to concept regardless of concept's involvement; (2) redirect concept to new briefs in parallel, no production pause for retrospective sign-off; (3) identify the structural root cause, which is typically an absent AI art policy if AI-generated reference material is in use -- teams guard-dog when they feel cut out, not when quality standards require it. AI art policy gap diagnostic: if there is no formal policy and AI reference material is being used, the concept team's friction is a predictable outcome, not a creative conflict. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-concept-art-support-not-gate]

**Concept-first gate for new art work:** Mandatory concept pass required before any new character or environment art work begins, established at a studio where artists had been bypassing concept sign-off (going directly to AI generation or direct 3D builds). Concept artists reframed as art style guardians -- their approval role is upstream, not retrospective. Root cause of bypass: AI-generation and direct-to-3D shortcuts had cut the concept team out of the production flow upstream, resulting in 25-35% capacity utilisation and style drift. Gate policy for new work only -- already-approved assets are not sent back for retrospective sign-off (see "concept art as support function" entry above). Team right-sizing: reduced from 5 to 2 (highest output, most versatile, AI-friendly); one reclassified to creative/marketing. A smaller concept team with a clear gating mandate outperforms a larger team without one. [source: 2026-07-07_ch-concept-first-gate-new-work]

**Concept art team at 35% utilisation as Art Director authority failure signal:** Low concept art utilisation in active production is almost always a lagging indicator of Art Director authority collapse, not low demand. Root cause sequence observed at a ~55-person MMO studio: (1) Art Director not functioning as a director -- not enforcing creative direction, not closing decisions with leads; (2) senior art and VFX leads began escalating directly to the game director, bypassing the Art Director; (3) those same leads also bypassed concept art using AI-generated imagery for style direction and early ideation rather than commissioning the concept team; (4) result: concept team at ~35% utilisation while environment art pipeline remained the longest production leg. Key insight: over-staffed concept team is a lagging indicator of Art Director failure, not a concept capacity problem. The team size masks the upstream role vacancy. Corrective staffing rule: retain minimum 2 concept artists to oversee and reject AI output and prevent style drift, regardless of AD functional state. The "reject bad AI output" function belongs to the Art Director, not the concept artist -- concept headcount is not a substitute for AD authority. Contrast with the 2026-07-09 entry below (AI adoption reducing demand): the mechanism here is AD authority collapse driving AI bypass, not mature art bible enabling deliberate AI leverage. [source: 2026-07-10_concept-art-utilisation-ai-bypass-pattern]

**AI adoption reducing concept art demand -- utilisation as leading indicator:** At a studio with a maturing art bible, AI tooling adopted by senior leads has reduced concept art utilisation to ~35%. Mechanism: once an art bible exists, AI tools follow it with sufficient fidelity to produce concept-quality output for supporting and environment work, removing demand previously routed to human concept artists. Headcount implication: one concept artist per major domain (environment, character) is now the assessed ceiling at studios with a mature art bible -- five concept artists at ~55-person scale is assessed as excessive. Counterintuitive finding: more concept artists degrades style consistency rather than improving it, because each artist introduces a different sensibility without tight direction. Leading indicator diagnostic: when concept art utilisation drops unexpectedly, diagnose AI adoption by leads as the likely root cause before treating it as a temporary workload trough. The art bible is now an AI-leverage precondition, not just a style-consistency tool -- building one is the prerequisite to effective AI integration in the art pipeline. [source: 2026-07-09_ai-adoption-reducing-concept-art-demand]

**AI tooling policy for game studios:** Blanket "no AI" contract clauses are unworkable -- Photoshop, Zoom, and IntelliSense all use AI. Policy principle: restrict narrowly ("no AI-generated assets in shipped art"), not broadly. Engineering AI: broadly supported; engineer remains accountable for recognising when output is wrong. MMO/Unreal-specific risk: Unreal + MMO constraints are underrepresented in LLM training data -- models default to behaviour trees and single-threaded patterns, not scalable MMO server architecture; specific, constrained prompting is required. Art-side AI: a culture split in most studios with no stable middle ground. Pragmatic studio position: hire 1-2 concept artists to set visual thematics and QA AI-generated output rather than a full concept team; frame as runway preservation and velocity, not headcount reduction. Avoid AI on shipped front-end art where quality bar is unmet; use in ideation and reference generation only. [source: 2026-07-07_ai-tooling-policy-game-studios]

**Definition of Done with QA integration -- flow, overflow calibration, and bug triage:** A ~55-person MMO studio formalised a DoD process with QA as a discrete block after the dev/creative cycle. Dev/creative DoD flow: backlog → sprint → tasks added → work in progress → review → done. Review uses a RACI chart: failure adds tasks, pass-with-comments creates mandatory carry-forward stories. Three-head review (Creative Director + Product + Direct Lead) runs concurrently at a single stage -- not sequentially -- with a hard time limit for each reviewer to prevent indefinite gate-hold. Jira field for current DoD stage added to all features and stories. QA block (added to the right of the dev/creative cycle): triggers once a story reaches "done" in the dev/creative flow; smoke and automated tests run against the story; minimum bar "if it won't run, it ain't done"; QA pass → story done → push to merge at sprint end; QA fail → bug raised and linked to originating story, story reopens (called overflow). Bug triage: P0 (build breaker) at top of backlog, fix immediately; P1 reviewed against next sprint priorities. Overflow calibration: ~15% overflow is normal and a healthy process signal; 40%+ triggers a process audit (diagnose root cause as unclear DoD criteria or QA bandwidth, not treated as a failure). Meeting trigger: this DoD walkthrough was initiated by a design bypass incident (designer going direct to VFX artists without production routing). [source: 2026-07-10_dod-qa-integration-overflow-targets]

**AI 3D asset pipeline -- 90% AI + human refinement pass and AI specialist role split:** A studio CPO validated an AI-first asset creation workflow and defined the AI specialist hire structure. Asset pipeline model: AI generates assets to approximately 90% completion; artists refine and polish. Every asset still requires a human pass (ideation, colour direction, prompt ownership, final polish). PBR textures achievable in approximately 3 hours vs days by hand, straight to a playable state. Live demonstration: Tripo produced a full Unreal Engine-ready 3D model approximately 70 minutes after a concept meeting. Artist adoption risk: environment artists who champion AI adoption for others' workflows resist using it for their own work when directly confronted -- requires explicit direction and supervision, not peer advocacy alone. AI specialist role split (one hire cannot cover both): code AI specialist = pipeline automation, tool integration, engine-level generation workflows; art AI specialist = prompt ownership, style consistency, AI output QA, human refinement supervision. Hiring priority depends on current studio bottleneck -- art-first vs tech-first is a genuine debate per studio context. Energy and ethics objection handling: AI is already embedded in all common productivity tools (Word, Excel, video conferencing); framing opting out as meaningful is not accurate; effective use is the only defensible position. [source: 2026-07-10_ai-3d-asset-pipeline-tripo-human-pass]

**Game dev QA pipeline -- tiered automated testing:** Five-stage pipeline: Task > Commit > Build > Test > Deploy. Tests tiered by cost and frequency: unit tests every commit (under 5 min); smoke/boot tests every build (under 10 min); integration tests nightly (under 30 min); performance tests nightly and on release candidates (under 45 min); platform cert matrix on release candidates only (under 2 hours). Highest-ROI starting point: one boot test verifying the game launches and reaches the main menu. Performance is a correctness criterion: frame-time, memory, and draw-call budget violations should fail builds, not generate advisory warnings. Target QA split: 80% exploration and game feel, 20% functional regression. Zero tolerance for unaddressed flaky tests. [source: web_2026-06-24_game-dev-qa-pipeline-architecture]

**QA scaling model -- contracted team with two-build pipeline:** For a ~65-person live-service studio with a single overwhelmed in-house QA person: target state is a 30-person contracted QA team (not in-house hires) with TestRail for test case management. Two-build pipeline: (1) Review build -- tested against a new sprint commit cadence; catches regressions early. (2) Playtest build -- runs two sprints behind the review build; already tested and patched; used for playtesting and stakeholder sessions. Rationale: mixing review and playtest means bugs from the current sprint contaminate stakeholder confidence. [source: 2026-06-25_ch-qa-contracted-team-model]

**Multi-discipline definition of done -- Game Director ownership:** A live-service studio had siloed DoDs per discipline: art had 9 stages, code had 8 stages, audio was undefined. Fix: Game Director owns and consolidates all DoD -- each discipline proposes its craft-level stages as inputs; Game Director consolidates into one unified structure (a single Miro board); Executive Producer arbitrates quality vs timeline conflicts. Collapsed stage targets: code 4 stages (not playable / playable / feature complete / scalable); art 3-4 stages mapped to actual handoff points; audio 4 stages (sample / board sounds / approved sample / compiled system). [source: 2026-06-26_ch-definition-of-done-multi-discipline]

**Bi-weekly update framing (output to impact):** Every update item must state what was built AND why it matters to the game. Surfaces which teams struggle to connect their work to player experience. [source: not_VAlGkyKnb8xGcs]

**Kick-it-back documentation gate:** If a TDD cannot be written from what is provided, reject the story before sprint commitment -- not on day one of the sprint. Stories exceeding 10 working days must be split or reclassified as features/epics. [source: not_VAlGkyKnb8xGcs]

**UE5 rendering cost hierarchy and performance decisions:** The rendering cost hierarchy in Unreal Engine 5 is not what studios assume. From highest to lowest impact on frame budget: ray casting and shadow casting (dominant) → character movement at scale → polygon count (NOT the primary driver in UE5 with Nanite). The performance lever is occlusion culling, not polygon reduction. Baked lighting yields approximately 50% performance improvement. Decision model for MMO/open-world: bake for VS1 (stability and predictability); re-evaluate for dynamic lighting at VS3/VS4 when the rendering budget is better understood. Instancing vs seamless world: instanced zones are approximately 4x easier to build and test than a seamless world, at the cost of loading screen transitions; seamless worlds carry multiplicative compute cost. At VS1, instancing is the correct default. [source: 2026-07-01_mmo-instancing-vs-seamless-decision]

## Studio Proposal Culture and Scope Management

**Structured case format for proposals:** Studios that allow informal feature requests through Slack or 1:1 conversations generate a volume of unvalidated scope that leads to routing-around behaviour. A structured case format -- problem / solution / rationale / ask -- acts as a lightweight filter. The format self-selects for genuine investment: low-conviction ideas do not survive the effort of writing a structured case. Three paths for any proposal: greenlight / defer to an idea board / slot into a named future milestone. One week is the maximum evaluation time for a plugin or tool addition. [source: 2026-07-01_studio-proposal-culture-framework]

**Scope redirect model -- "put it on the list" not "no":** Saying "no" to scope requests trains teams to route around the production process. The redirect model: every request gets a destination (greenlight / defer / slot into named milestone), never just a verdict. The team must observe approximately four deferred items being actioned so the list is perceived as a real mechanism, not a bin. Pillar language as filter: once vision pillars are locked, scope decisions shift from resource argument ("we don't have capacity") to design argument ("this conflicts with our principles") -- the design argument is more credible and less contentious. [source: 2026-07-01_scope-redirect-backlog-not-no]

**Serendipity preservation:** Not all valuable game features emerge from planned design -- some of the most commercially successful mechanics originated as accidents (Nemesis system, GoldenEye multiplayer, WoW public quests). Mitigation: the idea board should include an explicit "serendipity" tag for items that originated outside planned scope and show early player excitement signals; these items get evaluated against actual player response data rather than design-principle alignment alone. [source: 2026-07-01_studio-proposal-culture-framework]

## Statistical Evidence: Research and Historical Case Studies

**Shirinian postmortem analysis -- 71% scope failure rate:** Ara Shirinian analysed 24 consecutive postmortems from Game Developer magazine (Feb 2008 to Jan 2010), 240 data points. Key findings: 71% reported scope problems; 50% reported late-stage feature additions or changes; production management failures dominated at 68 of 120 failure data points; only 21% planned projects around team capability, and all of those completed under 2 years; 38% required time extensions; outsourcing success rate: 43%. NBI applicability: the 71% scope-problem figure is a client-facing anchor statistic. [source: web_2026-06-24_dissecting-postmortem-scope-statistics]

**Game Outcomes Project -- empirical correlates of production success:** Multi-studio survey, several hundred developers, correlated against four outcome variables. Central finding: specific production methodology showed no statistically significant difference in outcomes. Highest-correlating success factors: design risk management 0.57 (strongest single factor); team focus 0.50; crunch avoidance 0.44; team stability and communication 0.36-0.39; production methodology 0.29 -- meaningful but not dominant. Additional findings: 79% of studios did NOT use agile or Scrum explicitly; crunch "did not actually save projects from delays." NBI advisory position: "Choose your methodology for cultural fit, not because you believe it determines outcomes -- what determines outcomes is design risk management, realistic scoping, and crunch avoidance." [source: web_2026-06-24_game-outcomes-project-production-correlates]

**Mid-tier console pre-production failure case study:** Anonymous UK mid-tier console developer, sports fitness game with multi-camera and mocap integration. Key failures: GDD and ADD not signed off until 5 months into full production; lead coder not assigned until month 6 and departed 4 months later; task management simultaneously used messenger, whiteboards, Excel, email lists, Campfire, verbal requests, MS Project, Mantis, and Word; feasibility never conducted on two key technical features; publisher scope changes arrived 3 weeks before initial submission. Rules derived: (1) Never begin full production without signed-off GDD and ADD; (2) Lead technical roles must be filled on day one; (3) Feasibility studies must precede commitment to technically novel features; (4) A proliferation of task-tracking systems is a production warning signal; (5) Publisher-driven scope changes within 3 weeks of submission are a contractual failure -- negotiate change-freeze windows in the original contract. [source: web_2026-06-24_mid-tier-console-preproduction-failure]

## Org Design Patterns

### Structural Anti-Patterns and Viable Models

Three anti-patterns in 50-person studios: (1) Producer mis-parented through Finance/Ops; (2) CTO span too flat -- becomes a bottleneck; (3) Tech Art/VFX in a grey zone between Engineering and Art -- both assume the other is accountable. Three viable structures at 50-100 people: Classic Functional with strong EP hub (best under 60); Pod/Strike Team (best for parallel workstreams with clear boundaries); Platform + Game dual-track (only when platform is genuinely a strategic product). [source: chatgpt_69034e5d, chatgpt_6967809b]

**CPO model -- separating game from studio health:** At ~55+ staff, a hard structural separation between "the game" (producer track) and "studio health" (CPO track) removes accountability ambiguity. CPO scope: HR, Finance, IT, Legal, Project Management. Producers focus exclusively on the game; studio board view = top deliveries, headcount adds, health metrics. CPO does not own game content, milestones, or scope decisions. Marketing kept deliberately lean through development -- two people; PR and community engaged pre-launch only. C-suite composition at ~55 staff: Founder CEO, COO, CPO, CTO, Head of HR under CPO. [source: 2026-07-01_studio-cpo-model-game-ops-separation]

**Scope-first headcount decision sequence:** All headcount requests must pass a mandatory three-step sequence before approval: (1) Scope first -- can the deliverable be cut or de-scoped? (2) Prioritise -- if not cut, can it fall below the VS threshold? (3) Headcount -- only if neither applies. Headcount math formula: Jira backlog hours divided by team capacity (people multiplied by available hours per person) equals headcount delta. Rules enforced by CPO at a ~55-person MMO studio: requests must be backed by Jira-derived workload math, not director intuition; feature MoSCoW lock must precede reliable estimates (estimates built on unconfirmed scope are obsolete by definition); Jira velocity data is unreliable for the first ~3 months post-implementation and carries elevated uncertainty flags. Failure mode: approving headcount before scope is fixed creates estimates that become obsolete as scope changes, leading to re-hire or over-hire cycles. [source: 2026-07-09_scope-first-headcount-framework]

**Two-house budget governance with intentional friction:** A studio budget deliberately split into two separate houses -- game dev vs studio ops -- with intentional friction between them as a discipline mechanism for a founder-CEO learning to run a business alongside making a game. Five macro budget codes: Operations, Art/Game, Marketing/Brand/PR, Game development, CTO. L&D split: studio-wide L&D under HR budget (centrally owned); departmental training as a separate per-HoD line item with direct spend authority up to a threshold. Petty cash authority stops at director level; leads do not have direct spend authority. AI tools excluded from petty cash -- any AI spend involving IP goes through IT and legal regardless of spend size. Hardware refresh cycle: 18 months, critical roles first, based on runway; anything under 18 months requires director approval. [source: 2026-07-01_studio-budget-two-house-governance, 2026-07-01_studio-cpo-model-game-ops-separation]

### Director Accountability Separation from Production Coverage

Production stops covering for directors who are not performing their accountability functions. Director accountabilities (non-delegable): estimate quality (directors commit to estimates they have reviewed; approval without reviewing is a performance failure); scope management (directors own the scope of their discipline); delivery (saying yes and not delivering is treated as dishonesty). "You cannot direct what you don't understand" -- if a director cannot explain their estimates, that is a coaching and performance moment, not a production problem to absorb. Practical evidence: a director approved estimates without review; senior production staff cut them by 40% on closer inspection. Escalation rule for producers: if friction is resolved, great; if friction keeps recurring, pass it up, do not absorb it. "Stop cuddling directors" as a managerial instruction to senior production staff. [source: 2026-07-02_director-accountability-production-separation]

### Three-Signal Productivity Monitoring (Without Screen Monitoring)

**Three-signal productivity monitoring without screen monitoring:** A CPO at a ~55-person remote studio developed a monitoring framework that surfaces genuine underperformance without surveillance. The three signals: (1) Slack activity -- communication and collaboration signal; (2) Jira delivery patterns -- task closure and sprint behaviour; (3) VCS check-in frequency (Perforce or Git) -- code output signal. Interpretation rules: low on all three simultaneously = something is wrong, investigate; low Slack alone is NOT a red flag for an engineer in deep work ("I don't want bing bing bing, I'm working in the flow"); the combination tells the story, not any single signal. Jira red-flag patterns: tasks consistently delivered at 2x estimated size; no progress updates until the last 3 days of a sprint -- both are observable, objective signals not dependent on manager judgement. VCS red flag: one check-in per week from a full-stack engineer (calibrate to role; complex system work may legitimately have lower frequency). Screen monitoring explicitly rejected: damages trust, establishes a slippery slope; leads pulling Slack statistics to monitor staff is the first step on that slope. [source: 2026-07-08_productivity-monitoring-signals]

### Performance Composite Dashboard (Studio-Level Productivity Visibility)

A composite productivity index using three existing tool signals to surface underperformance before it becomes a management crisis: (1) Slack activity (presence, thread engagement, response times); (2) Jira task delivery per sprint (completed tickets vs committed); (3) Perforce check-ins (commit frequency, volume). Design principles: not shared company-wide -- avoids gaming the metric. Slack alone is misleading (engineers in deep work, people in back-to-back meetings have low Slack activity); composite index across all three signals gives a more reliable picture. Flags trigger a lead or manager 1:1 follow-up, not automatic action or HR process. Expected impact: visibility alone typically pushes effective output to approximately 40-45 person equivalent from 30 at a 55-person studio; remaining gap addressed through targeted performance process. [source: 2026-07-02_performance-composite-dashboard]

### Staged Staff Replacement Methodology

When a studio has identified a cohort of underperformers, replacement follows a phased pattern: (1) Open the replacement role; (2) Find and hire the candidate; (3) Overlap new hire with existing person 2-3 weeks for handover; (4) Exit the existing person. Wave sizing: groups of 3-5-7-8, not all at once. Communication sequence: brief COO and EP first, consolidate a step-by-step plan, then brief the CEO/studio owner. Some underperformers self-select out when new hires arrive, which is preferable to managed exits. [source: 2026-06-19_staged-studio-replacement-methodology]

### Studio Seniority Distribution and Staff Mix Targets

Target distribution for a quality-gated MMO studio in VS preparation: ~60% seniors, ~30% mids, ~10% juniors (only with real mentorship infrastructure in place). Remote juniors without senior mentors develop bad habits or stall. Replacements are product-driven, not budget-driven. [source: 2026-06-19_studio-seniority-distribution-target]

**Startup studio talent ratio benchmarks and animation throughput red flags:** Data from a ~55-person MMO studio mid-vertical-slice. Observed (wrong) talent mix: ~4% senior/vet, ~36% mid, ~60% junior. Correct target for a studio in active production: ~50% vets/seniors, ~30% mids, ~20% juniors. A junior-heavy hire in a production context without dedicated mentors delivers neither support nor output -- juniors need mentors and learning time, not a production environment. Failure framing: "Do you think hiring half your staff as juniors is supporting them when you're also trying to build a game?" Recovery path at 55-person scale: an exit wave followed by senior/mid backfill; cannot grow out of it organically. Retain junior records for re-engagement when the studio scales to 150+ with mentors and proper development plans. Animation throughput red flag: 3 production-quality animations through pipeline in 6 months with a dedicated animation team is a hard structural red flag requiring structural intervention, not coaching. Industry benchmark: animations should reach functional in-game state within approximately 1-1.5 days from blockout state; 20 blockouts with 3 finished animations means the system, not the artists, is broken. Root cause pattern: studio affection protecting underperforming team members delays the structural intervention and compounds the problem. [source: 2026-07-08_studio-talent-ratio-animation-benchmarks]

**80/20 mid/senior target at production scale:** A ~65-person live-service studio identified junior-heavy staffing as a structural quality risk. Baseline: ~63% junior-level. Target: 80% mid/senior, 20% junior (approximately 20 junior roles in a ~100-person studio). Risk articulation: in a market of 20,000+ Steam releases per year, "good enough" does not clear the bar. Phased departure approach: groups of 2-3 with 2-3 weeks pipeline overlap before contract closes. Communication framing: "evolution phase" and "skill uplift," not layoff or restructure language. [source: 2026-06-25_ch-studio-staff-mix-80-20]

### Junior vs Senior Mindset Diagnostic

The response a team member gives to a partial or incomplete vertical slice build is a cleaner seniority signal than their title or years of experience. Junior mindset: "When do I get to see the game?" -- waits for systems to be built before engaging; treats incompleteness as a reason to defer contribution. Senior mindset: reads the roadmap and figures out what to close before the next system lands; treats incompleteness as the work to close. Coaching implication: rather than replacing a junior-mindset contributor directly, prefer hiring someone who can model and mentor the senior mindset; overlap the two during onboarding, then exit the previous person once the new standard is visible to the team. Useful for assessing attitude fit during hiring, probation, or performance reviews. [source: 2026-07-02_junior-vs-senior-mindset-production]

### Leadership Management-to-Execution Ratio Framework

A phased ratio model for calibrating how much time a senior technical hire should spend managing vs doing hands-on work. Phase 1 target: shift from ~80/20 (managing/doing) to ~60/40 -- address the gap before team resentment builds; junior team members are not learning because the lead cannot demonstrate or model the skill. Trigger: feedback from multiple stakeholders independently converging on the same gap. Phase 2 target: once stabilised at 60/40, shift toward ~50/30/20 (managing/doing/cross-team). Diagnostic signals that the ratio is wrong: team unhappy that lead cannot teach core discipline skills; lead praised for organisational delivery but not for craft contribution; junior staff not growing. Specify concrete deliverables for what "hands-on" means for a given senior role -- without specifics, "do more doing" is not a coaching instruction. One-week lag between coaching conversation and hands-on re-engagement is too long for time-sensitive VS delivery. [source: 2026-07-02_leadership-ratio-management-to-doing]

### CTO vs Technical Director: Role Distinction and Hiring Implications

CTO and TD are frequently conflated in game studios but require different people. CTO profile: sets architecture direction; represents engineering to investors and external stakeholders; coaches against over-engineering; manages the engineering organisation and thinks in headcount and org design. Technical Director profile: closer to the code; teaches by writing code and showing; drives toward deadlines; manages individual contributor growth directly. Key distinctions: a strong TD with CTO ambitions is different from a CTO; for an MMO studio at 50-100 people, hiring two TDs (roughly front-end/back-end split) and letting their skills define the actual boundary is a valid approach over one CTO doing both; the org chart is always an intention -- people hired reshape it. Signal: a candidate who self-selects to TD after interviewing for a CTO role is demonstrating self-awareness, not a downgrade. [source: 2026-07-07_cto-vs-td-role-distinction]

### Art Direction Authority and Chain of Command

**Art direction authority correction methodology:** When an Art Director has lost effective creative authority because leads bypass them to influence the CEO/Creative Director directly, the correction requires a structured reset. Failure mode: AD defers to CEO on decisions within their own authority; CEO validates the bypass by agreeing to art style changes without routing through the AD; team reads this as passive leadership. Key insight: "whether this reflects reality or perception is irrelevant -- if the team believes it, the effect is the same." Correction process: (1) AD calls all art leads together and explicitly states art direction comes from the AD and bypassing has consequences; (2) AD separates creative spaces (leads own their craft) from direction authority (AD sets the visual bar and approves deviations); (3) Three scripts prepared by external CPO: when a lead bypasses to CEO -- "That's nice input. It's not the decision"; when CEO overrides without going through AD -- "Are you the creative director or the CEO right now?"; when AD is tempted to defer -- simply "no" (not argumentative, just firm); (4) CPO offers to attend the boundary-setting meeting as an advocate for AD's authority, backing the AD's "no" with the CEO when needed -- making the offer explicit without inserting uninvited. Three-stage escalation applicable to any director: (1) clear verbal direction; (2) follow-up if not actioned -- "I'm a little disappointed, were we not on the same page?"; (3) PIP if the pattern repeats a third time. Diagnostic dividing line: "you should be more forceful" (a suggestion) vs "you have to be" (a job requirement). [source: 2026-07-08_art-direction-authority-model]

### Tech Artist Role: Engine-Depth vs Maya-Tools and Hiring Interview Gap

**Tech artist role definition and interview failure mode:** A studio discovered its tech artist lead was a capable communicator and team manager but lacked deep engine and rendering knowledge -- a distinction not tested at interview. Role definition debate: one faction defines tech artist as an engine programmer (HLSL, rendering pipelines, Unreal deep systems); the other as a Maya tools specialist (rig tooling, export pipelines, DCC automation). Resolution: a strong hire covers both; the studio lacked anyone who could go deep into Unreal's rendering system and enforce performance standards. Consequence of the gap: ~147,000 objects in a modular world design flagged as "movable" instead of "static" -- a major performance hit that had been raised but not actioned. Interview failure mode: no technically qualified person on the original panel; proactive outreach, positive cross-team feedback, and good results under prior leadership are insufficient signals for a tech art lead role -- engine depth must be explicitly tested. Corrective approach: write a formal tech artist skill brief covering the full pipeline (DCC tooling and engine rendering depth), formally test the current holder against the brief before any exit or retention decision, and separately assess whether an engine programmer is also required. Audit diagnostic when reviewing a studio's modular world: explicitly check movable vs static object flagging -- this is the most common oversight without a technically strong TA. [source: 2026-07-10_tech-artist-role-engine-depth-vs-maya-tools]

### Systems Designer Role Definition for MMO

The "architect mechanic" definition: a systems designer blueprints systems on paper and tunes variables; does not write code. Two distinct sub-types requiring separate hires: (1) Gameplay systems designer -- auction house, guild, inventory, traditional metagame; (2) World/emergence systems designer -- dynamic world, corruption spread, day/night cycles, emergent NPC behaviour, world-state-driven gameplay; rarer and the differentiator vs traditional MMOs. Critical prerequisite: do not hire either until game pillars are locked. Hiring priority sequence: UI/UX designer and narrative designer come first; systems designer follows from pillar lock; economy/balance designers are mid-production hires. [source: 2026-06-30_systems-designer-role-definition-mmo]

### Quad Assessment and Staff Quadrant Review

**Quad Assessment for Production Readiness:** A one-time structured evaluation for entering a high-stakes production phase. Core question: "Can this person deliver high-quality content in their craft, at speed, right now?" Two criteria for staying: (1) Good to work with, self-managing, team-positive; (2) Delivers product-level quality consistently at pace. Result tiers: hard cuts (red triangle -- first priority), stars/saves (director owns the save), juniors (flagged J -- separate consideration), unmarked (exits but lower urgency). Leads given a cap ("you get three picks") to force honest assessments. [source: 2026-06-19_quad-assessment-staff-segmentation]

**Staff Quadrant Review (Ongoing Performance Framework):** A four-category 2x2: (1) Weak link -- low capability, low growth potential, managed exit; (2) Loose cannon -- high output but unpredictable or destructive; (3) Steady Eddie -- reliable, meets expectations; (4) Champion -- high capability, high behaviour alignment. Evaluation against the role standard, not peers. [source: not_ireYPwXIKrrsWd_quadrant]

**Strike-based performance protocol for returning or at-risk staff:** A structured re-engagement and escalation protocol designed for a CEO or founder who pushes for re-engaging a previously departed employee against team consensus. Three-strike system: senior advisor notified by the direct manager at each strike occurrence; after three strikes, contract closed with no further escalation. Decision attribution: the re-engagement decision is publicly attributed to the senior advisor, not the line manager or CEO -- protects the manager's and CEO's relationships with the employee; gives the senior advisor leverage to coach the CEO on people decisions regardless of outcome. Value is symmetric: if the employee succeeds, the studio benefits; if they fail, the documented trail supports the coaching conversation about people decision-making. Setup phase: deliver prior feedback themes to the employee upfront before re-engagement begins; establish documented 1:1 cadence from day one. [source: 2026-07-02_strike-based-employee-performance-protocol]

### Meeting Structure Discipline

**Four-layer studio meeting structure:** Studios past ~30 people that mix strategic, operational, and team-level content into the same meeting cadence suppress hard executive conversations. Four-layer structure: (1) Executive/C-level -- founders, advisors, GC, CFO; agenda: run the business, staffing decisions, financial runway; (2) Studio leadership -- department directors and above; agenda: product and studio health, directors own their vertical; (3) Product Council/Directors -- all directors and producers; agenda: alignment and feedback flowing between exec and leads; (4) Leads -- team leads per discipline; agenda: team-level updates, blockers, sprint commitments. Key design choice: legal and HR sit at executive layer. Mixed audience suppresses hard conversations. [source: 2026-06-25_ch-four-layer-meeting-structure]

**Director headcount meeting as leadership assessment tool:** A CPO uses the headcount review meeting not primarily for resource planning but as a leadership diagnostic. Format: each director presents their own headcount logic with hours-based justification; the facilitator (CPO) asks questions but provides no answers -- a honey-pot approach where asking the question tests whether directors know where the game actually is. An independent observer (senior EP or external advisor) attends but does not participate or intervene. Post-meeting: facilitator and observer compare notes privately. The key evaluation question is not whether the director chose the right number, but "did they use director-level logic to arrive at their number?" Per-director outcome: coaching plan or replacement plan. Companion format -- Studio Leadership meeting: named deliberately not "production meeting" to avoid discipline siloing; EP chairs; RAG status per department; only red and yellow items receive substantive airtime; green items are self-contained 30-second updates ("I have X, I'm doing Y about it, any objections?"); requires a CPO/EP working session to define format before rolling out -- do not launch cold. [source: 2026-07-08_director-headcount-evaluation]

**Three-tier studio meeting cadence -- operations model:** A complementary pattern to the four-layer structure. When a single large meeting mixes C-level, department, and project content, sensitive HR and financial decisions bleed into inappropriate audiences. Three-tier hierarchy: (1) C-level -- strategic decisions and sensitive HR/financial topics, made here before being handed to operations for execution; (2) Operations meeting -- department leads present top-5 items, RAG status, new/resolved issues, and asks; code-word system (e.g. "staffing") signals an HR issue requiring C-level to stay while others leave; agenda shared 1 day ahead; stakeholders with no relevant agenda items can leave early; first half structured, second half overflow or sensitive topics; (3) Project meetings -- spun up as needed, owned by an assigned lead, relevant people only, progress reported as a RAG item at operations level. Key design rule: sensitive decisions (terminations, performance actions) are made at C-level first, then handed to operations for execution -- the operations meeting is not the decision layer. HR meeting retirement: once the operations meeting with code-word escalation is running effectively, a dedicated HR meeting is retired. [source: 2026-07-07_three-tier-studio-meeting-structure]

**Executive meeting tracker -- structured spreadsheet over AI summaries:** AI-generated meeting summaries fail when they are not structured -- participants cannot quickly identify what was red, what needed their attention, or what had been decided. New format (shared Excel tracker): red/yellow/green status per person per week; mitigations for reds included inline; asks listed explicitly (what each person needs from others). Accountability mechanism: if any attendee arrives without their section filled, the meeting pauses in silence until they complete it -- silence is the structural enforcer, removing the social awkwardness of chasing. Previous tabs locked once the week closes, creating a permanent searchable decision record without a separate minutes process. C-suite only as standing attendees; other leads called in by topic. [source: 2026-07-02_executive-meeting-accountability-redesign]

**Named ownership "who has the ball" protocol:** A lightweight production ownership protocol for studios where tasks and workstreams lack a clear named driver. Each task or workstream is tagged with a single named owner ("who has the ball"). Red ball = P0: that owner drops all other work and unblocks immediately. Rollout approach: prototype within the production team first, then extend to leads with one area champion per department modelling the system. Supporting gate: engineering does not build features without a design document; if no GDD entry exists, the request is kicked back; if engineering is pushed to build without a GDD, the escalation path is the senior advisor. Production channel owns the broadcast layer: AI-generated call summaries posted with links; other channels receive links, not duplicated content. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-who-has-the-ball-production]

**Decision owner model:** Without named decision owners per pipeline stage, meetings expand to include everyone and collapse into non-decisions. A 12-person, 45-minute meeting with no decisions reached is the failure pattern. Fix: assign a named decision owner per pipeline stage (e.g. level design handoff: Level Design Director; character pipeline: Art Director). Meeting attendees = decision makers for that pipeline stage only. [source: 2026-06-23_decision-owner-meeting-discipline]

**Executive RAG meeting format:** Format: (1) Start by reviewing last week's action items -- names visible; (2) Each area owner gets 5-7 minutes: RAG status, plan, and closure date; (3) No update without a timeframe; (4) No problem-solving in this meeting -- identify, assign, spin off; (5) Persistent item titles week-to-week so progress tracks against a fixed reference. [source: 2026-06-23_executive-rag-meeting-format]

### Communication and Culture Patterns

**Poisoned phrase problem:** A phrase coined around a specific failed initiative becomes toxic -- using it in future contexts triggers defensive reactions from leadership regardless of the underlying concept's merit. Intervention: (1) identify the phrase and its associated failure; (2) understand what the stakeholder objected to vs what they actually want; (3) find an alternative phrase; (4) introduce the reframe proactively before others use the old language. [source: 2026-06-22_poisoned-phrase-studio-culture-reframing]

**Staged challenge sequencing for leadership development in defensive leaders:** When a studio leader is known to be insecure or defensive, direct confrontation produces defensiveness rather than growth. Staged challenge model with three ascending difficulty levels: Stage 1 -- introduce an advisor or peer with a non-threatening relationship (someone the leader naturally defers to or respects); Stage 2 -- introduce a peer who begins to encroach on the leader's space (mild challenge to authority); Stage 3 -- introduce an opinionated senior who directly challenges the leader's thinking. Key principles: the development arc is denial → do the scary thing → it goes okay → confidence builds; milestone indicator = voluntary vulnerability ("I don't know how to do this" said without prompting); the encroacher in Stage 2 must be actively managed -- the encroachment is a tool, not a natural relationship; language discipline: replace "I'll try to X" with "I X" (first-person present tense removes the permission structure from the language pattern); manufacture a low-stakes stumble before the real one arrives, so the leader has a recovery reference. Applicable when: a CPO or studio director hire is placed above a founder or long-tenured manager who has never had authority challenged; a new advisor enters a studio where senior leadership is territorially defensive; a first-time people manager is resistant to feedback. [source: 2026-07-10_staged-challenge-leadership-development (anonymised)]

### Managing Founder "Midnight Ideas"

Mechanism: a shared idea log where anyone receiving an ad-hoc founder request adds the item for weekly review. Ideas are not acted on ad hoc. Showing founders all their own ideas in one place is a natural self-regulator. For in-meeting scope pivots: the lead handles the interrupt in the moment; coach the founder on the pattern in their direct 1:1, not in front of the team. [source: 2026-06-19_founder-idea-log-scope-governance]

### Employee Satisfaction Survey Timing

Do not launch until the studio has capacity to act on results. A survey creates a commitment backlog; launching before infrastructure exists creates expectations that will be visibly unmet. Immediate morale intervention alternative: all-studio 1:1 rotation (~55 people over ~4 weeks). Target survey timing: after Jira, pipelines, and vertical slice are moving. [source: 2026-06-19_employee-survey-timing-principle]

## Onboarding at Scale

**Role-specific machine builds and common stack:** Artist kit, producer kit, developer kit. Hardware for FTEs only; contractors provide own. General: Slack, Google Workspace, Jira, Confluence, VPN. Development: GitHub, Perforce, Azure, Redis. Art: Perforce, Maya/3D Max, Photoshop, Miro. Production: Claude accounts, Granola, Whisper Flow. Four-month probation with 30/60/90-day check-in reviews. [source: granola_891cf074]

**Jira implementation: configuration principles.** From experienced administrators (Blizzard, My.games, Amplitude Studio -- all arrived independently at the same conclusions): (1) Use components, not labels -- labels break filters through typos; disable labels wherever possible. (2) Standardisation over team autonomy. (3) Custom fields only when genuinely needed, shared across projects. (4) Issue hierarchy: Epic > Feature > Story > Task. (5) Requirements gathering from three user levels: executives (dashboards), team leads (assignments + standups), individual contributors (task execution). (6) Observe a real standup before configuring. (7) Train 2-3 super users for light admin. [source: 2026-06-22_jira-setup-methodology-game-studio, 2026-06-23_jira-parallel-run-migration-approach]

**Jira migration: parallel-run approach.** Run Jira alongside the existing tool until feature parity is validated; only then cut over. Prune the existing backlog during migration rather than lifting and shifting. ScriptRunner non-negotiable for complex automation. Timeline: core structure within 1-2 months; 70-80% implementation by month 3. [source: 2026-06-23_jira-parallel-run-migration-approach]

**Jira implementation process discipline -- structure before workflows:** Implementation sequence: (1) projects, issue hierarchy, and default workflows (open/in progress/done) first; (2) lock real pipeline stages before reflecting them in Jira -- do not build workflows from an unfinalised pipeline; (3) complex automation and approval chains deferred until pipelines are stable. Ideation triage model: raw ideas never enter Jira directly; they go to an external queue (email alias or service request form) reviewed every 2-3 weeks; only validated ideas move into Jira as research tickets; rejected ideas stay out; approved tickets are promoted via automation. Process ownership: production owns the implementation, department leads give input on inconveniences but do not make decisions. Governance risk: experienced Jira users from prior studios frequently assume ownership of process design -- production must hold the governance line. Meta-practice: write user stories for the Jira implementation itself, modelling the same practice the team is learning for game dev. Velocity caveat: data is unreliable for approximately the first three months post-implementation. [source: 2026-07-09_jira-implementation-process-discipline]

**Jira + Perforce rollout sequencing (5 steps, ~6-7 weeks total):** (1) Load Jira (~3 weeks from rollout decision); (2) Art and tech producers onboard concurrently; (3) ~1 week to stabilise with a Jira admin hire; (4) ~3 weeks debugging pipelines via sprint retrospectives; (5) Sprint flywheel starts ~6-7 weeks from rollout decision. Do not open Jira until controls and structure are ready. Estimation exercise must precede rollout. [source: 2026-06-19_jira-perforce-rollout-sequence]

**Contractor compliance -- IR35 and day rate model:** Studios with mixed contractor/FTE workforces risk employment classification claims when contractors invoice at a fixed monthly rate during time off. UK IR35 fine: ~£60K per incident; Spain: up to €55K. Fix: gross-up day rate to cover expected time off; calculate as (monthly rate ÷ 22.5) × uplift for ~36 annual non-working days. New expected working days per year: ~226. All leave labels renamed from "vacation"/"paid leave" to "out of office." Contractors "notify" leads of unavailability -- they do not "request" approval. [source: 2026-06-26_ch-contractor-day-rate-compliance, 2026-06-26_ch-ir35-contractor-classification-risk]

**Contractor vacation policy restructure and red-team rollout approach:** Pay contractors on a 20-day/month average (lower than actual days worked under current model). Annual effective salary unchanged; monthly invoice varies based on actual days worked. Requires tool integration (e.g. HiBob) for day-logging; requires contract amendments for each contractor. Red-team rollout model: identify one trusted contractor per department; walk the policy through with them before studio-wide communication; goals are communication practice and ambassador-building, not policy testing. Communication ownership: senior advisor or HR owns the communication package -- department heads are not the right vehicle for policy rollout of this complexity. Finance must confirm statutory sick leave obligations before any contract amendments are executed. [source: 2026-07-02_contractor-vacation-red-team-rollout]

**Contractor dead contracts and vacation rate-uplift model (multi-jurisdiction):** Active but unperformed contracts ("dead contracts") must be closed immediately -- never left open during a period of non-performance (military service, personal leave, illness). Leaving a live unperformed contract open can be construed as placing a contractor on leave. In UK, Germany, Netherlands, US: treating a contractor as an employee triggers back-pay liability; labour court fines run approximately €60,000 per incident. Closure is "without prejudice" unless there is misconduct or non-delivery. Company handbook linkage in contractor contracts must specify the version at signing -- auto-binding to future handbook updates is a constructive employment indicator. [source: 2026-06-30_contractor-dead-contracts-vacation-rate-uplift]

**UK studio contractor compliance -- common failure points at scaling studios:** Rapidly-scaled UK game studios almost always accumulate active compliance failures before HR infrastructure matures. Highest-exposure item: contractor misclassification -- offering sick pay, vacation, or bereavement leave to contractors triggers £60,000 fines per infraction; multiple infractions can be active simultaneously. Right-to-work checks are required for UK contractors as well as FTE -- routinely missed at scaling studios. Immigration and sponsorship: Certificate of Sponsorship documentation must be complete before the HMRC/Home Office application, not assembled in parallel; right-to-work infrastructure and compliant banking must be in place before any application begins. Corporate banking: some fintech business banks are not accepted by financial counterparties in regulated UK payroll and compliance contexts; traditional business banking is required. Remediation principle: fix compliance quietly before attracting regulatory attention ("don't poke the bear"); treat each infraction category as a separate workstream with a named owner; design the data and document pipeline first, then configure tooling -- not the reverse. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-uk-contractor-compliance]

**Contractor exit protocol -- graceful vs swift removal:** Studios with a history of abrupt contractor removals (instant Slack deactivation, no goodbye) should codify a policy distinguishing hostile from non-hostile exits. Core framework: hostile actors (misconduct, IP risk, security concern) receive swift, precise exit with no notice; everyone else receives a thoughtful, graceful exit -- time to say goodbye, public acknowledgement of contribution, work celebrated before departure. Practical elements: Slack access maintained during a defined handover period; team notified with space to respond; tone set by leadership, not delegated coldly to HR. Legal caution: over-honesty about performance rationale in exit conversations creates legal exposure (unfair dismissal analogues, discrimination claims) -- communicate with dignity without disclosing the full performance rationale to the individual. HR executes; C-level makes the decision but does not handle the conversation. Reputational dimension: cold removals are observed by the remaining team and signal how they will be treated if their time comes. [source: 2026-07-07_contractor-exit-protocol]

**Early probation exit (UK employment law):** Three grounds for early termination: (1) Declaration of incapacity by the employee; (2) Competency misrepresentation at hire; (3) Structural misalignment signals (positioning for a more senior role, contradicting a peer lead within weeks). From 1 January 2027, unfair dismissal rights begin after 6 months (reduced from 2 years). [source: not_HubmSolirYMTbM, not_CPGgraRzP9tMoz, not_ireYPwXIKrrsWd_contractor_lexicon]

**Independent red and pink list framework for incoming leaders:** A structured onboarding tool for senior hires and external advisors entering a chaotic or rapidly-scaled studio. Red list: critical compliance or operational failures to address before anything else. Pink list: structural gaps and process improvements that matter but can be sequenced. The incoming leader builds their own list independently during the first 2-4 weeks; the senior advisor cross-references and narrates which reds are already in motion and which are not yet started. Value: fresh-eyes perspective surfaces issues the existing team has normalised; cross-reference reveals whether the organisation is aware of its own failure points; the delta between the two lists is diagnostic -- overlap = confirmed risk, gap = what was missed. Observed scale: senior advisor's own red list on joining a ~55-person MMO studio was 81 items across production, art, and tech. Protocol: all senior hires asked to build a personal red list within first weeks and share it in fortnightly 1:1s. UK probation note: the first four months are the statutory unfair dismissal window; structured performance testing must be established within probation, not retrospectively -- informal assessment is not sufficient legal protection. [source: 2026-07-06_ch-red-pink-list-framework]

**Hiring pipeline governance:** (1) Any open role with fewer than 3 valid candidates is red status; (2) Lead-level and above require scorecards and background checks; (3) HR screening as first step: collects salary expectations, contract type, relocation interest before technical evaluation. ## Quality and Delivery Standards

## Business Philosophy

**Games as recipes, not feature bags:** The job of a game studio is to find the recipe for fun before going bankrupt. Two failure modes bracket the space: (1) pure production mode -- the team ships on time but the game is boring because they never found the recipe; (2) pure exploration mode -- the team finds great things but never ships because they can't stop iterating. The recipe model: a great game is a specific combination of elements that work together holistically, not a list of strong individual features. Modern development has produced over-siloed teams where engineers never play their own game, designers never see code constraints, and no one owns the holistic experience. Chess as the model: 1,000-year-old recipe, no patches needed -- the pieces are balanced because the recipe is right, not because each piece is optimised individually. [source: 2026-07-01_finding-fun-recipe-philosophy]

## MMO Technical Architecture

**MMO infrastructure -- geo-distributed gameplay, centralised persistence:** No perfect solution exists for global MMO latency. Pragmatic architecture split by data type: gameplay servers geo-distributed for low latency (located where the player is); persistent data (inventory, characters, economy) centralised (e.g. US East); non-gameplay transactions (inventory, shop) latency-masked with caches and proxies. Player expectation management: FF14 model -- players opt in to a specific data centre knowing the latency tradeoff; build this choice in from launch; Australia is a known unsolvable, not a solvable problem. Cloud hosting comparison: AWS provides automated failover but creates vendor lock-in risk; third-party leased data centres are cheaper long-term but require self-configured failover; Hathora provides cross-cloud fallback (AWS, GCP) without managing it manually, worth evaluating as a middle path. Unreal-specific requirement: Unreal defaults to single-threaded server execution; MMO server architecture requires explicitly forcing multi-threaded patterns as an early decision, not a retrofit. [source: 2026-07-07_mmo-infrastructure-geo-distributed]

**MMO server authority model -- client/server split:** Full player simulation on the server is ideal for security but not feasible at MMO scale. Correct split for a combat MMO -- server-authoritative (always): combat hit resolution, cooldown checks, range and line-of-sight validation, AoE spatial partition lookup and conical hit check, all outcome state (health, inventory, progression). Client-side projection (for responsiveness): ability visual effects rendered immediately on client; client sends facing direction for targetless AoE and server resolves actual hits; client "hints" which enemies it believes it hit, server validates and does not trust. Anti-cheat posture: malicious client injection is a certainty, not an edge case -- design for it from day one; log cheat attempts silently; issue wave bans rather than real-time flags (real-time detection exposes the detection logic and causes false positives). Divergence management: client and server state diverge more in MMOs than smaller-scale games because less server time per player creates bigger prediction windows; making that divergence invisible to the player is the core MMO-specific engineering challenge. Table stakes principle: players compare feel to the best games they have played, not to what is technically hard -- client experience must feel instantaneous even when the server is authoritative. [source: 2026-07-07_mmo-server-authority-model]

**MMO world spatial vocabulary -- shared terminology as prerequisite for world systems:** Cross-discipline vocabulary must be agreed before world systems, zone design, or world-building tools are built. When terminology is used interchangeably (region/zone/subzone/area) across disciplines, each team builds and documents to a different spatial model, requiring expensive reconciliation later. The World of Warcraft 6-tier spatial hierarchy provides a well-documented cross-discipline reference framework: Zone → Subzone → Point of Interest → Hub → Instance → City. This is used as a calibration reference, not necessarily as the studio's own naming convention -- the studio defines its own tier hierarchy, then each term maps to the WoW reference for cross-team disambiguation. Standardisation session requirement: all disciplines (engineering, design, art, narrative, systems) must attend a single session before any world-building system documentation is written; the session output is a shared glossary with each tier's definition, scope, and example. Without this session, terminology disputes at world-building tool design time carry multiplicative cost compared to resolving them before any system is built. [source: 2026-07-14_mmo-world-spatial-vocabulary-hierarchy (public)]

### Systemic vs Scripted Design

**MMO systemic emergence -- verbs, rules, and consistent outputs:** Systemic games generate sustained content through emergence rather than authored scripting. Design principle: design verbs (what the player can do) and rules (what the world does in response) consistently; the output of one system feeds the next. Sea of Thieves is the primary public model: the seas, the ships, and the cargo all interact according to consistent rules rather than authored set-pieces, generating shareable player stories the developers never planned. Design economy rule: only simulate what is noticeable to the player -- unseen systemic complexity has no experiential value and degrades performance without return. Systemic games generate sustained content production (new rules extend the system); scripted games peak at launch and require linear content investment thereafter. [source: 2026-07-16_mmo-systemic-emergence-toxicity-culture-engineering]

**Viral moment architecture for MMO combat:** Systemic games generate shareable moments that marketing cannot manufacture. Two specific design decisions with documented viral moment effects: (1) kill-tag on the last hit rather than the most damage -- awards the kill credit to the final blow, enabling unexpected viral moments where an arriving player turns the tide; (2) body-fly distance set for spectacle rather than realism -- exaggerated ragdoll physics on kill produce screenshots and clips; understated physics do not. Sea of Thieves as public model. [source: 2026-07-16_mmo-systemic-emergence-toxicity-culture-engineering]

### Toxicity R-Value Modelling

**Toxicity R-value and culture engineering:** Toxicity in multiplayer games spreads as an infectious pattern, not a random distribution. Modelling framework: ~5% of players exhibit toxic behaviour; each toxic player infects approximately 8% of the players they interact with repeatedly (the "R-value" analogy from infectious disease modelling). Design implication: the culture of an online game is set by the early audience and by what the game visually highlights and celebrates -- a game that puts negative behaviour in prominent UI positions trains the community toward it. [source: 2026-07-16_mmo-systemic-emergence-toxicity-culture-engineering]

Shadow-muting outperforms bans as a toxicity control mechanism: the toxic player perceives normal play and cannot identify that they have been muted, removing the "ban badge" status effect that drives re-offending. Evidence: Blizzard research and Xbox data. [source: 2026-07-16_mmo-systemic-emergence-toxicity-culture-engineering]

Infamy systems backfire: when a game creates a negative reputation scale (most-wanted list, villain rankings, etc.), approximately 8% of the player population races to the top of that scale. The infamy system becomes a goal, not a deterrent. The system rewards the behaviour it is designed to suppress. [source: 2026-07-16_mmo-systemic-emergence-toxicity-culture-engineering]

Forced removal of player autonomy spikes toxicity: Fortnite's forced-concert events (where players had no control and could not leave) produced a measurable spike in toxic behaviour in subsequent sessions -- the loss of agency generated frustration that discharged into other players. Any event design that removes player autonomy requires a toxicity impact assessment. UCLA research cited as corroborating evidence. [source: 2026-07-16_mmo-systemic-emergence-toxicity-culture-engineering]

## Pre-Production (additions 2026-07-15)

### DoD Decision Authority Hierarchy

Without a named authority for definition of done, any senior stakeholder can reopen a milestone. Authority must be explicit, not assumed. [source: 2026-07-15_dod-decision-authority-hierarchy-game-director]

**Confirmed hierarchy:** (1) Game Director owns and approves definition of done at each milestone stage -- this is their primary authority, not a courtesy. (2) CEO override is valid only for a specific investor or commercial requirement and must be framed explicitly: "I am overriding as CEO because of [named investor/requirement]" -- not available for general design disagreements. (3) Creative Director has no vote on definition of done -- owns creative direction, not milestone readiness.

**Missing element:** Engineering definition of done is the most commonly absent component of a milestone framework. A studio can have design and art DoD well-defined while engineering DoD remains informal; this creates a gap where engineers can ship work that passes technical review but fails design intent.

**Applicable when:** A studio's milestones keep slipping because senior people reopen what was supposedly done -- establish a named DoD authority with a documented override protocol before the next milestone review. When a CEO softens milestone definitions under investor pressure -- the "explicit executive ask" framing creates accountability and prevents casual scope softening.

### MMO Player Archetype Two-Axis Design Framework

A single solo-to-group axis produces mediocrity in the middle. Adding a second axis reveals structural exclusions. [source: 2026-07-15_mmo-player-archetype-multi-axis-design-framework]

**Two axes:** (1) Solo to forced-group: how much does optimal play require other players? (2) No world impact to high world impact: how much does the player's presence change anything?

**WoW as cautionary success:** WoW succeeded by accident -- it built distinct content tracks per archetype (crafters, questers, raiders) with full game loops per type. Most studios don't map this explicitly and wonder why 70% of potential audience feels excluded.

**Pokemon model for archetype balance:** Optimal play exists but is never required. Skill-driven and cosmetics-motivated playstyles both win. Use this as the benchmark for a game that does not force everyone onto one axis.

**Pillar language test:** MMO design documents habitually write pillar language from one archetype's point of view. Before locking pillars, test each against four profiles simultaneously: achiever, combat-focused, cosmetics-motivated, and casual social player. "No gear treadmill" reads as "no progression" to an achiever. "No endless grinding" alienates players whose core loop is grinding.

### Pillar Language Precision Test

Pillar language encodes its author's player archetype. Imprecise pillar language generates imprecise design decisions downstream. [source: 2026-07-15_pillar-language-archetype-bias-precision-testing]

**Precision test (apply to each pillar before leadership alignment session, not in it):**
1. For each pillar, write "what this means" for each major player archetype (achiever, combat-focused, cosmetics-motivated, casual social)
2. If meanings diverge, the language is broken -- rewrite to capture the intent, not the author's shorthand
3. Add "is / is not" examples to each pillar: explicit statements of what the pillar does and does not rule out
4. Retire any source document used as pillar language if it predates the precision test

**Root cause:** Aspirational language written from a single archetype's point of view, then generalised as studio-wide direction. New hires receive it as gospel in onboarding documents, encoding the bias into the culture. "If we give these words to our world, our world will build to these words."

### Roadmap as Dual-Purpose Artifact

A studio roadmap should serve two purposes simultaneously; the format must be built to serve both. [source: 2026-07-15_roadmap-dual-purpose-investor-artifact-scope-lock]

**Purpose 1 -- Investor AMA readiness:** Shows rough direction of travel (epics in scope, approximate sequencing, milestone labels). Answers the investor question: "which year does this ship?" A "ballpark roadmap, iterating as we go" framing is more credible than false precision.

**Purpose 2 -- CEO scope lock:** Every feature addition by the CEO must visibly drag a bar out on the chart. The visual cost of scope creep becomes immediate and undeniable: "you just moved launch back by six weeks" is visible on the chart, not a verbal argument. Replaces recurring negotiation over whether a feature is "just a small addition."

**Build method for studios without detailed scheduling data:** Establish a standard process per feature type (e.g. standard 3C work takes X weeks of engineer time at Y headcount), apply a lookup table to estimate input requirements per feature, use existing headcount and feature priority data as inputs. EP or production lead builds the structure; design and engineering sign off on estimates.

**Process gate:** Non-negotiables (features off the table for the current milestone) must be declared explicitly before the must/should/could prioritisation pass begins -- otherwise every item enters as negotiable.

### Studio Finance Function: Clean Start vs Historical Cleanup

When a studio's financial history is inaccessible or unreliable, a clean-start model is faster and less risky than historical reconstruction. [source: 2026-07-15_studio-finance-function-setup-chaos-clean-start]

**Decision framework:** If historical data is held by someone slow to release it and the foundations are murky, a clean-start model plus a short-term contractor for legacy untangling is faster than letting the new finance lead's first months be consumed by cleanup. "A room of people involved in creating the financial chaos probably cannot untangle it."

**P&L model structure (working for a ~60-70 person studio):** Payroll vs non-payroll costs, broken down by department and cost nature. Summary views: company-wide and per department. Headcount plan as the input: status by role, colour-coded by priority (pull-forward vs push-back) within a capped budget. Producers booked 100% against their respective department cost lines, not to a general overhead pool.

**RAG operations cadence:** Weekly operations meeting with RAG status board on screen. Critical framing: Red = needs action, not failure. Train the team on this distinction before the cadence begins or problems will be concealed. Finance lead provides runway and P&L status as a standing agenda item.

**Hidden cost area:** Software and subscriptions. Assign a producer or admin to chase all heads of department for software lists and seat counts before building the budget model -- seat count is almost always unknown.

### Brand Identity Build Sequence: Pillars First

Brand identity built ahead of game pillars will fragment. Every iteration is driven by individual preference rather than strategy, producing materials with no shared logic. [source: 2026-07-15_brand-identity-build-sequence-pillars-first]

**Hard-sequenced build order:**
1. Game pillars ratified and locked (prerequisite -- nothing else starts until this is done)
2. Studio brand investigation Q&A with leadership and key staff
3. Game brand investigation Q&A (separate from studio; focused on USPs, tone, product approach)
4. Synthesise learnings into draft brand direction
5. Competitive research informed by draft (research against a draft, not in a vacuum)
6. Full brand identity: brand bible, colour system, typography, character and asset briefs
7. Issue colour, character, and asset briefs to concept team

**Pitch decks proceed as content-only in parallel:** Do not apply art treatment until the brand is established. Narrative and data can be built without brand colours or visual identity.

**North star test:** Within five seconds, an uninitiated person knows what the studio/game is and why they are here. If the five-second test fails, the brand has not done its job regardless of how polished the materials are.

**Artist vs brand designer distinction:** Artists optimise for visual quality; brand designers optimise for strategy and rules. Brand design cannot be delegated to artists without a brand designer owning the rules. Output from artist-led brand work will be aesthetically varied but strategically incoherent.

### CTO Search Under Investment Deadline: European Talent Constraint

Senior engineering leadership with multiplayer/MMO experience is genuinely scarce in Europe at CTO level. This is a structural constraint, not a search effort problem. [source: 2026-07-15_cto-search-european-talent-constraint-executive-search]

**European market constraint:** Microsoft's large-scale layoffs flooded the US games talent market; that supply did not translate to Europe. Studios relying on European pipelines for senior multiplayer CTO profiles face a structurally harder search than US counterparts.

**Investor round pressure management:** Walking into investor meetings without a named CTO weakens the studio's position. Correct response: advance strong Technical Director-level candidates through the pipeline to keep them warm while CTO search continues; do not hire below CTO until the top role is filled. Part-time senior advisors (ex-technical leadership with relevant domain experience) can bridge the investor credibility gap without a hiring decision.

**Compatibility pre-assessment:** When hiring both a Creative Director and a Technical Director/CTO, assess compatibility between the two candidates as part of the pipeline -- not after both are onboarded. Incompatibility between these roles is a structural production risk that is cheaper to surface before hiring decisions are made.

## Creative Alignment and Scope Methods (additions 2026-07-17)

### Creative Leadership Alignment: 2x2 Grid + Coloured-Dot Boundary Mapping

Two-tool sequence for surfacing and mapping creative misalignment at studio leadership level.

**2x2 creative alignment grid:** Maps each key creative stakeholder (Game Director, Creative Director, Head of Design) on two game-design axes relevant to the project. For a live service MMO RPG: X-axis = Choice/impact (high consequence, meaningful decisions) vs Cozy (low stakes, comfort-forward); Y-axis = Solo play vs Group play. Each stakeholder plots their own position independently; all positions are revealed simultaneously in a facilitated session. What the grid reveals: stakeholders who verbally agree on direction often hold materially different assumptions about tone, player agency, and social design; the grid makes disagreement visible without requiring a verbal declaration. First stated position may be diplomatic; position after discussion shows the real stance. World-state persistence (burn a house and it stays burned for all players) has major compute and cost implications at MMO scale; the 2x2 surfaces this as a real technical constraint, not just a design preference. [source: not_qzZxF63HD9velR, production session at a live service MMO studio, 17 Jul 2026]

**Coloured-dot boundary exercise (follow-on tool):** Run after the 2x2 to add boundary detail to the position map. Each participant places coloured markers against the same axes to indicate: what they will actively advocate for (strong preference), what they will accept (neutral / live with), what they will resist (strong objection). Reveals tolerance zones beyond position -- two people in the same quadrant may have entirely different boundaries within that space. Run with all relevant creative leaders simultaneously: positions stated in isolation are often diplomatic; positions stated in view of others' positions are more honest. Key insight: knowing where someone will stop advocating (their "accept" threshold) is often the most valuable data for predicting behaviour in production conflict. [source: not_odHxNAyfUCclXM, ~55-70 person remote MMO studio, 17 Jul 2026]

**Sequencing:** Run 2x2 first to establish positions; run coloured-dot to establish boundaries. Together they provide a complete creative alignment picture. Particularly valuable when: onboarding as an advisor (run early; gives a shared visual artefact without requiring anyone to explicitly call out disagreement); two senior creatives are producing conflicting design artefacts despite nominal agreement; a new creative leader joins and their real position relative to the existing direction is unknown.

### Game Director Monthly Touchpoint Operating Model

When a Lead Designer is onboarded and capable of owning design detail, the Game Director's role transitions from design contributor to directional overseer. Structural model:

**What the GD stops doing:** lead-designer-mode work (design documents, detailed system specification, day-to-day design decisions). These transfer entirely to the Lead Designer.

**What the GD does instead:** touches each department (engineering, art, design) once per month; asks directional questions only ("How do you feel about the direction of the game?") then listens without solving; brings findings to Product Council; Product Council decides on solutions. Owns the schedule proactively -- reviews last month, determines who genuinely needs GD time, avoids meetings happening by default.

**Why it works:** the GD is the only person with full cross-disciplinary sight; monthly touchpoints surface misalignment earlier than waiting for it to emerge in production. Asking without solving preserves team ownership of solutions while giving the GD the signal they need.

**Failure mode:** GD remaining in design detail simultaneously undermines the Lead Designer and destroys the cross-disciplinary view.

**Pull-back pattern:** GDs who came up through design have a strong pull back into design work; structural guardrails (monthly cadence, "directional questions only" rule) counteract this pull. "Don't let meetings happen to you" is the operating principle -- proactive schedule ownership is a meaningful shift for directors who previously had fully managed calendars.

**Applicable when:** a studio hires a Lead Designer into a team previously managed directly by the Game Director -- the transition requires explicit role boundary redefinition, not just an informal handoff. When a GD complains about losing influence as the team grows -- reframe: the influence comes from the monthly cross-team view, not from attending every design meeting. [source: not_odHxNAyfUCclXM, ~55-70 person remote MMO studio, Game Director transitioning from multi-hat to pure GD role after Lead Designer onboarded, 17 Jul 2026]

### Two-Bucket Scope Prioritisation for Pre-Pillar Teams

MoSCoW (Must/Should/Could/Won't) requires locked design pillars as a reference -- without them, Should/Could become opinion-based and sessions stall on debate. For studios that need to make scope decisions before pillar lock:

**Two-bucket method:** reduce to "must be in build" and "must not be in build." Everything else is implicitly deferred. Forces binary decisions that the team can actually execute.

**Application at a ~55-70 person MMO studio (VS1 scope cut, 17 Jul 2026):** Used to cut approximately one-third of VS1 line items. Cut: PVP combat, arena, range combat, loot distribution, mount pets, character creation. Retained: core item/entitlement system, consumables, equipment inventory.

**When to graduate to MoSCoW:** once design pillars are locked, reintroduce MoSCoW as the standard prioritisation framework. Two-bucket is a temporary scaffold for speed, not a permanent methodology.

**Use when:** a scope-cut session cannot wait for pillar lock; a prioritisation session is stalling on Should/Could debates; onboarding as a new CPO or advisor to a studio mid-production; a team lacks the craft maturity to navigate MoSCoW's nuanced categories. [source: not_qzZxF63HD9velR, 17 Jul 2026]

### Live Service Commitment Without Operational Readiness

Pattern at studios where a live service commitment was made by leadership without a corresponding understanding of what live service requires.

**Pattern structure:** Investor sold CEO on a live service proposition 3-4 years before current engagement. CEO committed publicly without a working understanding of live service requirements (ongoing ops team, backend infrastructure, perpetual feature cadence, retention loop design). Studio organised around prestige single-player craft mentality throughout development -- no live service capability built. Live service director (new hire) works around leadership that does not understand the model they committed to. A hard investor review is the inflection point.

**Diagnostic signals:** founder-CEO who championed live service on investor narrative rather than design intent; no retention loop in the design; team culture celebrates launch polish over post-launch engagement; no live service operations infrastructure.

**Advisory:** when onboarding as an advisor to a studio with live service ambitions, the first question is whether leadership has operated a live service title. Verbal commitment is not evidence. Examine team composition: absent operations function, no engagement/retention design lead, no live service experience in the founding team = the commitment is aspirational, not operational. Hard inflection points (investor reviews, board presentations) without live service readiness force either a pivot or a reckoning -- surface this before the inflection point, not at it. [source: not_CyGphiWiLa6dnp, live service gaming studio advisory context, 17 Jul 2026]

### Publisher Analytics Control: Embedded Data Scientist Pattern

Pattern observed where a publisher proposes embedding a data scientist at a studio as "partnership support" -- a standard analytics control model.

**Pattern:** publisher's embedded analyst reports to publisher, not studio, and funnels proprietary player data and KPI interpretation back. Historical parallel: EA used this model to establish data dependency before extracting commercial leverage. Studio analytics director identifies the control play at point of proposal.

**Studio countermove:** push back on physical embedding; propose remote analytical partnership with defined data-sharing parameters. Retains studio's analytical independence while satisfying the publisher's stated concern.

**Advisory:** when a publisher offers embedded support resources (data, marketing, technical), examine the reporting line and data access first. "Support" resources that report to the publisher and have unrestricted data access are analytics control, not partnership. Negotiate defined scope, defined data access, and clear exit terms before agreeing to any embedding. [source: not_CyGphiWiLa6dnp, Tencent-funded studio, 17 Jul 2026]

### Scope Change Communication Gate: Product Council First

Incident pattern and fix for scope changes that bypass the Product Council and land directly with the team.

**Incident structure:** Late-stage scope addition (investor-driven, added under pressure) announced directly to the team before going through Product Council. Art Director was blindsided; morale impact followed the surprise, not the change itself.

**Root cause:** no enforced gate between "scope decision made" and "scope announced to team." In the absence of a structural gate, urgent or emotionally charged scope changes bypass the Product Council because the decision-maker acts on urgency rather than process.

**Fix:** all scope changes must go through Product Council first, regardless of origin (investor pressure, CEO direction, late-night insight). Product Council provides: impact assessment, team briefing, and a controlled announcement frame. Even accepted scope changes are better received when the team first hears context and impact assessment rather than a raw requirement.

**Signal:** if scope announcements regularly bypass the Product Council, the Product Council has insufficient authority -- the process is advisory rather than structural, and will continue to be bypassed under pressure. [source: not_S2aqeqlWzBXtVY, ~55-70 person MMO studio, 17 Jul 2026]

### Studio AI Governance: Three-Tier Framework

Framework for managing AI tool adoption across a development studio by risk tier.

**Tier 1 -- Private/In-House:** Tools running locally on studio infrastructure (LLaMA local, ComfyUI local). No data leaves the studio. IP-safe by default.

**Tier 2 -- Public Frontier:** General-purpose cloud AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini). IP-risk zone unless enterprise data handling agreements are in place. Policy: restrict to non-IP content only unless enterprise accounts with data isolation are confirmed.

**Tier 3 -- Industry-Specific:** Domain-specific AI tools (Tripo, Meshy). Assume outputs may feed vendor training data unless explicitly excluded by contract. Audit each tool's data handling before adoption.

**Sub-team sentiment profile (observed at a ~55-person MMO studio, Jul 2026):** Environment art and animation: pro-AI. VFX: mostly pro. Tech art: mixed. Character art: sceptical. Concept art: opposed. Sentiment correlates with perceived threat to the discipline's core skills, not with actual AI capability in that domain.

**Trigger for formalising:** at the observed studio, prompted by a Head of Finance licence audit discovering undisclosed AI spend. Studios without formal AI governance are exposed to: undisclosed IP risk, unapproved spend, inconsistent practice across disciplines, and talent friction when AI-resistant staff feel unsupported.

**Companion policy:** when the three-tier framework is adopted, a complementary IP policy must cover: what constitutes studio IP, when prompts count as sharing IP, and an approved tool list per discipline. [source: not_S2aqeqlWzBXtVY, ~55-person MMO development studio, Art Director 1:1, 17 Jul 2026]

### Scope Management

**VS1 non-negotiable triage sprint pattern:** When a studio is 6-10 weeks from an investor vertical slice and the feature list exceeds the window, a "first-pass non-negotiable prioritisation" with all leads in a single meeting is the correct response. Categories: non-negotiable for VS1 (core demo loop) / deferred to VS2 / deferred to VS3 / PTC (Possible to Cut -- features needing design clarity before a binary decision). "PTC" is a holding category that avoids premature permanent cuts. Staging deferred features as VS2/VS3/VS4 rather than "cut" maintains team morale and signals a roadmap exists beyond the investor build. The triage output directly unblocks art direction, roadmap drafting, and headcount finalisation -- all of which are typically blocked on the feature list in this scenario. DoDs are set as permanent studio standards at the same time, not VS1-only, so the alignment investment persists. [source: not_q2JjcZ90725rEe_vs1-scope-triage]

**Scope management as continuous weekly discipline (Rob Sandberg #14):** Scope drift comes from individually reasonable decisions that accumulate to months of hidden work -- "a plan that has drifted from reality becomes a fiction that costs you the ability to see the actual problem." Three required questions for every scope addition: what is the actual time cost, who does the work, and what exits scope to accommodate it. A change request process only works if it is faster than ignoring it -- the overhead must be a 15-minute conversation and a single log line, or teams route around it. MoSCoW run as a standing 10-minute weekly agenda item keeps categorisation current; a long Won't-Have list at alpha indicates a healthy project with accurate self-knowledge. Two types of stakeholder scope argument require different responses: priority arguments (they want it) require showing trade-offs; capacity arguments (they need it) require reassessing MoSCoW against remaining time. Dependency mapping makes the true cost of "small" features visible -- a quick feature touching the core loop generates testing, tutorial copy, UI work, and design iterations that are invisible without the map. [source: web_2026-07-22_production-alchemist-14-scope-cutting]

### Pre-Production

**Pre-production exit criteria and producer role (Rob Sandberg #17):** Pre-production's purpose is reducing uncertainty before expensive commitments -- "the cost of decisions during this phase is conversation; the same decision in full production costs weeks of rework and stakeholder conflict." The producer's role is not to write the GDD but to own the process producing it, specifically to identify downstream dependencies that creative decisions create. Two mandatory documents: a Decision Log (what was decided, by whom, when, and why) and an Open Questions List (unresolved items with assumed answers currently driving other decisions -- the most dangerous form of technical debt in pre-production). Prototypes are question-answering instruments, not demonstrations; before running one, establish what question it answers, what decisions follow, and what assumptions will accidentally become permanent. Four conditions for full production readiness: core loop validated with target players; key technical risks resolved; team in place; first milestone acceptance criteria agreed with publisher or stakeholder. Pre-production ends at criterion completion, not calendar date. [source: web_2026-07-22_production-alchemist-17-pre-production]

### Build Cadence, QA Process, and Bug Triage

### Documentation Standards

**GDD and TDD quality: the fresh engineer runnable test:** Practical quality bar for studio technical documentation. TDD standard: a TDD must let a new engineer pick up and execute the feature without re-reading the meeting transcript that spawned it -- if the transcript is a required input to understand the task, the TDD is incomplete. GDD standard: a GDD is a structured design rationale document, not a command list. Failure mode: a guild system GDD presented as a list of commands with broken internal links -- shallow documentation is masked by broken links, which reveals the quality problem only when fixed. Batch conversion approach for existing GDDs: pick one representative TDD, bring it to full spec manually, and that corrected document becomes the template; run existing content through AI for mechanical structure conversion (80% lift), with human authors retaining design content. AI is valid for template scaffolding; AI for design rationale is an IP and quality risk. GDD standards ownership must be named -- leaving it unassigned means it defaults to no one. ### Meeting Facilitation and Prioritisation Frameworks

**R/Y/G/Purple executive meeting facilitation model and multi-column priority scoring:** Executive meeting facilitation model designed to prevent tactical rat-holes and enforce delivery accountability. Five-status signal set: Green (moving -- advance in meeting); Yellow/Watching (flag for awareness, no intervention needed); Red-with-mitigation (problem identified, mitigation agreed, owner confirmed -- record and close in meeting); Red-needs-help (intervention needed -- agree what help is needed, book a focused follow-up, extract from the exec meeting immediately); Purple (done -- acknowledge and move on). Purple is the status most commonly omitted in RAG frameworks; including it creates a complete signal and allows clean closure. Facilitation mechanic: push for specific deliverable + date + owner, not "I'll do that." Multi-column priority scoring: divide company work into columns by domain (Ops / Business / Game / Tech / Studio) and assign separate priority scores within each column; the item with the lowest aggregate score across all columns is the company's current top priority. This surfaces cross-domain blockers that a single-list priority approach misses. AI-assisted action extraction post-meeting (via Granola or equivalent) handles capture and formatting; the facilitator's role is driving actions and dates in the meeting, not note-taking. [source: not_ZcfWFukyOSn4KV_executive-meeting-facilitation]

### AI Policy and IP Governance

**Studio AI copyright and IP risk: operational policy framework for game development (public):** Key copyright risks from AI tool use in game studios. Copyright loss: accomplished works fed entirely through AI may not retain copyright -- a reported threshold of 30% human differentiation is required to maintain a derivative copyright claim; an Oregon-based operator is actively pursuing lawsuits using AI checkers to claim public-access rights on IP corpora (active litigation, July 2026). Embedded tools carry the same risk as frontier models: Rovo/Confluence AI, Adobe Photoshop (AI now embedded), and Unreal Engine 6 (AI embedded by default) all carry equivalent IP exposure to external Claude or ChatGPT usage. Policy format requirement: an operational usage document (tool-by-tool, role-by-role guidance) paired with an HR policy document is more enforceable than a generic do/don't list. Pattern: AI governance failures tend to come from well-intentioned misuse (developer building an unauthorised tool, executives distributing AI subscriptions without controls) -- policy must address intent as well as action. [source: not_9nQcKcphTysGNd_ai-copyright-ip-risk]

### MMO Game Design

**MMO pillar structure: three-component format and feature-to-pillar mapping gate:** Pillar count: 5-10 per game -- fewer loses nuance, more creates navigation overhead. Each pillar requires three components: (1) what it is (positive definition); (2) what it is not (anti-definition -- the Overton window setter, not a hard prohibition); (3) failure signals (observable indicators the pillar has been violated in a feature or system). Feature-to-pillar mapping is a gate, not a guideline: every feature must map to at least one pillar; features that cannot be mapped are red flags to be cut or deferred; features mapping to multiple pillars are desirable and deliver disproportionate value per unit of scope. Language risk in reductive "is not" framing: "no numbers games" can be read as "never show numbers" -- pillar documents require careful framing to prevent misapplication of the Overton window constraint. Steam tag thought experiment for market positioning: feed the game's lore or design document to an AI and ask it to infer Steam tags; the resulting list surfaces implied genre positioning and reveals missing signals. Founder-led ideation fix: centralise unilateral idea distribution into a single shared channel with all creative leads; prevents each recipient forming a different priority impression from the same idea. [source: not_Gpvov9xIRnEUle_mmo-pillar-design]

**Systemic design philosophy: resonance, complex vs complicated, and layered systems:** Resonance defined as the correct definition of done for systemic features: a gameplay moment that lands, surprises, and is worth retelling to another player. A rough emergent moment players share beats a flawless scripted moment they do not -- resonance is better than polish completeness as a gate. Complex vs complicated distinction: complex means many interacting elements players can reason through with effort (acceptable, even desirable); complicated means too many inputs for players to infer cause from effect (always a design failure). Test: if a player cannot infer what caused an outcome, the system has too many inputs. Layered systems approach: begin with a small set of simple, readable systems and add layers incrementally -- do not design all layers upfront. Day/night cycle as the archetype base layer: one state change applied consistently across everything sensitive (animal behaviour, herb spawning, visibility, NPC schedules) produces emergent content without dedicated feature work; seasons are a later layer. Player-driven discovery (wikis, emergent knowledge sharing) is part of the intended gameplay loop in systemic MMOs, not a failure of tutorial design -- design for it, not against it. Reference example: lightning plus a metal weapon in a goblin camp producing emergent electrocution from intersecting system rules, without a dedicated "stealing mechanic." [source: not_Gpvov9xIRnEUle_systemic-design-philosophy]

**MMO PVP design: consensual zones and playerbase splitting risk:** PVP engagement benchmark: historically 25-35% of MMO players engage with PVP content -- a stable figure across a wide range of titles, which should calibrate how much development investment PVP systems receive at launch. Recommended launch approach: open-world consensual PVP zones only; do not invest in structured PVP mechanics (arenas, ranked modes, dedicated PVP progression) at launch. Structured PVP is effectively a second product requiring a separate balancing and content pipeline that competes with the core game for resources during the period when the core game needs the most investment. Playerbase splitting cautionary tale: Sea of Thieves "Safer Seas" (PVE-only variant) split the playerbase into two groups, satisfying neither fully -- the PVP community lost opponents, the PVE community felt like a second-class product. The feature solved a symptom (PVP friction) rather than the cause (poor consensual opt-in design). Design principle: PVP opt-in should be zone-based (world-level choice), not interaction-level (individual consent prompts); zone-based consent is legible, interaction-level consent creates friction and ambiguity. [source: not_Gpvov9xIRnEUle_mmo-pvp-consensual-zones]

### Headcount and Contractor Management

**Studio contractor backfill cost model and wave prioritisation (anonymised):** Standard backfill cost model for contractor exits: one month notice payout plus one month dual-contractor overlap equals two months fully loaded cost per role. ### Alternative Production Frameworks (Public)

**Shape Up synthesis: applicability for game studios (20-100 people):** Shape Up (Ryan Singer, Basecamp, 2019; free at basecamp.com/shapeup) has no documented game studio implementations. Synthesised applicability analysis from the primary source. Core structure: six-week build cycles preceded by a shaping phase (senior staff define problems at intermediate abstraction -- concrete enough for teams, abstract enough to preserve creative latitude); a two-week cool-down follows each cycle. Appetite (time budget set before work begins) replaces estimates -- this inverts the typical "estimate it once designed" pattern that causes milestone slippage. The Betting Table: pitches compete for slots; nothing accumulates into a permanent queue (no-backlog discipline directly addresses "nice to have" scope inflation). The circuit breaker: unfinished work at cycle end does not get extended; triggers reshaping rather than continued investment. Game-production mappings: six-week cycle aligns with alpha-to-beta intervals at 30-80 person studios; the Betting Table maps to milestone planning meetings with added no-backlog discipline; cool-down is a natural phase for community feedback, live bug fixing, and experimental content. Adaptation required: the 1-2 programmer plus 1 designer team model suits narrative, UI, and system features but requires explicit adaptation for art, audio, and QA -- a game studio adopting Shape Up must define how these disciplines attach to cycle teams. Most useful when advising studios that have outgrown Scrum ceremonies but are not large enough for SAFe-style frameworks (typically 20-50 people drowning in backlog overhead). The appetite concept is portable without full Shape Up adoption -- start by asking "how long is this feature worth?" before defining it. [source: web_2026-07-22_shape-up-game-production-synthesis]

**Coffee Stain Studios (Satisfactory): Experimental-to-Stable staged release and transparency model (public):** Coffee Stain Studios (24-25 employees throughout Early Access) shipped Satisfactory through five years of Early Access (March 2019 to September 2024) with eight major updates before 1.0 launch. The production transparency model: public Trello roadmap; weekly "Friday News" live Q&A streams; a staged Experimental branch that holds each major update for community testing before promotion to Stable. The Experimental branch functions as a structured community feedback loop -- the studio observes player impact on live factory saves before committing the update to the default branch, surfacing edge cases that internal QA cannot replicate on a complex open-ended simulation. The Experimental-to-Stable pipeline separates "feature complete" from "production ready" without requiring closed beta infrastructure, making it a low-overhead staged release model accessible at indie scale. Promotion criteria must be explicit (bug threshold, performance baseline) to make the community testing window purposeful rather than ad hoc. Weekly streams serve a production function: they surface player friction early enough to address within the current update cycle rather than creating a new bug backlog. A public Trello roadmap commits the studio to transparency about what is in scope -- creating external accountability for scope decisions and a structural constraint on scope creep. [source: web_2026-07-22_satisfactory-experimental-transparency-model]

### Meeting Structure

**SLT three-tier meeting hierarchy with RYG department status:** For a studio of 55-70 people with a CPO/COO split, a three-tier meeting structure prevents information overload. Tier 1 (C-Level): strategic direction, no operational items. Tier 2 (SLT / Department Heads): each department reports top 3 priorities + owner + due date + dependencies, displayed as RYG status per department; operations side (COO: HR/Finance/IT/Legal) and product side (CPO: Art/Design/Game) present in the same forum with no crossover agenda items. Tier 3 (Leads): run by production, not by executive leadership. Delegation discipline: the producer/coordinator chases updates and builds templates; the EP/meeting owner runs the session but does not build it. Supplementary tool: a priority-scored Excel worklist with hierarchical numbering (2.2 = story under feature 2, 2.2.1 = subtask) and department codes; reviewed weekly in a 1-hour coordinator sync. [source: not_fboCpu8LrWHfDe, anonymised]

### Pre-Production

**GDD feature audit against pillars: Keep / Defer / Cut framework:** When a studio has accumulated GDDs over years without auditing against current design pillars, a pre-milestone audit is required. Process: (1) Head of Design scores each feature against pillars and vision; (2) verdict per feature: keep (VS or post-VS), defer (named phase), cut (backlog cleared); (3) knowledge tool restructured with stage templates; (4) critical missing design docs written before the VS milestone, not after; (5) review uses existing game systems map as starting frame. Prerequisite: the auditing designer must not be running VS execution concurrently -- the mandates conflict. Calibration signal: for every visible feature without articulated design rationale, assume three more lurking in the backlog. [source: not_wxY4jEVBZMQz1r, anonymised]

**Post-VS phase structure: jigsaw model (MVP / ELP first, outward):** When structuring the post-VS roadmap, build from the centre outward. Define the minimum viable player experience (MVP / ELP) first -- the smallest coherent loop that is fun and shippable -- then work outward through themed phases, each adding systemic depth. Sequencing rationale: systemic features lose value without their neighbours (crafting without economy delivers a fraction of intended experience). Phase mapping: map to high-level roadmap phases with the Head of Design responsible for auditing feature state, defining phase structure, and building the feature backlog per phase. Boundary rule: the post-VS planner does not get involved in VS execution unless explicitly requested -- VS execution ownership stays with the Game Director. [source: not_wxY4jEVBZMQz1r, anonymised]

**Beck Interactive Partners -- publisher-grade VS evaluation rubric (2026):** Based on 500+ publisher pitch processes in 2025, the quality criteria now applied by publishers. Primary self-test: "Would you feel comfortable selling this as 1/10th of your complete game on Steam at a pro-rated price?" Six independently-scored dimensions: UI (final-form menus, navigable by unfamiliar player); Art (near-final assets, Steam front page viable); Gameplay loops (primary loop end-to-end with full progression chain); Polish (smooth transitions, externally playtested, stable); Tutorial (teaches unique mechanics without external assistance); Technical (stable frame rates, minimal crashes). Any single dimension failing fails the evaluation. Known-issue disclosure (loading screen or accompanying document) is standard professional practice. The 2026 quality bar is substantially higher than 2021-2023; studios using pre-2023 playbooks are materially under-delivering. [source: web_2026-07-23_beck-interactive-vs-publisher-evaluation-rubric]

**Ellenor: VS as production trap -- what pre-production must also deliver:** A studio can pass the VS milestone and still not be production-ready. The VS tests gameplay and visual quality; it does not test tools, pipelines, approval workflows, or team decision-making cadence. Three failure modes: (1) Tool degradation -- pre-production tools built as hacks fail under production volume; (2) System shortcuts -- VS workarounds valid for one instance do not scale to hundreds; (3) Process gaps -- feature review, mission approval, and asset validation processes were never established. Primary production readiness metric: "The velocity of a team in production is determined by how much approved work they do." Approval speed is the KPI that predicts production success. NBI diagnostic question post-VS: "How does approved work move through the pipeline?" not "How fast is the team shipping?" [source: web_2026-07-23_ellenor-vertical-slice-production-readiness-trap]

**Toño Game Consultants -- five-stage prototype-to-production sequence:** Prototype Loop → Validation → Story Mapping → Backlog Creation → Vertical Slice / Production. Prototype gate: demonstrates "repeatable fun across multiple playtests, not just once"; prototypes are intentionally fast, dirty, disposable, non-scalable. Story Mapping gate (1-2 day facilitated session): outputs a structured user journey, organised backlog, mapped dependencies, aligned priorities, and early scope boundaries -- the most commonly skipped step and the most common cause of reactive development. VS scope: 3-5 minutes of polished content; "cut scope, not quality." VS audience is the team, leads, investors, and publishers, not players -- studios that confuse these over-polish community-facing features and under-polish internal production signals. Common VS failure: proving visuals not the core loop. If the VS was built with crunch, it does not prove the team can sustain production at that quality level. [source: web_2026-07-23_tono-game-consultants-prototype-vs-production-sequence]

### Org Design

**Studio design roles accountability model: Architect / Game Director / EP / Creative Director:** Four-role model for a 55-70 person studio with embedded advisory CPO. Architect (Head of Design): blueprints the vision, audits features, surfaces alignment gaps, coalesces information and gets the core group to agree -- pushes for decisions but does not make them unilaterally. Game Director: execution lead for VS and major milestones; aligns art and engineering to design intent; owns timeline and team coordination. EP / CPO: ships on budget, schedule, and acceptable risk; does not own creative direction. Creative Director (studio founder/CEO): owns the vision; needs "boxes to colour in" rather than open-ended creative direction -- defined constraints prevent divergence. Reporting (formation phase): Architect, GD, and EP all report to CPO during setup. Roles document approach: circulate individually for feedback before group alignment -- prevents public position-taking before private alignment. Founding pattern: a founder CD who communicates in black-and-white terms is often more open to reasoned pushback than their style suggests -- the initial position is a starting frame, not a final decision. [source: not_wxY4jEVBZMQz1r, anonymised]

**Player archetype: early-adopter audience definition before feature decisions:** Process order: internal cohort exercise first, then market research, not the reverse. (1) Core team members independently draft their view of the target early-adopter audience; (2) Head of Design consolidates into a single aligned document for group agreement; (3) internal alignment feeds into formal market research with external hired expertise; (4) external researcher (not internal design team) conducts market sizing. Key insight: Killers (Bartle taxonomy) are the largest enthusiast population in online games but are highly nomadic and transient -- poor retention anchors. The largest actual audience for a PvP-adjacent social game is often "competitive but friendly" -- wants the feeling of competition without hardcore toxicity; frequently undercounted because it does not self-identify loudly. Studios that mistake TAM research for internal audience alignment routinely build feature lists without agreed-upon target players. [source: not_wxY4jEVBZMQz1r, internal]

### Sprint / Cycle

**Three-tier studio delivery cadence: Sprint / Monthly Showcase / Quarterly Milestone:** Governing principle: the game sets the rhythm for the whole studio; operations, HR, finance, and legal roadmaps must align to milestone windows, not the reverse. Three tiers: (1) Two-week sprint -- kickoff, planning, mid-sprint check-in, review, retro; (2) Monthly showcase -- completed features shown to studio leadership, forces demonstrable progress each month; (3) Three-month milestone -- full playtest delivering a complete player experience; external-facing investment round checkpoint. Critical framing: milestone goals must be "deliver a coherent player experience," not "deliver a feature checklist" -- if experience-level goals are absent, teams deliver isolated features and miss integration gaps until late. Estimation technique: split "proxy completion" (feature present, rough) from "final quality" (feature polished, ship-ready) -- this split alone can halve an estimate by surfacing what was hidden contingency. Administrative activities (reviews, onboarding) scheduled in low-load sprint periods. [source: not_tWtAiezzkFgUle, internal]
